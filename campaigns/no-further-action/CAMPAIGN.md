## One-sentence throughline

Lilly Dawson spends a career closing Ashgrove-and-after cases the file wants closed, until she can break the function that decides what the record is allowed to say — and prove the haunting was always people.

## GM intent

Walk a solo detective career in three textures: 1986 local badge, 1998 Ashgrove detective pulled into FBI consultancy, 2016 senior / almost retired takedown. Each night is a real case. The behind-the-scenes thing is discovered through work, never as a briefing. Tone is suspense that flirts with the unnatural and is never confirmed as supernatural. 2016 is allowed to name the fear as human-made. Desired destination (she can break the function) is not a required path: a failed case changes cost, access, and who dies, not a reset.

## Campaign background

On **10 January 1986**, Lilly is promoted to Detective Third Class and transferred out of New Jersey. On paper, Ashgrove is a professional opportunity. In practice, her department is sending a promising officer away after she broke protocol and pushed an unrelated investigation past orders to stop. Captain **Harold M. Keane** privately warns her to respect the chain of command if she still wants the FBI.

Lilly does not want the assignment. She accepts because refusing an official promotion would confirm her reputation for disobedience and could close the path to the Bureau. She intends to do the work, prove herself, and return east.

Ashgrove is a random destination. Neither the continuity consortium nor Simon arranged it. The machine becomes interested in Lilly only after her ordinary police work disturbs its local operations.

In 1998 Lilly remains an Ashgrove detective while serving as a consultant to the FBI. In 2016 she is a senior investigator nearing retirement; her exact title and employer will be fixed when that era is designed.

## Campaign predetermined truths

Facts that are true for this table regardless of player action. Distinct from planned events and unused prep.

- There is **no** supernatural. Nightmares, static, “impossible” timing are trauma, method, or both. 2016 may prove the fear was manufactured. Earlier nights must not leak that speech through people who could not know it.
- A private continuity consortium created by wealthy family offices owns the small **custodial spine** behind the Concordance. Government contracts provide access but do not control it. Rotating shells and compartmentalized vendors prevent most participants from seeing a whole operation.
- **The Concordance** (GM working name only) is the enduring practice and capacity for harmonizing records into an allowed official story. A **Concordance operation** is one use of it. Letterheads die and return. Killing is enforcement, not the logo. Lilly cannot Google a membership list in 1986.
- **Ashgrove, Oregon** is ground zero for Lilly’s collision with it. The town is not required to know that.
- Lilly's assignment to Ashgrove was an ordinary disciplinary exile and an extraordinary coincidence, not a machine placement.
- **ClearWave** (and any project name like it) is **unused prep** until it is said or proven at the table. January 1986 play did **not** establish that name.
- Lilly's biological father was a communications technician and low-level crook selected as the scapegoat for a manufactured serial-killer case assembled from unrelated deaths. He was not a member or whistleblower and cannot identify the machine.
- Simon worked at a low level inside that operation, learned the case was false, and kept leverage proving one patron transferred one death into the pattern. That narrow proof let him leave and protect Medrick and Lilly; it cannot expose the consortium.
- Lilly’s **origin paperwork was already harmonized** (Simon arriving late, missing baby photos, Medrick’s repeated story). That is why a personal braid exists. At the campaign start Lilly knows only the official violent-offender story and has never contacted her biological father.
- Concordance operations leave a precise **synchronization gap** when multiple records are taken offline and harmonized. The technology changes; the missing interval remains. The gap proves common process, not motive or culprit.
- **1986 connection rule:** nights 1 and 2 share a thread (Abigail Carr’s call). Nights 3 and 4 are separate jobs on the calendar. Any “these were results of the Concordance” beat is **closing information of arc 1** (night 4), not a serial in night 3.
- **Frank Loman** is missing as of the end of night 1. What happened to him is **not** established.
- One **personal bruise** per era (nights 4 / 7 / 11 in the index). Full lock of origin + Concordance waits for 2016.
- **Chief Whitley** is locally corrupt (payoffs, quiet files). He is **not** Concordance and does not know the consortium. Intended 1986 destination: Lilly’s discoveries (likely night 3, settled by the end of the arc) take him off the force. The **unfair** outcome from her seat is the default: she puts the graft up the chain; he may sit a short hold or an investigation; political connections let him **walk** (resign/retire) instead of prison. By **1998** he is already a civilian. He blames her for the heat; she already knows the system let him out. At least one 1998 night requires her to ask him something. If a 1986 night fails to prove him, he is still out by 1998 by a quieter deal and still blames her.
