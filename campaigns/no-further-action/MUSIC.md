# No Further Action — Radio Direction

Music is a campaign tool, not decoration. It marks the changing decades of Lilly's career and lets ordinary broadcasts carry unease without confirming the supernatural.

## Rule: diegetic only

Every recommendation must have a source inside the fiction:

- 1986: 102.3 FM, another local AM/FM station, patrol-car radio, home stereo, cassette, diner or bar jukebox.
- 1998: commercial/local FM, car radio, CD player, late-night request show, store or bar system, early automated rotation.
- 2016: terrestrial car radio, phone/streaming, satellite radio, business playlist, or a logged automated broadcast when plausible.

Do not use a non-diegetic score under a scene. If no source could naturally be heard, omit the cue rather than making the world play soundtrack.

## Selection test

Apply both requirements in order:

1. **Airplay plausibility:** the track was released by the exact story date and fits the country, region, station format, and time slot.
2. **Subtle thematic relevance:** among plausible tracks, prefer one whose mood or theme quietly rhymes with the situation.

Do not select a song because its title gives away the answer. Do not quote lyrics in campaign files. Link or cite release/chart sources rather than relying on memory.

Music is atmosphere unless an adventure explicitly establishes a recording or broadcast as evidence. There is no recurring haunted song. The campaign's technical fingerprint is the **synchronization gap**, not a melody.

## 102.3 FM

Abigail Carr works at fictional **102.3 FM** in Ashgrove. In 1986 it is a small-market music station with local announcements and a contemporary pop/rock rotation broad enough to carry national hits, adult contemporary, and selected album-rock singles. A child adventure may narrow Abigail's show and schedule when it is designed.

The frequency is campaign continuity authored after session one. Do not claim the player heard “102.3 FM” during *Neblina sobre o Lago*.

Every child `ADVENTURE.md` should include:

Every substantial story point in `RUN.md` should include this compact cue when a source is naturally present:

```md

```

Use one primary track. Add one alternate only when the route can reach two meaningfully different sources or moods.

## Era palette

### 1986 — local signal

- Human DJs, station IDs, weather, local ads, request calls, tape carts, FM hiss, cassette wear.
- Patrol-car and office radios make music part of police work.
- The station may cut from ordinary national rotation to local interruption; the normal song is what makes the interruption unsettling.

### 1998 — consolidation and transition

- Tighter commercial formats, CD playback, syndicated programming, request lines, early digital automation.
- Radio can still feel local while ownership and back-office systems are becoming less visible.
- A repeated song is usually rotation, not an omen. Logs and automation matter more than mystical coincidence.

### 2016 — audible systems

- Terrestrial radio coexists with streaming, satellite, phones, and automated business playlists.
- Metadata, history, timestamps, and broadcast logs can establish what played.
- Convenience creates records; a source can be altered, but the alteration should leave a seam.
