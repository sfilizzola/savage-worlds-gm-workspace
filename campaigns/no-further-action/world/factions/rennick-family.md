# The Rennick Family

- **Canon status:** authored as true for this table; not yet in play. Background for future hooks, not established history and not session canon.
- **Visibility:** GM until a night gives Lilly a civic, property, or family reason to meet them. The *name* can surface as town memory (a picnic, a letterhead, a street of West End houses) before any Rennick is on screen.
- **Public identity:** The mill family. Two generations ran **Cinder Creek Lumber**; the town still borrows their pride to feel like a living place. Not an office, not a badge — the house everyone checks with before Ashgrove does anything that would look like admitting the mill is over.
- **Actual objective:** Keep the family name meaning something now that the payroll is gone. Internally divided on how (see the fracture below).
- **Methods:** Social weight, not authority. Grange standing, Zion Lutheran pews, West End tenancy, funerals and picnics, a word to the mayor, the quiet expectation that an ugly story is bad for property and for the town. They ask; they do not order.
- **Assets:**
  - The **West End houses** (leftover company/family housing at the mill camp) and the family name on **Cinder Creek Lumber** letterhead and civic clubs.
  - A **share of the paper** on the mill ground — enough to freeze a sale, not enough to force one alone (see split title).
  - Standing at the **Grange**, **Zion Lutheran**, and in mill-family memory across the valley.
  - **Len Pell's key** to the yard is a Rennick trust; the Pells were their people, not their equals.
- **Constraints:**
  - **Split title.** A Rookton-side holding shares the paper on the mill ground and wants to close. Neither side can sell cleanly; neither will say out loud that it is over. This is why the yard looks frozen.
  - Influence is **social, not legal**. They cannot shelve a file, sign an ordinance, or make an arrest. They rely on Lindstrom's vanity and Whitley's bought quiet lining up with what they already want.
  - Money is thinner than the name. The pride outlasted the payroll.
- **Leadership:**
  - **Margaret Rennick** (~72), widow of the last operating owner. Speaks for the family. **Will not sign a sale while she is alive** — selling is admitting the town died on her husband's watch and hers. Runs her weight through church, Grange, and "what would the town think." This is who Lindstrom actually checks with. Not a schemer; a matriarch defending a memory.
  - **Warren Rennick** (~44), her son, half-left to Rookton. The "reasonable" voice: wants the Rookton holding to close so the family can walk clean. Resented on 328 as the one who left. Reachable; tired of being the villain of a town he no longer lives in.
  - **Carol Rennick Halvorsen** (~40), daughter, stayed. Married into the **Halvorsen** name — kin to day-patrol **Don Halvorsen** (an unused PD/family thread). Holds her mother's side publicly; privately worn down by the standoff.
  - **Edward Rennick** (d. ~1983), Margaret's husband, the last owner. His death and the mill's closure ran together in town memory. Nothing sinister — it is simply the shape of the frozen title and Margaret's refusal.
- **Relationships:**
  - **Mayor Art Lindstrom** — needs them. His "look fine on 328" pride is *their* pride; ribbon-cuttings borrow Rennick legitimacy. He checks with Margaret; he does not command her, and she is not his relative.
  - **Chief Whitley** — his bought quiet happens to serve them (a shelved ugly story protects property value and the town's face). The Rennicks are a **reason** the quiet is worth buying, **not** a client of Whitley's graft and **not** part of any machine.
  - **The Pells** — Daisy fed Cinder Creek crews; Len watches the yard on a Rennick key. Their people, not their peers.
  - **Rookton** — the county seat is where the opposing paper and the buyer live, matching the campaign's "real leverage is 22 miles east."
- **The fracture (the engine):** **Hold vs. sell**, tinted by **stayed vs. left**. Margaret holds; Warren — the one who half-got-out — wants to sign. Carol is caught between them. That argument *is* the frozen title, and it is the town's public quarrel about the mill's afterlife wearing one family's faces.
- **Current clock/state (January 1986):** Static. Margaret alive and immovable; Warren negotiating quietly from Rookton; the yard dark under Len's key. Nothing is moving, which is exactly the point — the town is frozen around a woman's "no."
- **If ignored:** Nothing happens, loudly. The yard stays shut, the picnics shrink, the name slowly stops meaning anything. No Rennick calls the detective.
- **What could change its course:** Margaret's death or decline (unfreezes the sale); a buyer who forces the split title into daylight; an ugly headline that makes the town stop borrowing their pride; a Rennick needing something only a detective can do; a Halvorsen–Rennick tie pulling the family into a PD matter.

## Boundaries (read before using them in a night)

- **Strictly local.** The Rennicks are Ashgrove mill-town politics and property. They are **not** the continuity consortium, **not** Concordance clients, and **not** aware of any of it. Do not promote them into that lore. If a future night wants a family to *touch* a harmonized file, that is a separate, deliberate decision — not a fact seeded here.
- **Background, not a booked night.** No adventure is written around them. These are hooks a night may pick up: a name on a deed, a reason the yard cannot move, an off-screen funeral clock, a reachable heir, a quiet PD kinship.
- **Not a briefing service.** No Rennick knows adventure secrets, the lake, Frank Loman's fate, or the machine. They know their name, their ground, and their quarrel.

## Portrayal notes (if a Rennick reaches the table)

Fill a proper NPC file from `templates/npc.md` only when a night puts one on screen; keep it here until then.

- **Margaret** — courteous, immovable, church-and-Grange register. Talks about the town and the family, never "the property." A "no" that sounds like grief, not greed.
- **Warren** — reasonable, a little defensive, Rookton distance in his voice. Frames selling as mercy, not surrender. Knows how the town reads him and is tired of it.
- **Carol** — the one who actually lives with it; deflects to her mother in public. A Halvorsen at home.
