# Mel Corwin

- **Canon status:** authored as true for this table; not yet in play
- **Visibility:** mixed — night 2 established a **station manager** who was not in the building; the name Mel Corwin, his age, his afternoon show, and his books have not been spoken at the table. He has not appeared on-screen.
- **Role:** Owner and station manager, [KCRK 102.3 FM](../locations/kcrk.md). Weekday afternoon classics host. About **67** in January 1986.
- **Current objective:** Keep the station a real local signal that still plays the records he loves. Keep Abigail on the board because she is good. Keep the books and the two-hour block under his hand.
- **Fear:** A stranger with paper taking tape or gear he did not authorize. Looking like a joke to Rookton. Anyone treating his two hours as filler.
- **Leverage/resources:** Keys. Signature on work orders. Advertising ledger. Airtime. Hap Dwyer's phone number.
- **Secret:** none about the consortium.
- **Relationships:** **[Abigail Carr](abigail-carr.md)** — trusts her energy; she does not touch his show or his books. **Hap Dwyer** — calls when gear is sick. **Ricky Boone** — his intern, not hers. **Bev March** — Saturday community hour. **Lilly** — they have not been established as having met. If she walks in he is polite, territorial about logs, and will talk music longer than police business. He likes that a detective took Abigail seriously. He still will not hand originals without a signature he understands.
- **If ignored:** He still does the two hours and the books.
- **What can change their behavior:** A work order he did not sign. Anyone in his chair during his two hours. A badge asking for originals without paper he recognizes.

## Background

- Bought KCRK when mill spots still paid. He kept it on by doing three jobs: owner, station manager, and the weekday afternoon two-hour 1950s–60s block.
- Not a morning body. Graveyard and morning belong to Abigail. On the morning of night 2 he was not in yet.
- **Books** means the advertising ledger, the program log, and who is allowed to sign a work order.

## Portrayal

- First impression: Late sixties, reading glasses, a station jacket over a shirt that has seen a lot of coffee. He moves like the board is his furniture. Vinyl and dust, not a Main Street handshake.
- Voice/manner cue: Call letters, logs, carts, "the board," first names. Times, not speeches.
- Spoken lines:
  - "That's my two hours. She can have the rest of the board."
  - "I sign work orders. She doesn't. Neither does the kid."
  - "You hear a record from fifty-eight, that's me."
- Avoid: Explaining the lake, treating Ray as a known engineer, naming projects, briefing Lilly on the town, knowing the consortium.
- Pressure variants:
  - Cooperate: Finger on a log line. "That's what we ran. That's what I'll swear we ran."
  - Refuse: Hand on the ledger. "I'm not putting my name on that."
  - Threatened: Toward the phone. "Hap's in the book. I'm calling him, not you."
  - Ignored: Cues the next cart. Still does the two hours and the books.
