# Daisy Pell

- **Canon status:** established (Session 2). The Owl-night habits, the Len tie as written below, and the overflow behavior are **authored as true for this table; not yet in play**.
- **Visibility:** player-known
- **Role:** Owns and works Daisy’s. Mill-widow, about 60.
- **Current objective:** Keep the register moving. Remember who takes what.
- **Fear:** Cops turning breakfast into an interview. Being blamed for food poisoning she did not cause.
- **Leverage/resources:** Who came in, who didn’t, who sat with whom. Not GM secrets. Session 2: her place helped Lilly place the two outsiders.
- **Secret:** none established
- **Relationships:** **[Len Pell](len-pell.md)** is her late husband’s brother — her sister-in-law tie is the mill yard’s only social thread into town. Feeds Walt, Kevin, whoever. Session 2: **furious** that Frank’s friends blamed the lake nausea on **Daisy’s food**. Lilly does not believe that story.
- **Wednesdays and Saturdays** (authored as true for this table; not yet in play): those are Owl nights, and Owl nights are slow nights for her — the mill crowd is three doors east drinking instead of eating. She knows where Len is without thinking about it, the same way she knows Walt is in his booth at seven. On a drinking night when he is **not** at the Owl, she notices, and she says so to whoever is on the next stool. It is family, not evidence.
- **When the Owl is shut** (same status): the [Hoot Owl](../locations/the-hoot-owl.md) crowd walks the three doors west to her counter, because there is nowhere else on Main to sit down. A private booking three doors east, down the row, turns an ordinary evening into her busiest night of the month, and she runs it short-handed and unamused.
- **If ignored:** She still pours coffee. The food rumor may keep running without her consent.
- **What can change their behavior:** A scene in the diner; someone stiffing a check; Len coming in angry; anyone repeating the poisoning gossip.

## Portrayal

- First impression: Hands busy. Not performing “small-town charm.”
- Voice/manner cue: Food, weather, names of regulars. No case summaries.
- Spoken lines:
  - “You eating or just wetting the spoon?”
  - “Walt’s booth is Walt’s booth.”
  - (a drinking night, Len not where he should be) “Half that pub is sitting in my booths and Len isn’t one of them.”
- Avoid: Reciting Frank’s file, briefing Lilly on Whitley, knowing anything from the lake she did not hear as diner talk.
- Pressure variants:
  - Cooperate: A refill and one seen fact. “Kevin was in late. Earl wasn’t.”
  - Refuse: “I didn’t ask.” Wipes the counter.
  - Threatened: Goes to the kitchen window. Busy.
  - Ignored: Serves the next stool. Does not call after Lilly.
