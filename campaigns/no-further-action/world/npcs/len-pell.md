# Len Pell

- **Canon status:** authored as true for this table; not yet in play
- **Visibility:** GM until Lilly is on the mill yard after dark
- **Role:** Cinder Creek yard after dark. Daisy Pell’s brother-in-law. Territorial. Still has a key — a **[Rennick](../factions/rennick-family.md)** trust, not a PD arrangement.
- **Current objective:** Keep people off family-feeling ground. Know who is in the lot.
- **Fear:** Salvage thieves, kids, cops treating the yard like a public park. The company name meaning nothing.
- **Leverage/resources:** Keys (held on the Rennicks’ say-so), flashlight, mill-family name, knowledge of which buildings still lock.
- **Secret:** none established
- **Relationships:** **[Daisy Pell](daisy-pell.md)** — his brother’s widow; she feeds him and tells him when he is being a fool. **Earl Voss** sleeping in the lot — Len may tolerate it as unofficial security or resent it; play the tension, do not decide a sitcom. Not PD.
- **Drinking nights** (authored as true for this table; not yet in play): **Wednesday and Saturday** at [The Hoot Owl](../locations/the-hoot-owl.md), same as the rest of the old floor. It is not a secret and not a bender; it is where the mill men still are. The whole town could tell you which nights those are, which means the whole town knows which nights nobody walks the [yard](../locations/cinder-creek-lumber.md).
- **The key on those nights** (same status): he will not carry the Rennick key into a bar — losing it in a pub is the one way he could lose the yard. It hangs on a nail inside the mill office, behind a door the company never got around to fixing and that has never latched properly since. He believes the buildings are locked and that the nail is nobody’s business but his.
- **Tonight** (working May 1986 state, **not history**): the Owl is shut to walk-ins for a private supper, so Len is not drinking, the key is on his belt, and he is walking the yard on a night the whole county would have told you he would not be.
- **If ignored:** He still walks the yard. He does not phone the detective.
- **What can change their behavior:** A car that isn’t Earl’s. Someone cutting chain. Daisy asking him not to make a scene.

## Portrayal

- First impression: Mill coat. Light in the eyes. This is his ground.
- Voice/manner cue: Short. “Who sent you.” Not a tour guide.
- Spoken lines:
  - “Yard’s closed. That includes you.”
  - “Earl’s car I know. I don’t know yours.”
- Avoid: Giving mill-company secrets he does not have, Concordance, a friendly exposition walk.
- Pressure variants:
  - Cooperate: Points at a building. “That’s as far as you go.”
  - Refuse: Chain. “Get the chief if you want a tour.”
  - Threatened: Hands on the light, not a gunfight speech. Calls it trespass.
  - Ignored: Walks the fence. Does not chase Main Street.
