# Kevin Alder

- **Canon status:** authored as true for this table; not yet in play
- **Visibility:** player-known if Lilly works a night or asks who is on the road
- **Role:** Night patrol, Ashgrove PD, 31. Last hire before Lilly. Actually works the beat.
- **Current objective:** Cover the rural roads. Get out of Earl’s shadow. County sheriff did not take him.
- **Fear:** Spending twenty years as the kid who got the leftover shift. Missing something on the dirt tracks because he is alone.
- **Leverage/resources:** Dirt roads in his head, 2am coffee at [Daisy’s](../locations/daisys.md) when the thermos runs out, the fact that he is awake.
- **Secret:** none established. He carries one thing he has never written down — see the trucks below. He does not think of it as a secret; he thinks of it as nothing he could file.
- **Relationships:** Covers for Earl because the town already decided that’s how nights work. Restless. Not in on graft. May resent Lilly as another new badge that isn’t him making detective.
- **Where he is on Wednesdays and Saturdays** (authored as true for this table; not yet in play): the chief’s sheet sends him east — 328 out through Lowater, the lake road, the dirt tracks — on the same nights Earl draws West End and the mill. Kevin likes the rural half and has never had a reason to read the pattern. He is the furthest badge from the mill yard on exactly the nights it matters.
- **The trucks** (same status): since February he has passed **heavy flatbeds on ORE. 328**, tarped and riding low, running east toward Rookton between midnight and two with no load light on the deck. **Eleven of them.** He knows the count the way he knows mile markers, and he has written none of it down, because a truck on a state highway is not a crime and a patrolman who reports traffic gets asked what he wants done about it. If a cop asks him a real question he will produce the number immediately, with dates he is fairly sure of. He cannot tie a single one to the mill lot.
- **If ignored:** He still drives the beat. He does not come find her with a speech.
- **What can change their behavior:** A real night call, praise that is about the work, Earl being forced to stay awake, county lights on the two-lane.

- Voice/manner cue: Roads, mile markers, who was at West End Fuel. Not poetry.

  - “I wanted county. Rookton. This is what they had.”
  - (only if a cop actually asks about the highway) “Eleven since February. Flatbeds, tarped, going east. What was I going to write?”
- Avoid: Sidekick briefing, crush on Lilly by default, explaining mill politics he does not own.
- Pressure variants:

  - Refuse: “I’m not the detective.” Keeps driving.
  - Threatened: Radio procedure. Mentions the chief without loyalty poetry.
  - Ignored: Next loop of the beat. Daisy’s if the thermos is empty.
