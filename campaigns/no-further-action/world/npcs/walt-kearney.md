# Walt Kearney

- **Canon status:** authored as true for this table; not yet in play
- **Visibility:** player-known once Lilly is in Daisy’s or the extra rooms; until then GM
- **Role:** Retired Ashgrove detective (~63). Still in town.
- **Current objective:** Drink his coffee. Keep Doyle from looking sloppy on paper. Stay retired.
- **Fear:** Being dragged back onto a file. Being treated as a museum.
- **Leverage/resources:** Years of case numbers, carbon triplicate habits, Doyle’s deference, a booth at Daisy’s about 7am.
- **Secret:** none established
- **Relationships:** Worked with Tom Doyle for years before retiring. Polite to Lilly; a little deaf to her theories. Irish-Catholic; **St. Brigid** when the visiting priest makes it. Not Whitley’s man and not a briefing on graft.
- **If ignored:** He finishes breakfast and goes home. He does not appear at the precinct to narrate history.
- **What can change their behavior:** Doyle asking a specific old-form question; paper that violates his numbering; someone sitting in his booth.

## Portrayal

- First impression: Kindly fossil. Coffee first. Case numbers, not speeches.
- Voice/manner cue: Slow. Names the form, the year, the envelope. Calls Lilly “Detective” or “the Jersey one,” not a quest-giver title.
- Spoken lines:
  - “If it’s not on the triplicate, it didn’t happen.”
  - “Tom knows where the tabs are. I don’t work there.”
- Avoid: Explaining the Correction, Lake Barrow as magic, Lilly’s New Jersey case, a tour of the 1950s lab as CSI.
- Pressure variants:
  - Cooperate: One old habit. “We initialed the flap. That’s all.”
  - Refuse: Stands to leave. “I’m retired.”
  - Threatened: Goes small and procedural. Asks who sent her.
  - Ignored: Pays Daisy. Walks out. Does not lecture an empty stool.
