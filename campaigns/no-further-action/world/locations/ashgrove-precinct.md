# Ashgrove Police Precinct

- **Canon status:** established as Lilly’s workplace; building scale, empty lab, the 1986 patrol roster, the kept assignment sheets, and the after-hours night line are **authored as true for this table** (patrol and the night line not yet in play)
- **Region/time period:** Ashgrove, January 1986–
- **Function:** Lilly’s workplace. Small-town PD in a building that remembers a richer mill town.
- **First impression:** Old town hall, one floor, too many rooms. Cloakroom and floor wax. A radio room that still sounds like 1958. Empty offices used for dead paper.
- **Persistent truths:**
  - The building is the **former town hall**, one floor, raised in the **1950s** when Cinder Creek money made Ashgrove look complete. **North of Main / ORE. 328, Church–Pell**, between Spruce and Main (Sheet 2). Enough rooms for **three times** the 1986 headcount. **Two cells.** **Radio room.** An **old forensic lab** (period gear, not a modern unit). Real forensics and the sheriff live in **Rookton** (~22 mi east). Kearney’s carbon process, envelopes, a cork board, the **1984 Rook County highway sheet**, and the **Ashgrove town inset** still live in the extra rooms. Outside the county sheet’s patrol tint: **Rook County Sheriff**.
  - **1986 chain:** Chief Whitley; **Sgt. Tom Doyle** (sergeant-detective, ~44); detective Lilly Dawson (new); secretary **Nancy Iverson**.
  - **Day patrol (decent, not in on Whitley’s graft):** **Don Halvorsen** (53) and **Andy Foyle** (~34). They complain about the night logs and drink Nancy’s coffee. Hiring is rare; Andy is only “junior” relative to Don.
  - **Night patrol:** **[Earl Voss](../npcs/earl-voss.md)** (56) sleeps because “nothing happens after eleven.” **[Kevin Alder](../npcs/kevin-alder.md)** (31) actually runs the beat. Town knows. Whitley does not care if headlines stay down. Kevin was the last badge issued before Lilly. The chief writes the weekly assignment sheet himself and the old sheets go into the dead-paper rooms with everything else, so a year of who-was-where-on-which-night is sitting in the building for anyone patient enough to read it.
  - **Walt Kearney** (~63) retired by 1986 and still lives in town. Daisy’s at 7am. Doyle still defers. His tabs and triplicate still run how paper is supposed to look. Lilly is the other working detective with Doyle because of the NJ transfer, not because Walt picked her.
  - **Who answers the telephone, and when** (authored as true for this table; not yet in play): by day the town’s calls land on **Nancy Iverson**’s desk at the front. She goes home in the evening, and Ashgrove has **no standing night clerk in 1986** — the budget never carried one. After hours the incoming town line is switched through to the **night line**, a black phone on the shelf in the **radio room** beside the base set, so that whoever is holding the room can take a call and put a car on it in the same breath. On a normal night that is nobody: patrol is out in the cars and the building is locked and dark, and a caller either lets it ring or reaches **Rook County** in Rookton, who telephone or raise Ashgrove patrol and lose minutes doing it. **If anyone is in the building, it rings for them** — the phone does not care whether it is the desk sergeant, a patrolman writing up paper, or the detective at her desk with the lights on.
  - Whitley can shelf a case. Doyle still works it like a cop. Nancy hears the town.
  - By the **end of the 1986 arc** Whitley is intended off the force (unfair walk after channels, not prison). By **1998** he is already a civilian. A later chief (unnamed until that era is designed) holds command; Doyle/Nancy may still be the house.
- **Current state:** Loman death file closed and signed by Whitley (Session 2). Next 1986 job is months later and unrelated. (January 1986.)
- **People/factions:** See NPC files. Mill yard after dark: **Len Pell** (not PD).
- **Resources and hazards:** Radio, cars, files, local knowledge, political pressure from the sitting chief, empty rooms that keep old process, a lab nobody is qualified to treat as CSI.
- **Secrets:** GM — Whitley’s quiet is partly bought (local graft, not Concordance). Ordinary small-town self-protection remains true on the surface. Earl sleeping is an open secret, not a mystery, and **Earl is not paid for it** — the only thing the chief sells is where the cars are, and only he knows he is selling it.
- **How it changes if ignored:** The precinct’s default is **no further action**.
