# Cinder Creek Lumber (mill yard)

- **Canon status:** authored as true for this table; not yet required by play
- **Region/time period:** West End mill camp, **west of the Ashgrove city limit across Cinder Creek**; mill pond on the 1984 sheets; mill closed ~1982–84; play from January 1986
- **Function:** Dead employer that still owns the skyline. Night-shift geography. West End Middle sits in the old mill-camp orbit.
- **First impression:** Climate / See / Hear / Feel — stacks against rain, chain, puddles in a parking lot sized for a shift that does not come, a flashlight sometimes, a cruiser sometimes.
- **Persistent truths:**
  - Company name on leftover letterhead, the Daisy’s clock, and the stacks: **Cinder Creek Lumber**. **Cinder Creek** comes off **Elk Ridge** country into the **mill pond**. A **rail spur** still enters the yard and runs toward Ashgrove; nothing passenger has used it in years. The yard’s remaining power comes off the **same West End feed as the mill-camp houses** — the company built both — so the yard and those houses go dark together.
  - The mill family is the **[Rennicks](../factions/rennick-family.md)** — they ran the floor for two generations. **Split title:** the Rennicks hold the West End houses and a share of the paper; a **Rookton-side holding** shares the ground and wants to close. Neither can sell cleanly, which is why the yard sits frozen. Strictly local; **not** Concordance.
  - **Yard geography that stays true:** the stacks and the drying sheds fill the middle; the **mill office** sits by the gate with a door that never latched right, and the Rennick key hangs on a nail behind it whenever [Len](../npcs/len-pell.md) is drinking; the empty **scale house** stands just inside the fence on the West End side, its bay open, which is where [Earl Voss](../npcs/earl-voss.md) parks to sleep; the **rail spur** runs in past the stacks with room to back a flatbed against the old loading deck.
  - **The powder magazine** (authored as true for this table; not yet in play): when the company was still cutting its own road, blasting supply was kept in a low concrete **magazine set inland past the stacks**, deliberately away from the buildings, screened by second growth and reachable by a rutted track. Road work stopped years before the mill did, and the magazine was **never properly cleared** — old stock, badly stored, in a bunker nobody has aired since. It is a hazard that exists whether or not a night ever uses it. **Who knows:** men who worked the floor in the road years remember it the way they remember any old company building — including laid-off hands who have no reason to mention it and, in the wrong circumstances, a reason not to. It is on no current town, PD, or insurance paper, no owner has acted on it, and **Chief Whitley does not know it is there.**
  - After dark **Len Pell** is territorial and still has a key — a **Rennick trust**, not a PD arrangement. Earl Voss sometimes sleeps in a cruiser on the lot; that is a relationship, not scenery.
- **Current state:** Closed. Salvage / watch, not production. Working May 1986 state (**authored, not history**): the yard has been coming apart in stages since winter — copper, tooling, and deck plate leaving a piece at a time rather than in one raid — and nobody with standing has walked the buildings closely enough to total it up.
- **People/factions:** Len Pell (on a Rennick key). The **[Rennick family](../factions/rennick-family.md)** (owners of the local name and West End houses; split title with Rookton). Occasional PD. Not a workforce.
- **Resources and hazards:** Empty buildings, rust, places to hide a car, Len’s sense that this is still family ground. The rail spur and the loading deck make the lot easy to load out of. The uncleared magazine inland of the stacks is the real hazard on the ground; treat it as a danger and a clock, never as a required explosion.
- **Secrets:** none established as Concordance. Do not put the consortium in the mill office unless a later night proves a reason. GM — the magazine is the yard’s one buried danger and it is **ordinary industrial neglect**: no one is guarding it, no one has sold it, and the quiet the chief is paid for covers **salvage**, not powder.
- **How it changes if ignored:** The yard stays dark. Len still walks it.
