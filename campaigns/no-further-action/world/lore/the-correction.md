# The Correction

- **Canon status:** established as **GM predetermined truth** for this campaign; **not** player-known
- **Visibility:** GM-only

Working GM name only. It is not a letterhead Lilly can find.

## Terms

- **Continuity consortium:** a private network of wealthy family offices and interlocking holdings.
- **Custodial spine:** the small office able to see and authorize a complete operation.
- **The Correction:** the enduring practice and capacity for making records agree with a permitted official story.
- **Correction operation:** one use of that practice against one incident or cluster of incidents.
- **Machine:** GM shorthand for consortium, custodial spine, and compartmentalized vendors acting together.

## Private origin

In the late 1950s, wealthy family offices create a private continuity consortium. Their holdings include utilities, insurers, regional broadcasters, hospitals, laboratories, legal services, and private security. Government emergency contracts provide access, but private wealth owns the custodial spine.

The legitimate service coordinates contradictory instructions during crises. Its dangerous discovery is simple: if every trusted channel agrees quickly, a temporary account becomes permanent history. Later evidence is interpreted against the first coherent record.

## The manufactured serial killer

In the early 1960s, several member families each have a different death they need contained. The deaths have different causes and perpetrators: there is no real serial killer and no single hidden murderer.

The custodians consolidate those liabilities into one frightening, solvable pattern. Lilly's biological father is a communications technician and low-level crook. His routes, minor record, tools, and poor credibility can be made to fit. He is selected because he is disposable, not because he discovered the consortium.

Dispatch times, recordings, laboratory evidence, and witness chronology are harmonized until the official story becomes stronger than any single contradiction. He is imprisoned for crimes he did not commit. He knows he is not the killer but cannot expose a machine he never saw.

The operation establishes the consortium's enduring logic:

> One monster is publicly survivable. Evidence that institutions cannot be trusted is not.

## Medrick and Simon

Medrick knows the serial-killer conviction is false because she can disprove at least one essential time/alibi in the manufactured chronology. One contradiction cannot overcome the larger fabricated file. Simon's protective bargain requires silence, and Medrick believes telling Lilly would put the family back in danger. She does not understand the machine's reach or structure.

Simon works at a low level inside the evidence operation. He sees enough to know that unrelated deaths were transferred onto Lilly's father. He acquires **narrow leverage** with these required properties:

- it proves one wealthy patron and one intermediary participated in one transferred death;
- it does not reveal the custodial spine or the consortium's scope;
- disclosure would be costly enough that they let Simon leave and live in peace;
- an independent release arrangement makes killing Simon or harming the family riskier than honoring the bargain;
- its exact form, patron, and storage remain open until the relevant adventure is designed.

Simon uses that leverage to leave, protect Medrick and Lilly, and help construct an ordinary family identity. He later marries Medrick. Lilly's paternity and protective identity records are themselves corrected.

## How the machine survives

The custodial spine moves between rotating shells. Most workers see one defensible task:

- a broadcaster follows a continuity instruction;
- a lab reruns or relabels a sample;
- a supervisor changes a disposition;
- a lawyer seals a file;
- an archive contractor replaces original media.

Street killing is enforcement, not the machine's identity. The consortium does not own every institution and cannot alter anything without access, time, and people. A local Correction can fail, leave evidence, or be resisted.

## The synchronization gap

When custodians take multiple records offline and harmonize them, the workflow leaves the same precise missing interval:

- 1986: radio tape and dispatch recordings;
- 1998: analogue-to-digital transfers and vendor systems;
- 2016: live audit trails and automated records.

The gap proves a common process. It does **not** identify the actor, motive, client, or original event.

## Ashgrove node

Lilly's assignment to Ashgrove is ordinary departmental exile and an extraordinary coincidence. The consortium did not place her there.

Ashgrove is one of many old continuity nodes. A local Correction operation is already underway around Frank Loman before Lilly arrives; it is not waiting for her. Her investigation, not her identity, draws later attention.

The apparent use of Lilly's name on the radio comes from an automated capture/replay of recent local police traffic, not advance knowledge of her or a supernatural voice. How and when Lilly can establish that remains adventure preparation.

Ashgrove's surviving records later become dangerous because Lilly disturbed them. The node's existence predates and did not cause her random transfer.

## Supernatural boundary

There is no supernatural. Apparent hauntings arise from manipulated signals, staged evidence, controlled rumor, trauma, and ordinary human fear. Earlier NPCs know only their own evidence and cannot deliver the final explanation.
