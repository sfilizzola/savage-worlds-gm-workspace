# Revelation Ladder

- **Canon status:** planned delivery; **not** session history
- **Visibility:** GM-only

This document protects the 30-year arc from becoming either twelve unrelated nights or one forced clue chain. Only adventure 1 has happened. Later rungs are jobs for future adventure design, not guaranteed events.

Each child adventure remains a real professional case with its own objective and closure. A case can be independent while its **official record** is later touched by a Correction operation. Essential progress must remain reachable through multiple vectors; never require one roll, NPC, or surviving piece of evidence.

When an adventure is written, every essential revelation inside that night receives its own three-clue check. The campaign-scale redundancy at the end of this file does not replace night-level clue design.

## 1986 — effects

### 1. Neblina sobre o Lago — played

- Frank Loman vanishes near Lake Barrow.
- Radio/static phenomena appear to know Lilly's name.
- Chief Whitley shelves the case; Doyle admits he heard the sound.
- Abigail Carr calls about something her station recorded at the time of the disappearance. Ongoing continuity names the station 102.3 FM; that frequency was not spoken in play.
- **Boundary:** ClearWave, Walter Greene, and the Water Authority were not discovered in this session.

### 2. Vozes sem Corpo — first seam

- Begins the morning after night 1, only hours after Whitley shelves Frank's file; drafted outcomes remain preparation, not history.
- The station recording and local infrastructure produce the first **synchronization gap**.
- Ownership or responsibility is deliberately muddy, but no evidence names a national consortium.
- The Water Authority access trail is usable local evidence. Walter Greene and ClearWave remain unused material, not answers.
- Resolve the local case as far as Lilly's choices and evidence allow; do not turn Abigail into an exposition source.

### 3. Action case — independent crime

- The night is a genuine armed police case with live danger and shots.
- The perpetrators and motive are unrelated to the Loman/radio case.
- The live trail can reach **Chief Whitley’s** local graft (protection, quiet files, people who work for him). He is not the consortium and must not lecture the machine.
- Intended destination: Lilly’s work makes his job untenable. If the night fails to remove him, he is still out of the PD by 1998 by another route and still blames her.
- Behind the screen, custodians alter part of the official aftermath to protect an institution with a consortium connection. That alteration is not Whitley’s racket and must not require him as the only clue.
- Do not reveal the cross-case pattern here. Lilly may preserve an original without knowing its later importance.

### 4. Puzzle case — the Ashgrove rhyme

- The case requires reconciling clocks, tapes, logs, and evidence custody.
- Comparing independent files can reveal the same precise missing interval.
- The conclusion is administrative: unrelated events were standardized by the same process.
- If Lilly compares what she learned with her private investigator's old report, a routing mark there supplies the era's personal bruise. It links a process, not Ashgrove, to her family and does not name her father as part of the machine.

**1986 end state:** Lilly has evidence of a repeatable process, not an organization. Ashgrove's cases remain different crimes.

**Before 1998 design:** After nights 1–4 are played and recapped, fill [`between-1986-and-1998.md`](between-1986-and-1998.md). That interstitial is required before writing `federal-in-ashgrove`. Do not treat 1986 concept notes as the twelve-year history.

## 1998 — structure

### 5. Federal in Ashgrove — pattern too clean

- **Gate:** interstitial `between-1986-and-1998.md` filled from the four recaps.

- Lilly remains an Ashgrove detective and becomes an FBI consultant.
- A supposed interstate pattern brings federal attention into her town.
- The evidence looks assembled into a serial narrative rather than discovered from common behavior.
- **Chief Whitley is gone from the PD.** Someone else holds the chair. At least one 1998 night (this child unless another owns it) requires Lilly to ask the former chief something only he would know. He is a civilian with resentment; he does not brief the Correction. Failure to get his help changes cost and vectors, not the existence of the federal case.

### 6. Rhyme out of town — vendor layer

- Human motives and perpetrators differ across the deaths.
- Their records pass through common laboratories, archives, insurers, law firms, or continuity vendors.
- The synchronization gap survives the analogue-to-digital transition.

### 7. Sealed paper — family cost

- A current case creates lawful or earned access to custody and historical records.
- Planned pressure: by this point Lilly's biological father dies in custody from ordinary causes or institutional neglect, not because the machine silences a knowledgeable witness. The exact timing remains open to the designed situation and earlier player choices.
- Simon admits he worked inside one evidence operation and helped alter one part of Lilly's father's file. He points Lilly toward his protected fragment but cannot establish what happened in the other murders.
- His leverage proves one transferred death and one patron/intermediary connection; it undermines the serial narrative but cannot reveal the entire machine or independently clear every charge.

### 8. Dead letterhead — custodial spine

- Rotating shells map back to a small office coordinating compartmentalized vendors for wealthy clients.
- Lilly can expose one Correction operation and protect someone affected by it.
- The custodians sacrifice an active shell or personnel unrelated to Simon's protected fragment and survive. His independently held proof remains capable of authenticating one historic transfer.
- Lilly carries one durable authorization pattern into 2016.

**1998 end state:** Lilly knows a private system exists and can prove one part of it. She cannot yet prove its whole history or stop its capacity.

## 2016 — one urgent case

These four adventures are movements of one live case. Hours or days may pass for sleep, travel, warrants, and evidence work. Delay gives the custodians time to harmonize records, isolate witnesses, and finish the scapegoat.

### 9. Written in real time — the new monster

- Unrelated deaths are being narrated as one new serial killer's work.
- The intended scapegoat is a living, innocent network technician.
- Parts of the official story exist before the supporting evidence is secure.
- Lilly recognizes the logic of 1998 and the synchronization gap.

### 10. Break the vendor — preserve originals

- Lilly must reach source logs, samples, or witnesses before contractors replace them.
- The custodians escalate from administrative obstruction to direct danger.
- Success preserves independent evidence; failure changes time, access, safety, or public belief rather than stopping the case.

### 11. The Dawson file — personal lock

- The live operation provides the model needed to interpret her father's surviving records.
- Simon's narrow leverage authenticates one transferred murder.
- Multiple independent contradictions establish that the historic “serial killer” was manufactured.
- Lilly learns that her fear of inherited murderous violence was built from an official story.

### 12. Ashgrove disposition — break capacity

- Ashgrove is one of many forgotten continuity nodes; it was not chosen for Lilly.
- The custodians move to purge its dangerous archive fragment and finish the new scapegoat.
- Climax objectives: save the target, preserve independently verifiable evidence, expose the authorization chain, and disable the Correction's operating capacity.
- The apparently supernatural pattern resolves into signal manipulation, staged evidence, controlled rumor, and human fear.

**2016 end state:** earlier choices determine what Lilly can prove, whom she can save, which patrons face consequences, and what survives after the custodial spine is broken.

## Revelation redundancy

### Records were altered by a common process

1. 102.3 FM / dispatch synchronization gap (1986).
2. Analogue-to-digital vendor seam (1998).
3. Live audit gap and pre-instantiated narrative (2016).

### A private consortium sits behind the vendors

1. Ownership and routing records.
2. Repeated vendor relationships across unrelated clients.
3. Simon's one authorization fragment.

### Lilly's father was framed

1. Impossible chronology across the supposed serial murders.
2. An original record tied to one transferred death.
3. The 2016 mirror operation against a new scapegoat.

### The fear is human-made

1. Early physical signal evidence permits a technical explanation but does not prove the whole haunting was staged.
2. Later staged evidence and controlled witness accounts weaken the supernatural reading without settling it.
3. Final technical and authorization proof at the Ashgrove node confirms the human method.

## Anti-cliché guardrails

- No all-seeing cabal, matching insignia, inherited bloodline power, or single CEO whose arrest fixes history.
- Lilly is not chosen. Her arrival in Ashgrove is coincidence; her persistence makes her relevant.
- Her father was a flawed low-level crook, not a saint or secret mastermind.
- Simon knows one operation and holds narrow leverage. He cannot brief the campaign.
- Rich patrons have different motives. The custodial practice, not a shared occult faith, connects them.
- The synchronization gap never replaces ordinary detective work.
