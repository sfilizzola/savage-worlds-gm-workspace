## Opening mood

An ordinary Saturday evening shift closes around a small, defensible act of chief's business: a complaint folded away before its heading is read, an errand nobody wants handed to the newest detective. Nothing about it looks like a decision yet. Hours pass in paperwork and a packed diner three doors from a shuttered pub, and then a telephone rings in an empty building because the one person left in it happens to be Lilly. What is actually happening eighty yards behind a mill fence — a professional load-out, months old, with old powder moving toward a truck — has nothing to do with her chief's arrangement in the players' eyes yet. It will, but only for her, and only as certainty she can carry and never file.

## End states

Every end state leaves Lilly **certain** that Whitley is protecting that lot and unable to prove it — the suspicion floor guarantees that even in the branches where she picks up none of the four vectors. How many vectors she reached decides how much she could write down if she were foolish enough to try; none of it is proof.

- **Full success:** the flatbed never leaves the spur, all four men are accounted for, the six cases stay in the county, and Len is warm and on his way to Rookton. The salvage case is hers and Whitley signs it with a compliment. Ned Colfax will talk about the job in a cell tomorrow and will still not say who told them the lot was clear.
- **Costly success:** the case closes, but somebody paid for it — Kevin or Len in the county hospital, or dead, or the powder east on 328 in exchange for four men and a truck.
- **Partial success:** part of the crew is taken and part is gone, or the load is stopped and the men are not. Enough is on the ground that the yard cannot be called kids, and not enough to finish the salvage case tonight.
- **Main-objective failure:** the flatbed reaches 328 with the load and the powder on it, the crew is scattered, and the official story stays as small as it started. The yard, the magazine track, the cut chain, Rita's two calls, the roster, and the fold are all still there in the morning, and the arrangement survives intact into night 4.

Adjudicate what survives; do not let any of it produce a name above Roy Meeks.
