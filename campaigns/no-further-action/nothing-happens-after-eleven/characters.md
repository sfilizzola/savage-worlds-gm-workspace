## Night hook — Lilly Dawson

- **Why she is in the building:** Whitley handed her the Daisy's overflow on Main Street — compliance, keep it civil — and she does not want it. Standing in a packed diner telling a tired crowd to behave is not detective work, so she stays at her desk with paperwork and tells herself she will drift down and help Daisy close around one or two. Her own small act of avoidance is the reason she is sitting twenty feet from the night line at 00:20. Play it as an ordinary human dodge, not as foresight.
- **Professional hook:** a power-loss call is patrol business right up until nobody answers for the beat car, the outage has no weather behind it, and there is a man who should be walking that yard.
- **Institutional pressure:** she was told, in so many words, that the mill lot is not detective work. Going west is a small insubordination before it is anything else, and she will have to write that choice down.
- **Personal pressure:** Kevin is out there with a pattern he has never filed and no rank to protect him. He is the closest thing she has to a peer on nights.
- **Choice pressure:** stopping the truck, finding Len alive, keeping the powder off the load, and keeping the scene clean enough to survive a morning's reading will not all fit into the same minutes.
- **Boundary:** whatever she works out about her chief tonight is certainty, not a case. She cannot file it, and nothing she sees connects him to anything larger than Ashgrove. She learns nothing about the Loman crew, about January, or about any organization; there is nothing here to learn.

## Early removal mitigation

- **Opening risks that could hard-remove a PC:** none. The precinct, Main Street, Daisy's, and the West End houses carry no lethal threat; the first danger is at the mill fence, and only after she chooses to approach it.
- **Telegraphing or recovery built into those risks:** before the yard she gets dark houses with no storm behind them, a beat car nobody is answering, work light where a closed mill should be black, an engine running, and Earl asleep twenty yards inside the fence. The crew would rather leave than fight; the first shots are fired to break contact.
- **Reserve pregen or allied character:** none — the campaign is built for Lilly. Kevin Alder is on scene or minutes out and can hold the scene; Doyle can be called in at a cost in time; Len Pell is a pair of hands if she finds him able.
- **Fictional entry point for replacement:** if Lilly goes down, Kevin drags her clear and the clock advances — the load gets further, the crew scatters further, Len's clock runs. Return control to the player after a short transition with lost position, not a lost session.
- **Procedure for capture/separation without sidelining the player:** the crew restrains or shuts someone in only long enough to move the truck; they do not take hostages or hold ground. Give her the inside of wherever she is put, with something to do in it, and put her back into the night within a scene.

## Later lethality

Danger becomes fully lethal the moment she crosses the mill fence line. The transition is announced by the fiction, not by a roll: light where there should be none, a diesel idling, men loading in the dark, and a yard with no cover between the gate and the stacks. Everyone in the crew is an Extra and every one of them would rather be on the highway than in a gunfight, which makes them dangerous only while they are cornered or panicking — and one of them has already panicked once tonight. The magazine inland of the stacks is a hazard that can kill anyone, including the people who know it is there; it is a clock, never a scripted detonation.
