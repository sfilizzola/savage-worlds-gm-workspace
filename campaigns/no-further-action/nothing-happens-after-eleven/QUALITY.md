# Adventure quality gate

Score this adventure before compiling `RUN.md`. Re-score after a major plot change. Copy this file into every generated adventure.

The five-beat shape is a pacing and completeness diagnostic. It is not a scene script and must not replace story points or flexible situations. Map beats onto reachable situations. Players may hit them in another order, skip an optional situation, or create an unplanned path.

A good adventure does **not** need every Savage Worlds subsystem. Use the mechanic the fiction needs. A forced Chase or Dramatic Task is worse than a straightforward scene that belongs there.

## Coherence (prep)

Pass/fail. Does not change the 120-point total. Fail any row, or omit the logic summary after a pass, and do not compile `RUN.md` as ready.

Copy the one-sentence premise from `ADVENTURE.md`. That sentence is the contract. GM intent and the player-facing briefing are not. False intel is legal if the real problem is already in the starting state and the end states still answer the premise.

**Premise (contract):**

After Whitley steers Lilly onto Main Street, a 00:20 West End power-loss call pulls her and Kevin to the Cinder Creek yard, where she must stop a salvage load-out, account for Len Pell, and write a scene that can be written.

A row passes only if Evidence cites actual prep: premise, truths, starting state, essential story points, and end states, in `ADVENTURE.md` and/or `plot.md` as the adventure uses them. “It feels coherent” is not evidence. An adventure with no `plot.md` still fills this block from `ADVENTURE.md`. **Pass?** is `yes` or `no`, not a 0–5 score.

| Check | Pass? | Evidence |

**Not a fail:** false intel that the outage is kids/weather; optional Daisy's and Doyle; player-chosen dispatch and approach order; powder location as a clock rather than one unique room; end states that leave Whitley unprovable.

```text
Conclusion: end states agree with these paths. This is not a visit order.
```

## Warning signs

An adventure needs revision if any of these is true. Repair before compile.

- [ ] One failed Notice, Persuasion, or Research roll can stop the story. — Not present; Revelation audit and fail rows keep vectors alive (e.g. Ned's line survives to a cell).
- [ ] Most situations can be summarized as “enter room, fight enemies.” — Not present; opening and investigation are social/procedural; combat is optional at the yard.
- [ ] The plot only works if players make one specific choice. — Not present; contact states and multiple routes to SP5.
- [ ] A named NPC, secret, or likely fight appears without quoted lines, a GM Note, or at-hand stats beside that situation (`templates/adventure/SKELETON.md`). — Prep has spoken lines and encounter stats; **RUN.md not yet compiled**, so the SKELETON one-home placement is a compile-time check, not a prep fail.

## One-shot shape

Complete when `format` is `one-shot`. Ideal for testing an adventure, a convention, or a first-time group.

- [x] Target **3–4 hours**, or a longer declared runtime with identified cuts down to 3–4 hours. — 3–5 declared; 3–4 hour core in `ADVENTURE.md` Scope budget.
- [x] Pregenerated characters with immediate connections, or supplied-PC hooks that engage the premise at once. — Lilly via night line; `characters.md`.
- [x] Opening trouble within the first **10–15 minutes** of play (map-only briefing does not count as the opening). — Fold + steer + 00:20 call.
- [x] Roughly **4–6 substantial situations**, not a dungeon of rooms. — SP1–SP6; SP2 optional.
- [x] **One** twist or major complication. — Organized load-out + powder clock; not kids/outage.
- [x] **One** large climax. — SP5 yard.
- [x] Minimal lore and no essential side quests. — No Concordance; Daisy's/Doyle/magazine clock skippable.
- [x] A complete ending even if further adventures are possible. — End states + night 4 hook only.

Format preference when choosing how to publish or test:

1. Focused one-shot
2. Two- or three-session mini-adventure (`short-arc`)
3. Short campaign
4. Open-ended long campaign
