# Rookton haulers — Dale Runkle and Jimmy Sloat

Two named Extras who work for Roy Meeks and travel with the flatbed. Adventure-local; do
not promote them to `../../world/npcs/`. They are one file because they are one job and the
GM will read them together.

**Shared by both.** They are hired scrap haulers out of Rookton, not enforcers. They know
the lot is safe midweek and weekends and have never asked why. They learned there was old
powder up the track at about 00:30 tonight and neither of them likes it. They helped carry
Len Pell into dry kiln No. 2 at 00:02 and have not thought about him since. Neither can
name anybody above Meeks, because neither has ever been told anything (`../secrets.md`,
NHE-04).

They shoot to break contact and to keep the truck moving, never to hold ground and never
to advance on a police officer. Two badges on the fence is a reason to leave, not a reason
to fire — see [`../encounters.md`](../encounters.md), "When the crew fires".

---

## Dale Runkle

- **Canon status:** prepared for this night; not canon and not session history
- **Visibility:** GM until Lilly reaches the loading deck or the inland track
- **Role:** Meeks's driver. Forties, thick through the shoulders, does the loading and all the reversing.
- **Current objective:** Get the bed loaded, get the cases down off the track without dropping one, get home.
- **Fear:** Bogging the pickup in the soft ruts with two cases of 1978 powder on the bed.
- **Leverage/resources:** The crew-cab pickup and the generator on its bed; chains, rope, and the hand winch; a Ruger .22 in the door pocket that he keeps for coyotes and has never pointed at a person.
- **Secret:** none beyond the crew's shared ones.
- **Relationships:** Works for Meeks, drinks with Sloat, has decided Ned Colfax is a liability and says so with his face.
- **If ignored:** Winches, ropes, and drives. He makes three trips up the inland track between about 00:45 and 01:10, two cases per trip.
- **What can change their behavior:** Anybody standing on the inland track. He will not drive at a badge, and he will not fight over a load that is not his.

### Portrayal

- First impression: Backing a pickup in the dark by feel, one arm over the seat, talking to the truck.
- Voice/manner cue: Instructions to objects and to himself. He speaks to people in the same tone he speaks to the tailgate.
- Spoken lines:
  - (backing the pickup, to nobody) "Slow. Slow. Slow."
  - (a light or a figure on the track ahead of him) "I'm stopped. I'm stopped, look — I'm stopped."
  - (to Sloat, at the deck) "Leave it. That's his problem."
- Avoid: Menace. A gun he was not carrying. Any opinion about the mill, the town, or who owns the lot — he is from Rookton and does not care.
- Pressure variants:
  - Cooperate: Out of the cab with the keys held up, and he tells her what is on the bed before she asks, because he wants it off his hands.
  - Refuse: Stays in the cab with the window up and the engine running, looking straight ahead.
  - Threatened: Both hands flat on the wheel. "It's in the door. Driver's door. Take it."
  - Ignored: Three two-case loads down the track, then back on the winch.

## Jimmy Sloat

- **Canon status:** prepared for this night; not canon and not session history
- **Visibility:** GM until Lilly reaches the fence line — on a raise at the fence she picks him out as the man standing off the light with a long gun
- **Role:** The other Rookton hauler. Youngest man on the yard, early twenties, and the only one watching the dark instead of the load.
- **Current objective:** Keep whatever is out past the light away from the deck until the truck is roped.
- **Fear:** What is out past the light. He has been looking at nothing for an hour.
- **Leverage/resources:** A pump shotgun and a coat pocket of shells; young legs; he pulled the pole cutout at the fence at 00:08 because Ned told him which one.
- **Secret:** none beyond the crew's shared ones. He fired at a police officer at 00:52 only on the branch where Kevin came in alone.
- **Relationships:** Takes his instructions from Meeks and his cues from Runkle. Thinks Ned is a coward, which is the closest anyone on this yard comes to being right.
- **If ignored:** Stands off the cone of the light with the gun down and the safety on, and nothing happens.
- **What can change their behavior:** Movement he cannot resolve inside about twenty yards. Meeks telling him to stop. Anyone he can clearly see is police, in the light, not moving toward him.

### Portrayal

- First impression: A young man standing just outside the work light where he can see out and nobody can see in, holding a long gun like it is heavy.
- Voice/manner cue: Shouts before he does anything, and the shout is always a boundary rather than a threat. He does not negotiate and he does not banter.
- Spoken lines:
  - (into the dark, before he fires) "Stay off the deck!"
  - (a second challenge, if he has been given any reason to doubt) "Say something! Say something or I'm shooting!"
  - (surrendering) "It's down. It's on the ground. Don't."
- Avoid: A one-liner. Taking aim at somebody he has clearly identified as police standing still in the open. Any claim about the arrangement, the days of the week, or who pays.
- Pressure variants:
  - Cooperate: Puts the gun on the gravel and steps back from it further than he needs to.
  - Refuse: Backs toward the flatbed keeping the gun between himself and her, saying nothing.
  - Threatened: **He does not fire on reflex.** He fires once — at the noise, not at the person — and runs for the spur gate **only if all three firing conditions in [`../encounters.md`](../encounters.md) hold**: movement at the deck he cannot resolve, his one shout unanswered, and no stop order from Meeks. If he can see it is a police officer, if there are two badges or two lights on the fence, or if Meeks has called rope-and-go, he does not shoot: the gun stays up while he backs toward the flatbed, or it goes on the gravel and he runs.
  - Ignored: Stays off the light, gun down, watching the dark.
