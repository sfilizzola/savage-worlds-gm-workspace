# Roy Meeks

Adventure-local. Tonight he is the top of the chain and he stays the top of it; nothing in
this night may produce a name above him (`../secrets.md`, GM-only boundary). Do not promote
him to `../../world/npcs/` unless a later night brings him back.

- **Canon status:** prepared for this night; not canon and not session history
- **Visibility:** GM until Lilly sees the loading deck
- **Role:** Rookton scrap hauler. Runs the Cinder Creek lot in stages since November 1985, owns the flatbed, holds the buyer, and pays for the quiet.
- **Current objective:** The flatbed loaded and eastbound on ORE. 328, no later than 01:45. Everything else — the copper, the cases, the men — is negotiable against that.
- **Fear:** A shooting charge. He came for scrap and he knows exactly how differently a county treats a man who took a police officer off a lot.
- **Leverage/resources:** The truck, the buyer, the spur-gate padlock key, the cash, and a rule about days of the week that has held without exception for six months.
- **Secret:** He pays a local go-between for the quiet and was given the arrangement as a single sentence — Wednesdays and Saturdays, nobody walks it. He has never met a police officer about it, has never been given a name, and does not know one exists to be given. He learned about the powder magazine from Ned at 23:50 tonight and intends to sell it in Rookton. See `../secrets.md`, NHE-02 and NHE-04.
- **Relationships:** **Ned Colfax** is bought knowledge and a liability he is currently tolerating. **Runkle and Sloat** are his own men and will do what he says. **Len Pell** is, to him, a problem that happened in the dark two hours ago and has not been thought about since.
- **If ignored:** Finishes the load unhurried, ropes it, and drives out the spur gate at 01:45.
- **What can change their behavior:** Police on his ground — any form of it — starts his go-clock. A blocked spur road takes the clock away from him entirely and turns him into a man looking for a trade.

## Portrayal

- First impression: The only man on the deck who is not lifting anything. Coat, gloves, watch turned to the inside of the wrist, checking the load and then the time and then the load.
- Voice/manner cue: Arithmetic, not anger. He talks in loads and minutes, mostly to his own men and mostly in three or four words. To a police officer he is flat and economical and gives up the small thing fast to keep the large one.
- Spoken lines:
  - (to his own men, not to her) "Rope it. We're going."
  - (to his own men, when a badge is on the fence) "Nobody fires on a cop over copper. Rope it."
  - (to a police officer, once) "Scrap. That's the charge you've got. Write that one."
- Avoid: A speech. A threat with a number in it. Any word that implies a police officer, a rank, a schedule, or an envelope — he would not say it to her and would not say it in front of his men. Do not let him be talked, frightened, or bargained into the arrangement; he wants a scrap charge and he knows the difference.
- Pressure variants:
  - Cooperate: Only when the spur road is closed behind him. Hands out from his sides, keys dropped on the deck. "Truck's not going anywhere. Neither am I."
  - Refuse: Nothing. He looks at her and goes back to the rope, and that is the whole answer.
  - Threatened: He prices it out loud in one line and takes the cheaper end. "Then it's the copper. Take the copper."
  - Ignored: Ropes, kills the work light, gets his men up, and pulls out the spur gate.
