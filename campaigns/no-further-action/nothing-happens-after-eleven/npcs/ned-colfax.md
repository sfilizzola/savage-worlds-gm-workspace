# Ned Colfax

Adventure-local. He belongs to this night and, after it, to a cell and a Rook County
docket. Do not promote him to `../../world/npcs/` unless a later night actually brings him
back.

- **Canon status:** prepared for this night; not canon and not session history
- **Visibility:** GM until Lilly reaches the loading deck, the shed row, or the inland track
- **Role:** Laid-off Cinder Creek millwright, mid-fifties, on the crew for what he knows about the buildings. The only local man in the yard tonight.
- **Current objective:** Get the copper aboard, get paid, and get off this lot before anybody opens the door of dry kiln No. 2.
- **Fear:** That the watchman is dead and that he did it. Under that, a smaller and more constant fear of being the man who took Rookton money on a mill floor he used to work.
- **Leverage/resources:** Knows which buildings still lock and which never did, where the copper runs, and that the powder magazine inland of the stacks was never cleared. Bolt cutters, a cable knife, a flashlight. He is the reason the load is this far along at 00:20.
- **Secret:** At 00:02 he swung a 24-inch pry bar at Len Pell above the left ear and helped drag him into kiln No. 2. He also carries the sentence Meeks gave him at hire in November 1985 — the lot is clear on Wednesdays and Saturdays — and he has never known who guarantees it, because he never asked and was never told. See `../secrets.md`, NHE-02, NHE-03, NHE-04.
- **Relationships:** **Roy Meeks** hired him and pays him; that is the whole relationship, and Ned does not like him. **Runkle and Sloat** are Rookton men he has worked four or five nights beside and could not tell you much about. **Len Pell** he knows by sight from the floor, which is the part he cannot get past.
- **If ignored:** Keeps working badly and keeps looking back at the shed row.
- **What can change their behavior:** Being cornered. Being asked directly about the watchman. Anyone saying out loud that the cases are going on the truck. He will not be talked into naming a source he does not have.

## Portrayal

- First impression: A heavy man in a mill coat doing a young man's work, standing slightly wrong, with his hands not doing what his eyes are doing.
- Voice/manner cue: Mill floor. Short flat sentences about objects — hasp, bus bar, kiln, bar. He answers questions he was not asked and goes quiet on the one that matters. The words `clear` and `supposed to` do most of his work.
- Spoken lines:
  - "He came round the sheds. He wasn't supposed to be here. Nobody's supposed to be here."
  - "That stuff up the track is seventy-eight. You don't put seventy-eight on a truck."
  - (**on a success, never on a fail** — once he cannot leave; once only, unsigned, never repeated. The roll and its fail/success/raise are `../plot.md`, SP5.) "We were told the lot's clear Wednesdays and Saturdays."
- Avoid: Naming or hinting at Chief Whitley, the assignment sheet, the police, or an envelope — he has no name and no access to one. A confession speech. Explaining the crew's finances. **Giving the Wednesday/Saturday line on a failed roll** — on a fail he gives his name and asks for somebody in Rookton, and the line waits for a cell tomorrow (`../plot.md`, SP5). Repeating it a second time once it has been given, or signing anything.
- Pressure variants:
  - Cooperate: Gives his own name first, then the buildings, then the copper, and then asks the question he has been carrying. "Somebody needs to go and look at him. North side. The kilns."
  - Refuse: Hands loose at his sides, eyes on the gravel. "I'll talk to somebody in Rookton."
  - Threatened: He is on his knees before the sentence is finished. "I've got cutters. That's all I've got on me."
  - Ignored: Keeps working the winch badly, keeps turning his head toward the shed row, and drops something.
