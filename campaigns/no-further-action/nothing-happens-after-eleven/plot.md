# Plot Model

This is flexible preparation, not a scene sequence or session history. SP1–SP6 are reachable
situations, not a tour; the player may skip, reorder, or invent a route. Clock times below are a
pressure model for the GM, not a timetable to enforce against the table.

## Main throughline

- **GM's desired destination:** Lilly stops or breaks a salvage load-out at the Cinder Creek yard, accounts for Len Pell, keeps the old blasting stock off the flatbed and off ORE. 328, and leaves the night certain that Chief Whitley has been keeping that lot off the record — with nothing she can file.
- **What must remain flexible:** whether she answers the night line herself or the building is empty; whether she rolls at once, rides with Kevin Alder, wakes Sgt. Doyle, or works Main Street first; which of the four graft vectors she actually picks up and in what order; whether Len is found early, late, or by someone else; whether the crew is taken, scattered, or gone; whether the powder is secured at the bunker, intercepted on the track, or lifted off the flatbed.
- **What failure changes:** time, position, Kevin's and Len's condition, how much of the load leaves the county, how much of the crew is available to talk tomorrow, and how small the official story is allowed to be. It does not close the case, erase the yard, or take the four graft vectors off the table.

**Three contact states. Dispatch decides who is west; whether Kevin is alone decides whether 00:52 fires.**

- **Dispatched, Kevin alone.** Lilly answers at 00:20, puts it on the air, and does not roll with him — she stays on Main, waits for Doyle, or keeps paperwork. Kevin is at the camp by about 00:35 and at the fence by 00:41. The 00:41 and 00:52 rows above run on this state **only**.
- **Dispatched, Lilly with him or already on the fence.** Kevin is west, but he is not crossing the apron alone. He may still transmit at 00:41 if he arrives first. **Do not fire the 00:52 beat.** Meeks sees two lights, or a badge already at the gate, and starts his go-clock; the truck can leave before a shot is fired.
- **No dispatch.** She is already on Main at 00:20, or she walks out without raising anybody. The phone rings out, Rita tries again at 00:41 and then sends Gil Sorenson into town at about 00:55, and Kevin reaches the mill camp on his ordinary loop at about 01:20 with no idea anything is wrong. **Nothing is fired at 00:52** — there is nobody at the fence to fire at. The yard stays quiet until somebody walks up to it, and the load is nearly roped when they do.

**Meeks's go-clock.** From the moment Meeks knows police are on his ground — a cruiser at the gate, a
light on the apron, a shouted challenge, a radio he can hear, a shot — he needs roughly fifteen to
twenty minutes to rope what is aboard, kill the work light, get his men up, and pull out the spur
gate. He will not wait past 01:45 whatever happens. The often-quoted 01:10 is simply what that
arithmetic produces on the dispatched-and-alone branch, where he learns at 00:52; it is **not** a
universal departure time. Contact at 00:38 means wheels up around 00:55. Contact after about 01:25
buys him nothing, because 01:45 is the ceiling and he pulls out then, roped or not. If the spur road
is blocked he cannot use the clock at all, and he starts thinking about what he will trade instead.

## Len clock

Len is **not** a scripted corpse and **not** guaranteed to live. He is behind the barred door of dry
kiln No. 2, bleeding from a scalp and skull wound above the left ear, on wet concrete at about 46°F.
Treatment values live in `encounters.md`; what follows is the fiction the GM runs against.

- **Reached by about 01:05 — inside roughly 45 minutes of Rita's 00:20 call, which is the anchor on either branch.** Conscious, concussed, furious, banging the kiln door at intervals — audible inside about 30 yards of the shed row without a roll. Opening the kiln gets him out; it does not make him able to walk. Successful treatment completed within the Golden Hour (which closes at about 01:02), or equivalent support justified by the fiction, is what lets him walk with help and hold a flashlight. He knows four men, the work light, and "the one who swung was a mill hand," but he did not get a name.
- **Reached later (she worked Daisy's first, waited for Doyle, or cleared the yard before searching).** No more banging. Unconscious, breathing, cold. Findable by the blood on the kiln apron and the dropped bar, not by sound. He gives nothing tonight.
- **Reached after the load moves, or found by Kevin or Earl instead.** Deep hypothermia on top of the head injury. This is where he can die, as a consequence of elapsed time and a 22-mile ambulance, not because prep decided it.
- **Never reached tonight.** Daisy raises it in the morning, or his truck is found inside the gate. The yard, the file, and the graft vectors all survive either way.

Rook County's ambulance out of Rookton is about 30 minutes to the yard once it is called, and 30 back.
Calling it early costs nothing and is the single most useful thing Lilly can do for Len before she
ever finds him.

## Story Point 1 - Night Line

- **Purpose:** put the chief's habit in front of the player as ordinary office behavior, hand her a job she does not want, and then ring a telephone she is the only person who can answer.
- **Reachable when:** play begins. Phase A is Whitley's office at about 18:10 Saturday 17 May 1986; phase B is the radio room at 00:20.
- **Situation:** Whitley catches Lilly at the start of the evening shift holding a Citizen Complaint sheet Nancy typed at 4:05 p.m. on Friday 16 May — complainant **Rita Holm, No. 4 Mill Camp Road**, reporting trucks and engine noise at the mill yard late at night, "three or four nights a month." He pencils `Ref. patrol — Voss` on it, folds it so the heading disappears, runs the side of his hand down the crease once while he is talking about something else, and gives her the Daisy's overflow on Main instead: the Owl is shut for a private supper, the whole mill crowd is eating three doors west, keep it civil. He is in his car by 18:25 and the folded sheet is in the `PATROL — READ` basket on the radio-room shelf. By midnight the building holds Lilly and nobody else. At 00:20 the night line rings two feet from that basket.
- **Spoken lines:**
  - Whitley: "Kids, or it's Voss's. Either way it isn't detective work."
  - Whitley: "Half that pub is going to be in Daisy's tonight. Stand where they can see you and keep it civil."
  - Rita Holm, on the night line: "It's not the fuses. I did the fuses. It's the whole row and the mill both."
  - Rita Holm, if Lilly keeps her talking: "I called Friday about the trucks. Nobody came about that either."
- **Essential information:** a power-loss call in the mill camp at 00:20 with no weather behind it; Earl Voss is the unit posted to West End and is not answering; Kevin Alder is in the car and will come if he is asked; Lilly is the only person in the building. Secondary and not required tonight: the chief has a specific way of making a piece of paper smaller.
- **Discoverable rolls:**

| Trait | Fail | Success | Raise |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Notice` while Whitley handles the complaint | She registers that he shrank something and cannot say what. The sheet still ends up in the patrol basket where she can read it any time tonight. | She sees the fold and the single crease and could describe the habit to another cop. | She reads the heading before it disappears: Citizen Complaint, 16 May 1986, **Rita Holm, No. 4 Mill Camp Road**, trucks at the mill at night. |
| `Common Knowledge` on the errand | It looks like a chief giving his newest detective a nothing job, which it also is. | She knows the Owl is shut for **Oscar Lind's** send-off supper, so the mill crowd is not where it normally is on a Saturday. | She also knows Saturday and Wednesday are the drinking nights — which means Saturday is a night nobody walks the Cinder Creek yard, and the whole town could have told her that. |
| `Notice` on the telephone call | She takes the name, the address, and the complaint. | She hears that Rita already checked her own fuse box and her neighbour's porch before she picked up the phone. | Rita volunteers that she telephoned on Friday about trucks at the mill and nobody came — giving Lilly the complaint before she has read the folded sheet. |

- **Independent vectors:** Rita Holm on the night line; the folded complaint in the `PATROL — READ` basket; Nancy Iverson's carbon of the same complaint in the Friday day file; the radio — Earl's silence since his 23:10 "West End clear" and Kevin answering at once; the duty board showing Voss on West End and Alder on 328 east.
- **Escalation:** every minute she spends here is a minute Kevin spends arriving alone. If she never answers the phone — she is already on Main, or she walks out — Rita rings again at 00:41, then sends her neighbour **Gil Sorenson** of No. 6 into town, and Kevin reaches the mill camp on his ordinary loop at about 01:20 instead of 00:35.
- **Failure consequences:** missing the fold costs her nothing tonight; the sheet and its carbon both survive to SP6. Handing the call to patrol and keeping the Main Street errand is a legitimate choice, not a trap — it moves the night into SP2 and puts Kevin at the fence alone.
- **Links forward:** SP3 if she rolls west; SP4 if she rolls west with or behind Kevin; SP2 if she keeps the Main Street job; SP6 for the sheet in the basket at any hour.
- **Optional/essential:** essential opening. No single outcome inside it is required.

## Story Point 2 - Owl Overflow (Daisy's)

- **Purpose:** the parallel route. Give the assigned errand real content, and make sure the night still reaches her if she sat on the radio.
- **Reachable when:** any time after about 19:30, and specifically if Lilly took the Main Street assignment, handed the call to patrol, or left the building before 00:20.
- **Situation:** Daisy's is jammed three doors west of a pub that is shut — thirty-odd mill people who expected beer and got a diner, coats on the backs of chairs, the counter radio losing to the room. Daisy Pell is working it herself and is not enjoying it. The crowd is loud, good-natured, and entirely uninterested in a detective standing by the register. Through the west windows, Main Street and West End Fuel's canopy are lit and the mill-camp porch lights are not. Nobody in the room has connected those two facts.
- **Spoken lines:**
  - Daisy: "Half that pub is sitting in my booths and Len isn't one of them."
  - Daisy, if pressed about the key: "He won't take that key into a bar. Never has. If he's not drinking he's out there."
  - Gil Sorenson, if it gets to 01:00 with nobody working the call: "Rita's been on the phone to you people twice. Whole row's black and there's a light up in the mill."
- **Essential information:** Len Pell is not with the crowd on a night he would be; the Owl was shut, so his routine broke; the mill camp is dark while the town is lit. Optional and useful: Kevin came through for coffee at about 23:20 and Earl has not been in at all.
- **Discoverable rolls:**

| Trait | Fail | Success | Raise |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Persuasion` with Daisy across a full counter | She is slammed and gives one sentence: the Owl is shut and half of it is in her booths. | She says Len is not among them, on a night he would be, and that she has been half-watching the door for him. | She adds that he will not carry the Rennick key into a bar, so if he is not drinking he is on the lot — and that his truck went west past her window at about half past nine. |
| `Notice` from inside the diner | Noise, wet coats, and a crowd. | West along 328 the mill-camp porch lights are out while Main Street and West End Fuel are lit — whatever this is, it is not the town. | She can put a time on it: the row went dark while she was standing there, a little after ten past twelve. |
| `Common Knowledge` on the room | Thirty faces and no use. | Earl Voss is not here and has not been; Kevin Alder came through for coffee at about 23:20. | Lind's supper was booked in April and anyone who actually drinks at the Owl has known for weeks that tonight was not a drinking night — which the men working the yard plainly did not. |

- **Independent vectors:** Daisy Pell; the dark row visible west along 328; Gil Sorenson at about 01:00; the Owl emptying onto Main at 01:15 with Pat Calhoun in the doorway. Only if the call went out: her own portable carrying Kevin's traffic. The pops at 00:52 only if he was dispatched **and** went in alone.
- **Escalation:** **00:52 pops only if Kevin was dispatched and went in alone.** Then a short string of flat pops carries across Cinder Creek; the west end of the diner hears it and argues about whether it was a truck, Daisy's cook comes out front, and if Lilly is still here after that she is working with a fifteen-minute-old shooting. **If she rolled with him she is not in this diner.** **If nobody raised Kevin** — she was already on Main at 00:20, or she left the room without putting it on the air — there is nothing to hear at 00:52 and the diner stays a diner. On that state the night reaches her through Gil Sorenson at about 01:00, the Owl emptying at 01:15, or not at all until Kevin drives into it on his own loop at about 01:20. Do not fire the 00:52 beat to force her west.
- **Failure consequences:** time. Every vector in this point is repeated elsewhere; none of it is required, and staying for all of it costs Len position and costs Kevin cover.
- **Links forward:** SP3 or SP4 west across the creek; SP1's radio room if she goes back for the car and the sheet.
- **Optional/essential:** optional. Skippable without blocking anything, and the likeliest cut if the table is running short.

## Story Point 3 - Mill Camp Road

- **Purpose:** confirm the outage is not weather and is not kids, put a person behind the telephone call, and turn her toward the fence on evidence rather than on a hunch.
- **Reachable when:** she drives west out of town on 328 and turns onto the gravel loop at the mill camp. Six to seven minutes from the precinct in the day car.
- **Situation:** eight company houses on a gravel loop that the 1984 sheets do not name; five still tenanted. Every porch and window on the row is dark. No wind, no downed limb, no storm — the drizzle stopped around 23:00 and the ground is wet and still. Rita Holm is on her step with a kerosene lamp and a coat over her nightclothes. Gil Sorenson at No. 6 has been out to the road twice. Past the row, across the mill road, the fence line of the Cinder Creek yard is a black edge with one hard white light behind it, well back toward the rail spur, and something running.
- **Spoken lines:**
  - Rita Holm: "The mill and us are the same wire. Always have been. It goes, we go."
  - Rita Holm: "Three, four nights a month. Late. Going east, loaded. That's what I called about Friday."
  - Gil Sorenson: "That's not a flashlight. That's a work light."
- **Essential information:** the yard and the mill-camp houses share the company-built West End feed, so the houses going dark means something happened on the yard side; the outage began a little after 00:08; there is working light and a running engine inside a closed mill; Earl Voss's car is not on the street where the roster says it should be; Rita has heard loaded trucks leaving east three or four nights a month.
- **Discoverable rolls:**

| Trait | Fail | Success | Raise |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Persuasion` with Rita Holm | She repeats the complaint she has already made twice tonight. | She gives the shared feed — mill and houses on one wire, always — and that she called Friday about trucks. | She gives times: the lights went a little after ten past twelve, the trucks run three or four nights a month between midnight and two, and they go east. |
| `Notice` from the road | Dark houses and no storm. She has confirmed an outage and nothing else. | Light inside the fence, well back by the spur: hard, white, fixed — not a flashlight — with an engine under it. | She also separates a diesel idling from a small two-stroke, and places the light at the loading deck on the far side of the stacks rather than at the mill office by the gate. |
| `Notice (-2)` on the mill road gravel | Wet gravel and standing water. | Heavy dual-wheel tracks turning in at the rail-spur road rather than the main gate, in tonight. | Two sets: one laid down tonight and an older ridge underneath it, holding its shape under the new water. This has happened before. |

- **Independent vectors:** Rita Holm; Gil Sorenson; the dark row against a lit town; the light and engine noise inside the fence; the dual-wheel tracks at the spur road; Earl Voss's absence from the street he is posted to; the power company's Rookton answering service, which will not have a truck out before 2 a.m. and will say the trouble is on the mill lateral.
- **Escalation:** standing on the road is safe and stays safe. What advances is the load: copper is already aboard, the deck plate is going on, and the inland track has not been driven yet. A radio call from here reaches the base set cleanly; her portable is patchy out at the fence and much worse inside the yard.
- **Failure consequences:** none physical. Missing the tracks or the engine note costs her the reversal in advance — she walks up to the fence expecting a power cut and finds a load-out instead.
- **Links forward:** SP4 at the fence; SP5 if she goes straight through the gate; back to SP1's radio room for Doyle, Rook County, or an ambulance she has not called yet.
- **Optional/essential:** essential as a route, but no single witness or observation in it is mandatory.

## Story Point 4 - Fence Line and Earl's Nest

- **Purpose:** deliver the reversal in a place where she can still choose. Give her the crew, the truck, the missing watchman's vehicle, a sleeping patrolman, and the last moment before the yard becomes lethal.
- **Reachable when:** she comes up the mill road to the yard fence, with Kevin, ahead of Kevin, or after him.
- **Situation:** the main gate's chain hangs open — Len unlocked it and never closed it. Ten yards inside, the mill office door stands ajar the way it always has; the nail behind it is empty. A primer-gray F-100 is nosed up beside the office. Twenty yards further, in the open bay of the empty scale house, Ashgrove car 1 sits nose to the gate with its windows fogged, radio muttering, and Earl Voss asleep in it. Sixty yards of open gravel apron separate the gate from the first stacks, with nothing on it to stand behind. Beyond the stacks, at the loading deck on the rail spur, a 500-watt lamp clamped to the deck rail throws a cone about twenty-five yards wide; inside it four men are working a hand winch and a flatbed backed against the deck, with a crew-cab pickup nose-out at the spur gate and a generator running on its bed. The spur-gate chain was cut months ago and rehung with a padlock that is not the Rennicks'.
- **Spoken lines:**
  - Kevin, on the air or at her window: "Camp's black and there's a light in the yard. Earl's not answering and I've called him twice."
  - Kevin, if she asks him a real question about the highway: "Eleven since February. Flatbeds, tarped, going east. What was I going to write?"
  - Earl, woken: "Len's truck's been sitting there since before I shut my eyes."
  - Earl, pushed toward the gate: "Call Kevin. This is Kevin's."
- **Essential information:** four men, a loaded flatbed, a second vehicle, and working light inside a mill that has been closed since 1984 — this is theft in progress, not an outage; Len Pell's truck is inside the gate and the key nail is empty, so the watchman is on this ground somewhere; Earl has been asleep since about 23:50 and saw Len's truck arrive well before that; the apron between the gate and the stacks has no cover on it.
- **Discoverable rolls:**

| Trait | Fail | Success | Raise |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Notice` at the fence line | She has light, an engine, and movement — she cannot say how many men or what they are loading. | Four men, a flatbed backed to the deck, a pickup at the spur gate, a work light on the deck rail, copper and plate going aboard. | She also places the man who is not working — Sloat, standing off the light with a long gun — and picks out the primer-gray F-100 by the mill office. |
| `Notice` inside the mill office | The office is as dead as it has been for four years. | The nail behind the door is empty and there is a fresh wet boot track on the floor. | Len's thermos is on the desk and still warm — cold inside another forty minutes, which is a clock she can read. |
| `Persuasion` or `Intimidation` waking Earl Voss | He is awake, frightened, and wants it to be Kevin's problem. He will not leave the car. | He gives what he actually saw: Len's truck in before 23:10, nothing since, and he has heard nothing at all. | He stays useful — works the radio to the base set and Rook County, or puts car 1 across the spur gate. He still will not walk into the yard, and he has nothing to confess, because there is nothing. |

- **Independent vectors:** the light, the winch, and the diesel; Len's F-100 and the empty nail; Earl Voss's memory of the truck; Kevin's eleven counted flatbeds; the cut-and-rehung spur-gate chain; the pulled pole cutout at the fence.
- **Escalation:** on the dispatched-and-alone branch Kevin crosses the apron at about 00:52 and is fired on. If Lilly is here with him, the crew sees two lights instead of one and Meeks starts thinking about leaving rather than shooting — the truck can go before a shot is fired. If nobody was ever dispatched, the fence is quiet until she walks up to it herself. In every version, the moment anyone in the cone of that work light knows a badge is on the fence, Meeks's go-clock starts and wheels-up is fifteen to twenty minutes later, not 01:10 by default — see the night clock.
- **Failure consequences:** being seen early costs surprise and starts Meeks's go-clock. The crew drops the winch and takes escape positions — Runkle to the pickup, Sloat covering the spur, Meeks on the deck to rope and go. **Do not force shots at the fence when Lilly and Kevin are together.** Gunfire is not the cost of being seen; the cost is a truck that is already leaving. Armed resistance remains possible later, in SP5, if police block the load-out or the crew is cornered; that is not manufactured here. Waking Earl badly costs a minute and a witness who is no use; it does not alert the crew, who are eighty yards away behind a running generator.
- **Links forward:** SP5 through the main gate, along the fence to the spur, or up the inland track; SP3 if she withdraws to the road to call; SP6 regardless of how SP5 ends.
- **Optional/essential:** essential as the reversal. Which piece of it she reads first is open.

## Story Point 5 - The Load-Out

- **Purpose:** the climax home. A dark wet yard with four Extras, a truck that wants to leave, a man behind a barred door, and six cases of old powder moving toward the flatbed.
- **Reachable when:** she crosses the fence line, by the main gate, along the fence to the rail spur, or up the inland track to the magazine.
- **Situation:** copper bus bar and cable are already roped on the flatbed with four bundles of deck plate; Runkle and Sloat are working the winch; Meeks is on the deck checking the load and the time; Ned Colfax has not been right since 00:02 and keeps looking back at the shed row. Ned told Meeks about the magazine tonight and Meeks intends to take it — a Rookton buyer will take old stumping powder without a conversation. Where those six cases are depends entirely on the clock: all six in the bunker until about 00:45; then three two-case pickup trips, with cases split between bunker, transit, and flatbed as the trips advance; all six roped under the tarp after about 01:10; gone east if the flatbed rolls. Len is behind the dropped bar on dry kiln No. 2 in the shed row, out of the light. None of these men came here to shoot a police officer, and all four of them will if they are cornered between the deck and the fence.
- **Spoken lines:**
  - Meeks, to his own men, not to her: "Rope it. We're going."
  - Sloat, into the dark, before he fires: "Stay off the deck!"
  - Ned Colfax, taken or cornered: "He came round the sheds. He wasn't supposed to be here. Nobody's supposed to be here."
  - Ned Colfax, once he cannot leave, unsigned and never repeated: "We were told the lot's clear Wednesdays and Saturdays."
  - Len, if he is still conscious behind the door: "Whoever that is — the bar's on the outside."
- **Essential information:** this is months of organized salvage, not one night's raid; Len is alive behind kiln No. 2 and on a clock; there is old blasting stock in this yard and it is moving toward the truck; one of these men was told this lot is safe on Wednesdays and Saturdays, and he will not say by whom.
- **Discoverable rolls:**

| Trait | Fail | Success | Raise |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Notice (-2)` searching the shed row in the dark | The shed row is one long identical wall and she loses two minutes to it. Len's banging, if he is still conscious, gives him away anyway inside thirty yards. | Blood on the concrete apron and a 24-inch pry bar dropped where it was thrown. | The bar came down across the latch of kiln No. 2 and the door bar is down — she can open it without searching the rest of the row. |
| `Notice` on the flatbed's load | Tarped freight, roped down, nothing legible. | Copper bus bar, cable, and mill deck plate — a professional load, not a night's scavenging. | Stencilled wooden cases under the tarp, or the empty space and the fresh drag marks where they are about to go, plus the cut hasp track up the inland corner. |
| `Persuasion` or `Intimidation` with Ned Colfax, once he cannot leave | He gives his name and asks to talk to somebody in Rookton. He says it anyway tomorrow, in a cell, to her or to Doyle — the vector survives at a cost in time and in how the morning goes. | He gives the shape of it — months, a Rookton man running it, himself hired for what he knows about the buildings, his bar that hit the watchman — **and the line**, unsigned and never repeated: they were told the lot was clear on Wednesdays and Saturdays. Pushed for who told them, he stops talking. | He can date and place it: Meeks said it to him at hire in November 1985, in one sentence, and it has held every Wednesday and Saturday since without a single exception. ### The suspicion floor

This is the part of the revelation that is not discoverable, because it is not a clue — it is something that happened to her, and it happens in every branch of the night including the ones where she reads nothing, rolls nothing, and never hears a shot.

At about 18:10 the chief of police told her, in person, that the Cinder Creek lot was kids or Earl's business and not detective work, and handed her Main Street instead. Somewhere between midnight and dawn that same lot is revealed as a months-long salvage load-out — organized scrap theft, a watchman on the ground, a truck being made ready to leave. Whether anyone fired is irrelevant to the shape of it. In the morning the same man hears it from Doyle first, backs her loudly in front of the day shift, signs the salvage close, and treats the night as finished police work with nothing about his assignment of that lot needing to be explained.

She does not need the fold, the roster, Ned, or Kevin to know what that means, and she cannot do anything with it — a chief is allowed to be wrong about a complaint, allowed to send a detective to Main Street, and allowed to be pleased afterwards. **Every ending therefore ends the same way: she is certain he is corrupt, and she cannot prove it.** The four vectors decide how much language she has for why; they do not decide whether she is certain, and nothing tonight decides whether she leaves with proof, because there is none to get.

## Possible end states

Every end state leaves Lilly **certain** that Whitley is protecting that lot and unable to prove it.
The suspicion floor above guarantees that even in the branches where she picks up none of the four
vectors. How many vectors she reached decides how much she could write down if she were foolish
enough to try; none of them decides whether she ends the night certain, and none of them is proof.

- **Full success:** the flatbed never leaves the spur, all four men are accounted for, the six cases stay in the county, and Len is warm and on his way to Rookton. The salvage case is hers and Whitley signs it with a compliment. Ned Colfax will talk about the job in a cell tomorrow and will still not say who told them the lot was clear.
- **Costly success:** the case closes, but somebody paid for it — Kevin or Len in the county hospital, or dead, or the powder east on 328 in exchange for four men and a truck. The file survives a morning's reading and so does the gap between what she knows and what she can write.
- **Partial success:** part of the crew is taken and part is gone, or the load is stopped and the men are not. Enough is on the ground that the yard cannot be called kids, and not enough to finish the salvage case tonight.
- **Main-objective failure:** the flatbed reaches 328 with the load and the powder on it, the crew is scattered, and the official story stays as small as it started. The yard, the magazine track, the cut chain, Rita's two calls, the roster, and the fold are all still there in the morning, and the arrangement survives intact into night 4.
- **Unexpected outcome:** Meeks trades — the truck and his own men for a road out; Earl does something in the last five minutes that he will never be able to explain; Ned walks up to the fence with his hands out before anyone fires; a case goes up on the inland track and rewrites the yard. Adjudicate what survives; do not let any of it produce a name above Roy Meeks.
