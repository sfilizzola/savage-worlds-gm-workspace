## One-sentence premise

After Whitley steers Lilly onto Main Street, a 00:20 West End power-loss call pulls her and Kevin to the Cinder Creek yard, where she must stop a salvage load-out, account for Len Pell, and write a scene that can be written.

## GM intent

Deliver the arc's **action** night: a solo 3–5 hour case with shots, a live clock, and a yard she has to walk into, weighted more toward pressure and movement than night 2's evidence work. The crime is ordinary and complete on its own terms — organized scrap theft from a dead mill — and it is solvable by police work tonight.

Underneath it, let her discover that her chief has been keeping that lot off the record, and let her discover it the way a working cop would: a habit she watched hours earlier, a roster that reads badly, a thief's unsigned line, a patrolman's uncounted trucks. She ends the night **certain and unable to prove it**. That gap is the point of the night; do not resolve it here. Evidence and Whitley's unfair walk belong to night 4 (`ashgrove-puzzle-1986`).

Keep the texture human throughout. No gap, no nausea, no altered record, no Concordance and no consortium — a machine touching paper on the same night would poison the one tell that means Whitley and only Whitley.

## Player-facing mission

Answer the West End call, take the crew, and account for Len Pell.

## Predetermined truths

Facts that are true regardless of player action. Keep these distinct from planned events; none of this has happened at the table.

- Cinder Creek Lumber's yard has been stripped in stages for **months**, not raided once.
- The crew works nights when the watchman is reliably absent: **Len Pell drinks at The Hoot Owl on Wednesdays and Saturdays**, which the whole town knows.
- On those nights Chief Whitley runs a quiet arrangement for cash: **Earl Voss is posted to West End and the mill**, **Kevin Alder is posted away**, and whatever Lilly draws is small and in town or out at the lake. He steers assignments; he never warns the crew directly.
- Whitley is paid **cash at a remove**, through a local go-between. No scrap hauler ever hands the chief money.
- Whitley knows only *salvage — keep the lot unpublished*. He does **not** know about the blasting magazine.
- Whitley is **not** Concordance and knows nothing of the consortium. This is ordinary small-town graft.
- **Earl Voss is not bought.** He is genuinely asleep in a nested cruiser from roughly midnight to 5 a.m., he has no envelope, and he has nothing to confess. He is a hole in the beat, not a conspirator.
- **Tonight is one of those nights** — the working date is **Saturday 17 May 1986**.
- **Tonight The Hoot Owl is closed** to walk-ins for Oscar Lind's mill old-shift send-off supper. The Owl crowd floods **Daisy's** for food.
- **Len does not sit in Daisy's** while his pub holds a private party. He goes to the yard instead, with the Rennick key on his belt. The crew keyed off the day of the week and never checked the Owl door.
- The crew is mixed: local ex-millwright **Ned Colfax**, laid off around 1983, who knows which buildings still lock, where the copper runs, and that the **powder magazine** past the stacks was never cleared; plus Rookton scrap haulers **Roy Meeks** (runs the night, pays for the quiet), **Dale Runkle**, and **Jimmy Sloat**, with the flatbed and the buyer.
- The Rookton men are plain scrap thieves. They are **not** the Rookton-side title holder in the Rennick split-title quarrel. The Rennicks stay off-screen.
- The magazine holds old, badly stored stock in a leftover bunker set away from the buildings. It is a hazard and a clock, **not** a required explosion.
- The crew kills the yard's remaining light and pulls power for their own gear. Yard and mill-camp houses share the same West End feed, so **the houses go dark too**. Tenant **Rita Holm** telephones the precinct at about **00:20**.
- **Len walked into the loading crew.** They are thieves, not killers; the local panicked. Len's condition runs on a clock rather than a pre-decided outcome.
- **Kevin has been counting heavy tarped flatbeds on ORE. 328 since February** — eleven, east toward Rookton, between midnight and two — and never wrote any of it down, because a truck on a state highway is not a crime.
- After midnight there is **no standing night clerk**: the town line switches to the precinct **night line**, a phone in the radio room. Nancy is home, Doyle is home, Kevin is already in the car westbound, and Lilly is the only person in the building. She answers.
- The morning after, Whitley does **not** try to shrink a shooting with his own detective on scene. He backs her loudly, signs the salvage close, praises the work, and quietly lets the arrangement die.
- **Nothing supernatural occurs and nothing in this night's paper has been altered.** There is no synchronization gap, no missing interval, no nausea, and no Concordance operation anywhere in it.

## Objectives and stakes

### Main objective

Stop the load-out, account for Len Pell, and bring the night home as a scene that can be written — bodies, brass, truck, and a file that survives a morning's reading. The objective stays reachable whether she rolls at once with Kevin, rolls late, arrives after the shooting starts, or reaches the yard with the flatbed already moving.

### Secondary objectives

- Keep Len Pell alive and get him treated.
- Keep the powder out of the load and off ORE. 328.
- Keep Kevin Alder — and herself — out of the casualty column.
- Take the crew rather than scatter it; a man in a cell is a man who can talk tomorrow.
- Notice what the night says about her chief: the tucked fold, the assignment roster, the thief's unsigned line, Kevin's counted trucks. This is **certainty, not a filed graft case** — see the resolution boundary below.

### Stakes

- **If Lilly acts quickly:** Kevin does not go in alone, Len is reachable while his clock is early, the truck is still being loaded, and the powder is still in the bunker where it can be secured rather than chased.
- **If Lilly delays:** the load advances and the night gets louder — a second call, Kevin exposed if he was sent in alone, Len's clock running. Shots carry across the creek to Main **only** if Kevin was dispatched and went in alone; if she is with him, or if nobody was raised, delay is quiet at Daisy's and costly at the yard. Delay costs people and position; it does not close the case or hide the yard.
- **If Lilly fails:** the flatbed reaches 328 with part of the load, some or all of the crew is gone, the official story stays small, and the arrangement survives intact into night 4. The yard, the magazine, Rita's call, the roster, and the fold all still exist in the morning.

**Resolution boundary (the arc split).** Every ending leaves Lilly certain that Whitley is protecting that lot and unable to prove it. **Forbidden this night:** a ledger, a signed statement, cash in hand, a confession, or a chain-of-command packet that names him. Tonight's salvage file is ordinary police paper, not proof of payoffs. Night 3 does not take Whitley off the force.

> Hey, hey, it's me. Abigail. I know, I know, you're probably still getting ready or already out the door, but I had to complain to somebody and you're my favorite person to complain to lately, so.
>
> Three. Night shifts. In a row. Whitley posted your rotation and I about lost it when I heard — and today's a *Saturday*, Lilly, the one day we could've actually done something instead of me just yelling case notes at you across the KCRK counter for twenty minutes. I had this whole thing planned. Okay, I didn't have a whole thing planned, but I was *going* to plan a thing, and now you're gonna be stuck behind a desk till who knows when and I'm gonna be behind my board till two in the morning, and neither of us gets a night off. It's criminal. You should arrest whoever's in charge of scheduling. Oh wait.
>
> Remember two weeks ago when you fell asleep in the studio chair waiting for me to finish my shift and I had to wake you up because Mel almost walked in? I still think about that. You looked so unimpressed with the entire world. It was great.
>
> Random fact for you, since I know you like collecting those about me for some reason — I have never once in my life eaten a whole sandwich without taking the crust off first. Three years old, twenty-three now, doesn't matter. Some habits just stay.
>
> Anyway — okay, actual question, since you always have an opinion on everything even when nobody asks: I've got two records fighting for the same slot tonight and I can't decide, and Mel's useless for this kind of thing, he'd just tell me to play whatever's shortest. So — Whitney, or the Van Halen? You've heard both enough times by now, you must have a take.
>
> Call me back if you get five minutes between whatever it is you're doing over there. Or don't, and I'll just tell you at the counter whenever your shift lets you breathe. Miss you already, this is ridiculous. Bye, Lilly.

## Starting state

Saturday evening, **17 May 1986** (working date), at the Ashgrove precinct. Whitley catches Lilly at the start of the evening shift with last night's mill-noise complaint in his hand — folds it so the heading disappears, runs the side of his hand down the crease once while he is talking about something else, calls it kids or Earl's business and not detective work, and hands her the **Daisy's overflow** on Main Street instead: the Hoot Owl is shut for a private supper and the whole mill crowd is eating three doors west. Compliance, keep it civil, nothing that needs a detective.

She does not love the errand. The prepared default is that she stays at her desk with paperwork, intending to drift down to Main and probably help Daisy close around one or two; the player may instead walk Main early, and the night still reaches her. By midnight the chief is at home, Doyle is at home, Nancy is long gone, Earl is nested inside the mill fence, and Kevin is in the car on the east side of the beat.

At about **00:20** the night line rings in the radio room: Rita Holm, West End, her lights and her neighbours' gone with no storm in it. Lilly is the only person in the building who can pick it up. Her immediate decision is what to do with a power-loss call — hand it to patrol and keep her assigned errand, ride with Kevin, roll alone, or spend minutes waking Doyle first.

## Radio brief

- **Exact date / time window:** working date Saturday 17 May 1986, from the start of the evening shift to the morning after; the campaign calendar date is a working pin, not established play.
- **Diegetic sources:** precinct office and radio-room set, Daisy's counter radio, patrol-car radio, Earl's nested cruiser muttering to itself, the Hoot Owl's bar radio behind a closed door.
- **Station or source format:** small-market contemporary pop/rock with local announcements, weather, ads, and a human late-night presence; the same KCRK 102.3 FM rotation Ashgrove hears everywhere else.
- **Era texture:** FM hiss, a station that thins out after midnight, radio traffic over music, a car radio left on low, a diner radio losing to a crowd.
- **Tracks available by this date:** all in national rotation for the chart week of 17 May 1986 — Whitney Houston, "Greatest Love of All"; Van Halen, "Why Can't This Be Love"; The Outfield, "Your Love"; Phil Collins, "Take Me Home"; Miami Sound Machine, "Bad Boy"; Mike + The Mechanics, "All I Need Is a Miracle"; Journey, "Be Good to Yourself". Pet Shop Boys' "West End Girls" is also in heavy rotation and is period-correct; the collision with the West End call is a coincidence of a neighbourhood name, so use it only if it lands as texture and drop it if it plays as a wink.
- **Evidence boundary:** music is atmosphere only. Nothing broadcast tonight is evidence, no recording is in play, no lyric is quoted, and no cue points at the crew, the magazine, or the chief. There is **no** cue on the yard itself once the lights are out.

## Scope budget

- **Essential story points:** Precinct (the fold, the Main Street steer, the 00:20 night line); West End houses (the outage is not weather and not kids); Mill fence and Earl's nest (Kevin, the sleeping cruiser, the lit buildings, the truck); Cinder Creek yard (the load-out — climax home); Paper after (the fold callback, the salvage file, the morning).
- **Optional story points:** Daisy's under the Owl overflow (parallel route, and the vector that reaches Lilly if she sat on the radio); waking Sgt. Doyle; the rail spur and loading deck; running the magazine as an active clock rather than background hazard.
- **Likely cuts if time runs short:** skip or narrate Daisy's if she rolled straight out; fold the West End street into the approach; keep the magazine as a hazard the players smell rather than a timed second problem; resolve the morning in a single scene instead of a precinct round.
- **Expected climax window:** final 60–90 minutes, at the yard.
- **3–4 hour core:** Precinct, a compressed approach that merges West End with the fence line, the yard, and Paper after. That is four substantial situations; Daisy's, Doyle, and the magazine clock are the material that expands it to five hours.
- **Opening trouble in the first 10–15 minutes of play:** the tucked fold and the steer onto Main Street, with the West End call landing into a near-empty building while she is still deciding how much of her evening the errand deserves. Not a briefing and not a map.
- **Prepared climax situation:** the Cinder Creek yard under the stacks — four Extras (Colfax, Meeks, Runkle, Sloat), a loaded flatbed being made ready to move, a dark wet lot with stacks, mill office, scale house and rail spur for cover, the uncleared magazine inland as hazard and clock, and Len Pell somewhere in it on his own clock. Objectives beyond winning the firefight: stop the load, find Len alive, keep the powder off the truck, and keep the scene writable.
- **One reversal only:** this is neither kids nor a simple outage — it is an organized load-out on ground the chief made small, and there is old blasting powder in play. Where that powder is when she arrives runs on the clock, not on prep: still in the bunker if she moves fast, on the pickup on the inland track if she is slower, roped on the flatbed if she is slower still. Do not narrate it as already on the truck. No second twist.
- **No essential side quest.** Every optional situation must be skippable without blocking the main objective.
