## Ashgrove Precinct — the radio room after midnight

- **Role in adventure:** SP1's two phases and SP6's paper. One home for the night line, the duty board, the `PATROL — READ` basket, and the car keys.
- **Geography lock:** former town hall, north of Main / ORE. 328, Church–Pell, between Spruce and Main (Sheet 2). One floor, too many rooms, two cells, an old forensic lab nobody is qualified to use, and dead-paper rooms full of the mill era.
- **First impression:** Floor wax and cold coffee in a building built for three times the people in it. Most of the lights are off; the radio room's are not. The base set hums against a wall of 1958 cabinetry, a black telephone sits on the shelf beside it under a wire basket of patrol paper, and rain works at the windows of a lot of empty offices. Nobody else is here.
- **Current situation:** Nancy went home at five and the town line is switched through to the night line on the radio-room shelf. Earl Voss cleared West End at 23:10 and has said nothing since. Kevin Alder is in car 2 on 328 near Lowater. Doyle is at home; Whitley is at home. Lilly has paperwork, an errand on Main she does not want, and a telephone within reach. At 00:20 it rings.
- **Environment:** dry, warm, and safe. Base set and night line together on one shelf; car keys on the board (car 3, the day car, in the hall lot); duty board with this week's postings; dead-paper rooms down the hall holding the weekly assignment sheets back to January 1985; two cells; a typewriter and carbon sets.
- **People/forces present:** Lilly alone by midnight. Whitley until 18:25. On the telephone: Rita Holm at 00:20, and again at 00:41 if nobody picks up. Reachable by telephone: Doyle (about 40 minutes to the fence if she wakes him), Whitley at his house on Larch Street, Rook County in Rookton for a deputy (35–40 minutes) or an ambulance (30 minutes out).
- **What can be learned:** the outage, its caller, and its address; that Earl is not answering; that Kevin will; the folded complaint in the basket with `Ref. patrol — Voss` in pencil; the duty board's posting of Voss to West End and the mill; and, in the dead-paper rooms, a year of who was where on which night.
- **Spoken lines:** Whitley: "Kids, or it's Voss's. Either way it isn't detective work." Rita Holm: "It's not the fuses. I did the fuses. It's the whole row and the mill both."
- **Discoverable rolls:** see `plot.md`, SP1 Night Line and SP6 Paper After.
- **GM-only truth:** the fold is a habit, not a signal to anyone. Whitley refers the complaint to patrol because that is genuinely defensible and because it keeps the lot off his detective's desk. He does not telephone anybody tonight, before or after Lilly calls him.
- **What changes over time:** if the room is empty at 00:20 the phone simply rings. Rita tries again at 00:41 and then sends Gil Sorenson into town. Kevin reaches the mill camp on his own loop at about 01:20 instead of 00:35, which costs Len and costs the load.
- **If violence begins:** it does not. Nothing in this building is a threat tonight.
- **Relevant mechanics:** ordinary Trait tests. HR-NFA-002 Hitting the Books uses Research for the assignment-sheet reconstruction in SP6; a failure costs an hour, not the fact.

## Daisy's under the Owl overflow

- **Role in adventure:** SP2. The assigned errand, the parallel route, and the vector that reaches Lilly if she never touched the radio.
- **Geography lock:** south side of Main / ORE. 328, first numbered door east of Church, **three doors west of The Hoot Owl** past Iverson's Market and Pell's Hardware.
- **First impression:** Every booth is full and half the room is standing, coats over chair backs, the air thick with fryer smoke and wet wool. Thirty mill people who came out for beer and got a diner are making the best of it, loud and good-humoured, and the counter radio has already lost. Through the west windows Main Street is lit all the way to the fuel canopy, and past that there is nothing at all.
- **Current situation:** the Owl has been shut to walk-ins since about 18:30 for Oscar Lind's send-off supper, and it has been like this since half past seven. Daisy Pell is working the counter alone and has been half-watching her door for a man who has not come through it. Nobody has connected the crowded room to the dark row west of the creek.
- **Environment:** warm, loud, and entirely safe. One street door, a counter, booths, a kitchen window, a payphone. Lilly's portable reaches the base set from here. The Hoot Owl's door is three doors east and stays shut until about 01:15.
- **People/forces present:** Daisy Pell; thirty-odd displaced Owl regulars; her cook; Gil Sorenson from about 01:00 if the call has gone unworked; Pat Calhoun in the Owl doorway at 01:15. Marnie Quade is behind the Owl's bar all night, serving a supper.
- **What can be learned:** Len Pell is not here on a night he would be, and he will not carry the Rennick key into a bar; his truck went west at about half past nine; the mill-camp porch lights are out while the town is lit; Earl has not been in; Kevin came through for coffee at about 23:20.
- **Spoken lines:** Daisy: "Half that pub is sitting in my booths and Len isn't one of them." Daisy, if pressed: "He won't take that key into a bar. Never has. If he's not drinking he's out there." Gil Sorenson, at about 01:00: "Rita's been on the phone to you people twice. Whole row's black and there's a light up in the mill."
- **Discoverable rolls:** see `plot.md`, SP2 Owl Overflow.
- **GM-only truth:** Daisy's interest is family, not evidence, and she has no idea anything is being taken. No envelope passes in the Owl tonight; Marnie Quade is running a private supper and would not do it in front of thirty people. If Lilly walks into the Owl she finds a retirement dinner.
- **What changes over time:** **only if Kevin was dispatched and went in alone**, at about 00:52 a short string of flat pops carries across Cinder Creek and the west end of the room argues about whether it was a truck. If Lilly rolled with him, or if nobody ever raised him, there is nothing to hear at 00:52 and the room just stays loud — Gil Sorenson at about 01:00 is then a vector, not gunfire. At 01:15 the supper breaks up and the Owl empties onto Main either way.
- **If violence begins:** it does not begin here. The worst case is a drunk mill hand and a scene Daisy will remember.
- **Relevant mechanics:** ordinary Persuasion and Notice. HR-NFA-002 Patter if she wants Daisy aside from a full counter; a failure costs minutes in the middle of the night.
- **Links:** SP2; [`../world/locations/daisys.md`](../world/locations/daisys.md); [`../world/locations/the-hoot-owl.md`](../world/locations/the-hoot-owl.md); [`../world/npcs/daisy-pell.md`](../world/npcs/daisy-pell.md); [`../world/npcs/len-pell.md`](../world/npcs/len-pell.md).

## Mill Camp Road

- **Role in adventure:** SP3. Confirm the outage, meet the caller, and see working light inside a closed mill.
- **Geography lock:** West of the city-limit dash, across Cinder Creek, on the West End mill road that runs from ORE. 328 to the yard fence. A gravel loop of eight company houses, five still tenanted, with West End Middle beyond them. The 1984 sheets do not name the loop.
- **First impression:** Eight houses on wet gravel with not one light in them, and no wind and no downed wire to explain it — the rain stopped an hour ago and the row is just standing there. A woman is out on her step with a kerosene lamp and a coat over her nightclothes. Across the mill road the yard fence is a black edge, and well back behind it something hard and white is burning, with an engine under it.
- **Current situation:** the row has been dark since a little after 00:08. Rita Holm at No. 4 telephoned the precinct at 00:20 and will telephone again at 00:41. Gil Sorenson at No. 6 has been out to the road twice and has decided the light in the yard is a work light. Nobody on this road has gone to look, and nobody is going to.
- **Environment:** open gravel, no streetlight now, standing water, about 46°F and still. The mill road runs on west to the main gate; the rail-spur road branches north before it. The yard fence is fifty yards across the road. Her portable is patchy this far west; the car radio works. The power company answers out of Rookton and will not have a truck here before 2 a.m.
- **People/forces present:** Rita Holm; Gil Sorenson; three other tenanted houses with people in them who will stay in them. If the call went out, Kevin Alder reaches the mill camp and Rita at about 00:35, spends roughly six minutes checking Rita, the dark houses, and the shared outage before reaching the fence and Earl's nest at about 00:41. If it did not, he reaches the camp on his ordinary loop at about 01:20.
- **What can be learned:** the yard and the houses are one company-built feed; the outage began a little after ten past twelve; there is a fixed work light and a running diesel inside the fence; heavy dual-wheel tracks turn in at the spur road, tonight and before; Rita has heard loaded trucks going east three or four nights a month; Earl's car is not on the street he is posted to.
- **Spoken lines:** Rita Holm: "The mill and us are the same wire. Always have been. It goes, we go." Rita Holm: "Three, four nights a month. Late. Going east, loaded. That's what I called about Friday." Gil Sorenson: "That's not a flashlight. That's a work light."
- **Discoverable rolls:** see `plot.md`, SP3 Mill Camp Road.
- **GM-only truth:** Sloat pulled the pole cutout at the yard fence at 00:08 so Ned could cut into the motor-room bus bar without it being live; nobody intended to black out the houses and nobody has thought about them since. Rita's Friday complaint is the sheet in the precinct basket.
- **What changes over time:** nothing on this road. What advances is eighty yards away behind the fence: copper is aboard, the deck plate is going on, and the inland track has not been driven yet.
- **If violence begins:** it does not begin here, and the crew will not come out to the road. If shooting starts inside the fence, the tenants stay indoors and one of them telephones again.
- **Relevant mechanics:** ordinary Notice and Persuasion. No hazard on this ground.
- **Links:** SP3, SP4; [`../world/locations/cinder-creek-lumber.md`](../world/locations/cinder-creek-lumber.md); [`../world/npcs/kevin-alder.md`](../world/npcs/kevin-alder.md).

## The yard fence, the gate, and Earl's nest

- **Role in adventure:** SP4. The reversal, the last safe ground, and the two things that say the watchman is inside: his truck and an empty nail.
- **Geography lock:** West End side of the Cinder Creek yard. Main gate on the mill road; mill office ten yards inside it; empty scale house twenty yards inside the fence with its bay open; sixty yards of bare gravel apron between the gate and the first stacks.
- **First impression:** The gate chain hangs loose and the mill office door is standing open the way a door stands open when its latch gave up years ago. A primer-gray pickup is nosed in beside it. Twenty yards on, a police cruiser sits in the open bay of the scale house with its windows fogged from the inside and a radio muttering under the glass. Past it, sixty yards of wet gravel with nothing on it, and then the stacks.
- **Current situation:** Len opened the main gate at 21:30 and never closed it. Earl Voss has been asleep in car 1 since about 23:50 and will sleep until roughly 5 a.m. or until something wakes him. The crew came in at the spur gate at 23:25, after his 23:10 "West End clear," and is eighty yards away at the loading deck behind a running generator with the stacks between them and the scale house; they are not watching this gate. They know the cruiser is there, they have worked around it for months, and they keep their noise on the spur side.
- **Environment:** wet gravel, no yard lighting since 00:08, about 46°F, low overcast. Cover at the gate: the office wall, Len's truck, the scale-house structure, the fence line itself. **No cover at all** on the sixty-yard apron. The mill-office door does not latch and never has — it is a door that swings, not a puzzle. The nail behind it is empty.
- **People/forces present:** Earl Voss asleep in car 1; Kevin Alder at the fence at about 00:41 if called, after reaching Rita and the mill-camp houses at about 00:35 and spending six minutes checking the outage on the row; the crew visible at range under their work light.
- **What can be learned:** four men, a flatbed backed to the loading deck, a pickup at the spur gate, a hand winch, a 500-watt lamp on the deck rail; Len's F-100 inside the gate; the empty key nail and a fresh boot track in the office; Len's thermos still warm; Earl's memory that Len's truck came in before he shut his eyes; that the spur-gate chain was cut and rehung with somebody else's padlock.
- **Spoken lines:** Kevin: "Camp's black and there's a light in the yard. Earl's not answering and I've called him twice." Earl, woken: "Len's truck's been sitting there since before I shut my eyes." Earl, pushed toward the gate: "Call Kevin. This is Kevin's."
- **Discoverable rolls:** see `plot.md`, SP4 Fence Line and Earl's Nest.
- **GM-only truth:** Earl is asleep, not bought. He has no envelope, no arrangement, and nothing to confess, and he does not know his own postings are worth anything to anyone. The scale-house bay is his spot whenever the sheet gives him West End and the mill — which on Wednesdays and Saturdays it always does — not a place he sits every night of the week. Treat the obvious conclusion, that the sleeping cop is on the take, as a fair and wrong read; see `secrets.md`, NHE-05.
- **What changes over time:** the 00:35 arrival is at Rita and the mill-camp houses; the fence arrival is about 00:41. **Only if Kevin was dispatched and came alone**, he crosses the apron at about 00:52 and is fired on; the shots wake Earl. With two of them on the fence, Meeks starts thinking about leaving instead of shooting, and the truck can go before a round is fired. If nobody raised him, the fence stays quiet until Lilly walks up to it or Kevin arrives on his own loop at about 01:20. Whenever contact happens, Meeks's go-clock starts then — see the night clock in `plot.md`.
- **If violence begins:** the crew fires from the deck and the stacks to drive people off the apron and to cover the flatbed; they do not advance on the gate. Earl wakes, stays in or behind car 1, and is a witness and a radio, not a gun. Len's truck, the office wall, and the scale house are the only cover on this side.
- **Relevant mechanics:** darkness applies from here in — Illumination, SWADE Fifth Printing (2023) p.102, via [`../../../sources/systems/SWADE/reference/combat.md`](../../../sources/systems/SWADE/reference/combat.md). Cover values, crew statistics, and the full illumination handling are owned by `encounters.md`; do not improvise them here.
- **Links:** SP4, SP5; [`../world/npcs/earl-voss.md`](../world/npcs/earl-voss.md); [`../world/npcs/len-pell.md`](../world/npcs/len-pell.md); `encounters.md`.

## The yard under the stacks — the load-out

- **Role in adventure:** SP5. Climax ground: opposition, darkness, a truck on a clock, and a man behind a door.
- **Geography lock:** stacks and drying sheds fill the middle of the yard; the dry kilns stand on the north side of the shed row; the rail spur runs in past the stacks to the old loading deck with room to back a flatbed against it; the mill pond lies south of the stacks; the inland track leaves from the yard's back corner.
- **First impression:** One clamp lamp on the loading-deck rail cuts a hard white cone about twenty-five yards across and makes everything outside it blacker than it was. Inside the cone men are working a hand winch onto a flatbed, unhurried, talking in short words, and a generator is running on the bed of a pickup by the spur gate. Everywhere else there are stacks, wet rope, standing water between the sheds, and a long blind row of shed doors.
- **Current situation:** copper bus bar and cable and four bundles of deck plate are roped aboard. Runkle and Sloat are on the winch; Meeks is on the deck watching the load and the time; Ned Colfax is working badly and keeps looking back at the shed row. Len is behind the dropped bar on dry kiln No. 2, out of the light. Where the six cases are depends on the clock in `plot.md` — bunker, pickup bed, or under the tarp.
- **Environment:**

| Feature | State tonight |
|---|---|
| Loading deck and rail spur | Flatbed backed against the deck; spur road runs out to the mill road and onto 328 eastbound in under a minute |
| Spur gate | Chain cut months ago and rehung with a padlock that is not the Rennicks'; Meeks carries the key |
| Work light | 500-watt clamp lamp on the deck rail; a 25-yard cone and hard shadow outside it. It ran off the yard's own service until 00:08 and off the pickup's generator after that |
| Stacks and drying sheds | The middle of the yard; cover, blind corners, wet ground between them |
| Dry kilns | North side of the shed row; kiln No. 2 has its bar dropped and Len behind it; blood and a 24-inch pry bar on the apron |
| Mill office and gate | Sixty yards back across open gravel; door that does not latch; empty nail; Len's thermos |
| Scale house | Twenty yards inside the fence; Earl and car 1 |
| Mill pond | South of the stacks; dark, deep at the old log dump, nothing anyone should back into |
| Inland track | Leaves the back corner toward the magazine, about 400 yards, rutted and soft after rain |

- **People/forces present:** Roy Meeks, Ned Colfax, Dale Runkle, Jimmy Sloat — four Extras. Len Pell in kiln No. 2. Kevin Alder on scene or arriving. Earl Voss at the scale house once he is awake.
- **What can be learned:** the load is professional and months old, not one night's scavenging; the spur chain and the older tire ridge date it; there is old blasting stock and it is moving; Ned will give the shape of the job and, once, the unsigned line about Wednesdays and Saturdays; Len can say four men and a work light and that the one who swung was a mill hand.
- **Spoken lines:** Meeks, to his own men: "Rope it. We're going." Sloat, into the dark before he fires: "Stay off the deck!" Ned Colfax, cornered: "He came round the sheds. He wasn't supposed to be here. Nobody's supposed to be here." Len, if he is still conscious: "Whoever that is — the bar's on the outside."
- **Discoverable rolls:** see `plot.md`, SP5 The Load-Out.
- **GM-only truth:** Ned swung the pry bar at 00:02 in a panic and has been useless since. Meeks pays a local go-between for the quiet and has never met a police officer about it; he believes a day of the week is a guarantee and is finding out tonight that it is not. None of these four can name Chief Whitley, because none of them has ever been told.
- **What changes over time:** undisturbed, Meeks wants wheels rolling at 01:45. From first police contact of any kind he needs fifteen to twenty minutes to rope, kill the light, and pull out, so the departure hour follows the contact hour rather than a fixed number — 01:10 is only what that produces when contact lands at 00:52. Runkle moves the powder in three two-case trips across roughly 00:45–01:10: all six begin in the bunker, each trip puts two in transit, and all six are on the flatbed only after the third trip. Len's clock runs the whole time. Full go-clock in `plot.md`.
- **If violence begins:** the crew shoots to break contact and to cover the truck, then leaves. Meeks gives up copper before the truck and the truck before a man. Runkle and Sloat run rather than hold ground. Ned may drop everything and put his hands out. Collateral risk: Len behind a steel door in the line of nothing, Earl in a car, Kevin on open gravel, and six cases of degraded stock somewhere on the map.

## The magazine and the inland track

- **Role in adventure:** the clock inside SP5, and an optional second problem if the table has the time for it.
- **Geography lock:** about 400 yards inland from the yard's back corner, up a rutted track through second growth. A low concrete bunker set deliberately away from the buildings, left from the years the company cut its own road. On no current town, PD, or insurance paper.
- **First impression:** The track leaves the gravel and turns into two ruts full of water under second growth that closes over the top of it. At the end there is a low concrete box with a steel door, a cut hasp hanging off it, and moss to the roofline. Inside, the smell is sweet and oily and wrong, and it is colder than the night outside.
- **Current situation:** Ned walked Meeks up here at 23:50 and cut the hasp. Six wooden cases of ammonia stumping powder left from 1978, gone greasy where they have sweated in a bunker nobody has aired, plus a taped tin of blasting caps and a coil of leg wire. Until about 00:45 all six cases are still here. Between about 00:45 and 01:10 Dale Runkle makes three pickup trips, two cases per trip; during each trip two cases are in transit, later loads remain in the bunker, and earlier loads wait at the flatbed. After about 01:10 the bunker is empty and all six are on the flatbed.
- **Environment:** soft ruts after rain, a pickup that can bog, no light except what somebody brings, second growth close on both sides, and 400 yards between here and anyone who could help. A vehicle on this track can be heard from the deck and cannot be seen from it.
- **People/forces present:** nobody until Runkle drives up. Meeks stays at the loading deck while Runkle makes all three trips.
- **What can be learned:** the cut hasp and fresh boot traffic; stencils and dates on the cases; the caps stored with the powder, which is the part that would make a road man wince; drag marks and tyre ruts that say where the load is going.
- **Spoken lines:** Runkle, backing the pickup, to nobody: "Slow. Slow. Slow."
- **Discoverable rolls:** see `plot.md`, SP5 The Load-Out, `Notice` on the flatbed's load and `Common Knowledge` on the cases.
- **GM-only truth:** the magazine is ordinary industrial neglect. Nobody is guarding it, nobody sold it, no owner has acted on it, and **Chief Whitley does not know it is there** — the quiet he is paid for covers salvage. Meeks learned about it tonight and intends to sell it in Rookton.
- **What changes over time:** empty bunker by about 01:10 unless somebody interferes. Interference can be as simple as standing on the track; Runkle will not drive at a badge.
- **If violence begins:** this is the worst ground in the adventure to fire a weapon on, and the fiction should say so before anyone does — the smell, the sweating cases, the caps in the same room. It is a hazard and a clock, **never** a scripted detonation.
- **Relevant mechanics:** `encounters.md` owns every value here, including whether any explosive effect is resolvable under active authority. Do not write a homebrew blast result in this file, in `plot.md`, or at the table without the ruling `encounters.md` records.
- **Links:** SP5; `secrets.md` NHE-02; [`../world/locations/cinder-creek-lumber.md`](../world/locations/cinder-creek-lumber.md).

## The morning desk

- **Role in adventure:** SP6. Where a real case gets signed and a folded sheet is still sitting where it was left.
- **First impression:** Grey light through the windows, the smell of the first pot of coffee, and the day shift arriving to a story that is already ahead of them. Her own handwriting from 00:20 is at the top of the radio log. On the shelf beside the phone the patrol basket has not been touched since yesterday evening, and the sheet on top of it is folded in half with one crease down it.
- **Current situation:** whatever came back from the yard becomes ordinary paper — men in the two cells, a flatbed nobody can move yet, a hospital in Rookton, a county deputy writing his own version. Whitley arrives about seven, hears it from Doyle first, and backs her in front of everybody. He signs the salvage close, signs the overtime, and does not try to make any of it smaller.
- **Environment:** the same building as the radio room, the cells, the dead-paper rooms with a year of assignment sheets, and Nancy's carbon files. Rookton owns the forensics, the hospital, and the district attorney.
- **People/forces present:** Whitley, Doyle, Nancy Iverson, Kevin Alder if he is not in a hospital, the day shift, a Rook County deputy, and Daisy Pell by telephone about her brother-in-law.
- **What can be learned:** the fold and its heading; Nancy's carbon if the original walks; the Wednesday and Saturday postings back to January; Kevin's eleven trucks and what days of the week they were. None of it is evidence, and the file she can actually sign does not contain any of it.
- **Spoken lines:** Whitley, in front of the day shift if all four men were taken: "My detective took four men off that lot with one patrolman and no help from anybody. Write that down somewhere it'll get read." If fewer than four were taken: "My detective worked that lot with what this town gave her. Put what she brought home at the top of the report." Whitley, at his desk: "Sign it and I'll sign it. Overtime too." Doyle: "You had the room, you had the phone, and you rolled. That's the whole report."
- **Discoverable rolls:** see `plot.md`, SP6 Paper After.
- **GM-only truth:** he is not performing. He is genuinely pleased, genuinely finished with the arrangement, and genuinely never going to be touched by tonight. Do not let him confess, slip a name, or behave like a man who knows he has been read. His behaviour here is also the third unmissable piece of the suspicion floor in `plot.md`: controlled dawn praise, in public, for work on a lot he personally steered her away from twelve hours earlier. That lands whether or not she ever picks up a single vector and whether or not a round was fired, and it is still not proof of anything.
- **What changes over time:** the original complaint is out of the basket by Monday. Nancy's carbon stays in the Friday day file. The assignment sheets stay in the dead-paper rooms indefinitely.
- **If violence begins:** it does not.
- **Relevant mechanics:** ordinary Persuasion and Research; HR-NFA-002 Hitting the Books for the assignment sheets.
