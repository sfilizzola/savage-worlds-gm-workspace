# Secrets, Revelations, and Twists

GM-only. Every truth below is predetermined preparation for this night, not session history and not
campaign canon until play establishes it. Items tagged **NOT FILEABLE TONIGHT** can be reached,
believed, and written in a notebook, and still cannot become evidence before night 4
(`ashgrove-puzzle-1986`).

## Secret NHE-01 - The arrangement

- **Truth:** Chief Whitley is paid cash to keep the frozen Cinder Creek lot unpublished. What he sells is the assignment sheet, which he writes himself: on Len Pell's drinking nights — Wednesday and Saturday — Earl Voss draws West End and the mill, Kevin Alder is posted east on 328 or the lake road, and whatever Lilly draws is small, in town, or out at the lake. He steers; he never warns anyone in the lot, and he has never met a scrap hauler. The cash reaches him at a remove, in an envelope passed by **Marnie Quade** behind the bar at The Hoot Owl. He believes he is being paid to look away from **salvage**.
- **Who knows:** Whitley. Marnie Quade knows she passes envelopes and does not know what for. Roy Meeks knows he pays a local for quiet and does not know who ultimately takes it. Nobody else in Ashgrove knows any of it.
- **Why it matters:** it is the night's real discovery and the arc's hinge. Lilly ends **certain and empty-handed** in every branch. The four vectors decide how much she could put in a notebook; they are not required for the certainty itself. That gap is the whole point of night 3.
- **Player-facing signs:** the fold on Friday's mill-noise complaint and the pencilled `Ref. patrol — Voss`; the weekly assignment sheets in the dead-paper rooms; Ned Colfax's unsigned line; Kevin Alder's eleven flatbeds, all on Wednesdays or Saturdays; the chief asking after Voss in the morning before he has been told.
- **Independent discovery vectors:**
  1. The folded complaint in the `PATROL — READ` basket, or Nancy Iverson's carbon in the Friday day file, read against the fold she watched him make at 18:10.
  2. The weekly assignment sheets back to 1 January 1986: Earl on West End and the mill for 37 of the 39 Wednesday and Saturday nights, against Kevin drawing West End 17 times on other weeknights and never once on a Wednesday or a Saturday.
  3. Ned Colfax's line about the lot being clear on Wednesdays and Saturdays, set beside Kevin's count and the days those eleven trucks ran.
- **If never discovered:** the suspicion floor still lands tonight and is not deferred — she closes an ordinary salvage case and keeps a chief who steered her off that exact lot at ten past six, whose lot then turned out to be a months-long load-out, and who shook her hand over it at seven in the morning. She is certain he is protecting that lot. She cannot prove it. The four vectors are all still in the building and all still reachable in night 4, which is where they can become something.
- **If discovered early:** nothing tactical changes. She cannot act on it tonight, she will not accuse a chief on a fold, and pushing it in the morning gets her praise and a signature and no ground.
- **NOT FILEABLE TONIGHT.** No ledger, no signed statement, no cash in hand, no confession, no chain-of-command packet. Marnie Quade passes nothing tonight — she is running a private supper in front of thirty people. The four vectors are a habit, a defensible roster, an unsigned sentence, and traffic on a state highway.
- **False assumptions/red herrings:** the sleeping patrolman looks bought (see NHE-05); the Rennick split-title quarrel looks like a motive for stripping the lot (it is not — the Rookton haulers have no connection to the Rookton-side title holder, and the Rennicks stay off-screen).

## Secret NHE-02 - The powder in the bunker

- **Truth:** about 400 yards inland of the stacks, up a rutted track, a low concrete magazine holds six wooden cases of ammonia stumping powder left from 1978, a taped tin of blasting caps, and a coil of leg wire. It has never been aired or cleared. The stock has sweated and is dangerous to handle, drop, or drive. Ned Colfax told Meeks about it tonight; Meeks has a Rookton buyer who takes things without conversation.
- **Who knows:** men who worked the floor in the road years, including Ned. Meeks as of 23:50 tonight. Runkle and Sloat as of about 00:30. Len knows the bunker exists and assumes it was cleared. **Chief Whitley does not know it is there**, and nobody has ever told him a truck off that lot carried powder.
- **Why it matters:** it is the night's hazard and its clock, and it keeps the chief's guilt proportionate — he sold quiet over scrap, not over explosives on a state highway.
- **Player-facing signs:** the cut hasp; fresh boot traffic and wheel ruts on a track nobody uses; stencilled wooden cases on a pickup bed or under the flatbed tarp; the smell in the bunker; Ned volunteering it the moment he thinks somebody is going to drive it.
- **Independent discovery vectors:**
  1. Walk or see the inland track from the yard's back corner — cut hasp, drag marks, fresh ruts.
  2. Read the flatbed's load, or see the pickup come back down the track.
  3. Ned Colfax, who will say it unprompted; Len Pell, if he is conscious, who can say what used to be up there.
- **If never discovered:** the cases leave on the flatbed. That is the failure state the adventure is allowed to have: powder east on ORE. 328 in a truck with no plate light.
- **If discovered early:** she can secure it by doing nothing more than standing on the track, which is a legitimate and quiet way to spend the climax — and costs her the deck.
- **Where it is, by the clock:** all six in the bunker until about 00:45; then three pickup trips at two cases per trip, with the stock split between bunker, pickup in transit, and flatbed as the trips advance; all six roped under the tarp after about 01:10. **Do not assert it is already on the flatbed before that.** A fast arrival finds all six in the bunker; a delay finds one two-case load in transit while earlier loads wait at the flatbed. See the night clock in `plot.md`.

## Secret NHE-03 - What happened to Len Pell

- **Truth:** at 00:02 Len came around the drying sheds into the work light. Ned Colfax panicked and swung a 24-inch pry bar, catching him above the left ear. Len went down on the concrete apron. The crew dragged him into dry kiln No. 2 and dropped the outside bar so he would stop being their problem while they loaded. Nobody has looked at him since. They are thieves and they did not mean to kill anybody; whether they have depends on the clock.
- **Who knows:** all four of the crew. Ned is the one it is eating.
- **Why it matters:** it is the human objective under the firefight, and it is what makes elapsed time cost something real.
- **Player-facing signs:** his primer-gray F-100 inside the gate; the empty nail behind the mill-office door; a thermos still warm at 00:45; blood on the kiln apron; a pry bar thrown down across a latch; banging from behind a steel door while he is still conscious; Ned's inability to stop looking at the shed row.
- **Independent discovery vectors:**
  1. Sound — inside about thirty yards of the shed row, while Len is conscious, no roll is required.
  2. Physical trail — blood, the dropped bar, and the barred door of kiln No. 2.
  3. People — Daisy on his routine and the key, Earl on his truck arriving before 23:10, Ned once he is cornered, or Kevin searching the sheds while Lilly holds the deck.
- **If never discovered:** he is found by Kevin, by Earl at 5 a.m., or in the morning by whoever comes for the truck. The later it is, the worse it is; a death here is a consequence of the clock, not a prepared outcome.
- **If discovered early:** he is a pair of hands, a witness who can describe four men and a work light, and a reason to call Rookton before anybody fires a shot.
- **What he cannot give:** a name. He never saw who swung, only that the man moved like a mill hand.

## Secret NHE-04 - Meeks pays for the day of the week

- **Truth:** Roy Meeks has been running the Cinder Creek lot in stages since November 1985. He hired Ned Colfax for what a laid-off millwright knows about which buildings lock, where the copper runs, and what the company left behind. He pays a local go-between for the quiet and was told the rule in one sentence: Wednesdays and Saturdays, nobody walks it. He has never met a police officer about this, does not know the chief's name is attached to it, and believes a day of the week is a guarantee.
- **Who knows:** Meeks. Ned heard the rule from Meeks at hire and never asked who guaranteed it. Runkle and Sloat know only that the lot is safe midweek and weekends.
- **Why it matters:** it is the only bridge between the crime and the chief, and it is deliberately a sentence rather than a witness.
- **Player-facing signs:** the spur-gate chain cut and rehung with a padlock that is not the Rennicks'; a heavy dual-wheel track laid over an older ridge on the mill road; a load too professional for one night; eleven flatbeds on Wednesdays and Saturdays; Ned's unsigned line.
- **Independent discovery vectors:**
  1. Ned Colfax, cornered — the line comes on a success, once, unsigned; a raise only adds when and how he was told. A failed roll delays it to a cell tomorrow rather than killing it.
  2. The physical age of the operation — the chain, the tyre ridge, the stripped motor room, Rita Holm's three-or-four-nights-a-month.
  3. Kevin Alder's count, once somebody asks him what day of the week those trucks ran.
- **If never discovered:** the salvage case still closes on what is on the ground tonight.
- **If discovered early:** it sharpens the certainty and changes nothing she can file.
- **NOT FILEABLE TONIGHT.** Meeks will not repeat the arrangement to a police officer, to a deputy, or to a district attorney; he wants a scrap charge, not a conspiracy. Ned will talk about the job all day and will stop talking the moment he is asked who said the lot was clear, and he will not sign.

## Secret NHE-05 - Earl Voss is a hole, not a conspirator

- **Truth:** on the nights the assignment sheet gives him West End and the mill, Earl backs car 1 into the scale-house bay and sleeps — tonight from about 23:50 to roughly 5 a.m. It is his spot on those nights, not a place he sits every night of the week. He has no envelope, no arrangement, and nothing to confess. He does not know his own postings are useful to anybody and would be insulted by the suggestion. He is the reason the arrangement works and he is not part of it.
- **Who knows:** the whole town knows he sleeps. Nobody knows what it is worth, because nobody except Whitley knows it is worth anything.
- **Why it matters:** it is the fair wrong answer. A detective who finds a sleeping cop twenty yards from an organized theft will reach for the obvious conclusion, and the obvious conclusion is false. Letting her be wrong here, and then correcting it with Earl himself, is part of how the night teaches her what the real shape is.
- **Player-facing signs:** a fogged cruiser in the same bay it has occupied for two years; "West End clear" at 23:10 and nothing since; a man who wakes frightened, gives up what little he saw immediately, and wants it to be Kevin's problem.
- **Independent discovery vectors:**
  1. Earl himself under any pressure — he has nothing to protect and no story to keep straight.
  2. The assignment sheets: he draws the easy half on Wednesdays and Saturdays and takes it gladly; he does not choose it and could not have.
  3. Meeks and Ned, who have never paid a patrolman and have no reason to protect one.
- **If never discovered:** she carries a wrong suspicion into night 4 and it costs her time there. That is an acceptable consequence, not a failure.
- **If discovered early:** the search for the bought man narrows to the man who writes the sheet, which is exactly where the night wants her.
- **False assumptions/red herrings:** keep the wrong read fair and distinguishable — Earl's habit is public, unpaid, and unchanged for years, and there is no money, no meeting, and no behaviour anywhere tonight that supports it.

## Secret NHE-06 - Kevin Alder's eleven trucks

- **Truth:** since February, Kevin has passed eleven heavy flatbeds on ORE. 328 — tarped, riding low, no load light, running east toward Rookton between midnight and two. He knows the count the way he knows mile markers and has written none of it down, because a truck on a state highway is not a crime. The first was Wednesday 5 February and the most recent was Saturday 3 May. Every one of them fell on a Wednesday or a Saturday, which he has never noticed, because nobody has ever asked him what day it was.
- **Who knows:** Kevin. He does not think of it as a secret; he thinks of it as nothing he could file.
- **Why it matters:** it is the fourth graft vector and the night's quiet rhyme — a cop with a pattern and no paper, standing next to a detective who is about to have the same problem.
- **Player-facing signs:** he produces the number immediately if a cop asks him a real question, with dates he is fairly sure of.
- **Independent discovery vectors:**
  1. Ask him about the highway at the fence, in the car, or afterwards.
  2. Rita Holm hears the same trucks from No. 4 Mill Camp Road and puts them at three or four nights a month, late, going east.
  3. The physical record on the mill road — a fresh dual-wheel track over an older ridge — dates the traffic without either of them.
- **If never discovered:** the other three graft vectors still stand on their own.
- **If discovered early:** it makes the fence line worse for him, because he will have worked out on the drive over what he has been watching all spring.
- **NOT FILEABLE TONIGHT.** Eleven remembered trucks with approximate dates and no plates, no photographs, and no reports is a patrolman's memory, not a case.

## GM-only boundary

Nothing in this night is supernatural, and nothing in this night's paper has been altered. There is
no synchronization gap, no missing interval, no nausea, no ClearWave, no consortium, and no
Concordance operation anywhere in it. Whitley is ordinary small-town graft and knows nothing of any
of it. This is deliberate: the tell in this night has to mean Whitley and only Whitley, and a machine
touching paper on the same night would poison it.

Night 3 does not take Whitley off the force. Evidence, channels, and the unfair walk belong to night
4 (`ashgrove-puzzle-1986`). Do not let any end state here produce a name above Roy Meeks.
