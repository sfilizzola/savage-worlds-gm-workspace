# Take the Baton

Complete every front-matter field before mechanical preparation.

## One-sentence premise

Twenty-five years after their state championship, four former teammates return to Colston for the anniversary of that win and must save one of their own — this year's anchor runner — from the same fate that quietly took their fifth teammate the night they became legends.

## GM intent

This is folk horror, not a monster hunt. Nobody in Colston is a villain by their own lights: the booster who's a little too kind to tonight's runner, the retired coach who's carried a secret for a generation, Jesse's own parents — they all believe they are honoring something, not feeding it. The horror is that the PCs already benefited from this once and never asked questions; tonight, the bill comes due for someone they love.

Identity erosion should read as loss of self through *belonging*, not violence: a look that lasts a beat too long, a phrase repeated exactly the way an old photo's caption reads, a kindness that leaves the recipient calmer and less like themselves. Keep Jesse's presence uncanny rather than monstrous — he is grief made visible, not a jump scare. Avoid cultist imagery entirely: no robes, no chanting circles, no altar. The town's ritual is a pep rally, a retired jersey, a story everyone already knows how to tell.

Play fair with the players: give at least two independent ways to notice the pattern and at least two independent ways to intervene at the climax. No single missed Notice roll should be able to end the night badly. Do not let anyone speak plot information they could not know — Jesse's parents believe he drowned; the retired coach suspects more than he says; only the entity (never a speaker) and Jesse himself (barely) hold the full shape of it.

## Player-facing mission

The PCs understand they are in Colston for one reason: it's the 25th anniversary of the town's only other state championship team — their team — and the school has invited them back to be honored at tonight's state meet, where Colston is fielding a serious contender in the 4x400 relay again for the first time in a generation. They think tonight is about nostalgia, a plaque, maybe a few beers with people they used to know. They do not yet know why one of them can't stop looking at the boy standing very still at the far end of the infield.

## Predetermined truths

Facts that are true regardless of player action. Distinct from planned events.

- In 1958, the Colston No. 4 shaft collapsed, trapping and killing eleven miners. The mine never reopened; the town nearly died with it.
- In 1962, the town built its high school athletic field directly over the sealed shaft entrance, as both memorial and civic rebuilding project. Nobody now living remembers deciding to build it exactly there; the town simply did, and prospered afterward in ways it never fully explained to itself.
- Something under that field answers Colston's collective wanting when it is concentrated hard enough, in one place, on one person — which happens exactly once in a great while, when the town's team is good enough to win a state championship. It always takes payment through whoever ran anchor leg, weeks after the win, during a solitary early-morning training run, near the flooded quarry the collapse created a half-mile from the field (locally: Miller's Hole). No body has ever been recovered.
- This has happened three times: 1966 (Wesley Corbin), 1983 (Denise Kowalski), and 2001 — the PCs' own team, whose anchor was Jesse Calloway. Nobody in Colston has ever laid these three disappearances side by side; the gaps are long enough, and the town does not want to.
- Jesse did not drown. He was hollowed out and taken in some sense the entity uses, not killed outright. He has lingered near the field and the quarry ever since — present, physically real, silent or nearly so, and very difficult for most people to consciously notice or remember noticing. The PCs, who loved him, are among the only people who still can.
- Jesse's parents, the retired 2001 coach (Hal Prewitt), and most of the town genuinely believe Jesse drowned. They are not lying; they were never shown otherwise, and something about the town makes the true shape of events hard to hold onto.
- Tonight, Colston's team is good again for the first time in 25 years, and Toby Sharpe's daughter Nora has just been moved to anchor for the state final. The entity's attention is already on her.
- Cell service in the valley bowl around the field has always been poor; tonight it is worse. The only road out is closed for the meet's parking and the town's anniversary parade. None of this was arranged by anyone — it does not need to be.
- The entity itself has no name, no voice, and is never a speaker. It is not evil in a way that can be reasoned with or bargained against; it is not sadistic either. It answers wanting. It does not initiate.

## Objectives and stakes

### Main objective

Get Nora Sharpe through tonight without losing her — to the quarry, to the hollowing, or to the town's help.

### Secondary objectives

- Learn enough of the truth about Jesse, and about 1966 and 1983, to actually understand what is happening in time to act.
- Decide what becomes of Jesse, if he can be reached at all.

### Stakes

- If the PCs act quickly: they can pull Nora away from the ritual's specific moments (the pep rally naming, the retired-jersey ceremony, the anchor leg itself) before each one deepens its hold.
- If the PCs delay: Nora grows calmer, more distant, more "ready" by the hour; by the final race she may not want to be saved.
- If the PCs fail: Nora disappears near the quarry in the following weeks, exactly like Jesse, Wesley, and Denise. Colston mourns her sincerely and remembers nothing true. The pattern continues, unbroken, for another generation.

## Starting state

Table start is the afternoon of the anniversary, hours before the evening's state meet: a school assembly and reunion lunch where the 2001 team is publicly honored, the current team (including Nora) is introduced, and the whole town's attention briefly, uncomfortably converges on the PCs and on Nora at once. Somewhere during this, at least one PC sees Jesse — standing where he shouldn't be, dressed for a race that ended 25 years ago, and no one else so much as glances at him.
