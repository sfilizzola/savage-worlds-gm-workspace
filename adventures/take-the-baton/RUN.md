## Table terms

Define once; used throughout.

- **The toll / the mark:** shorthand for what the entity under Colston's field takes from whoever runs anchor leg at a state-championship win, sealed at the moment of peak crowd attention.
- **Hollowed:** what happened to Jesse (and, before him, Wesley Corbin and Denise Kowalski) instead of dying — present, but emptied and very hard for most people to notice.
- **The Winner's Curse:** the town's own half-joking name for the pattern, when it comes up at all.
- **Anchor leg:** the fourth and final leg of a 4x400m relay — the runner who takes the baton last and crosses the finish line. If unfamiliar with track terminology at the table, this is the one line the GM may need to explain up front.

## Prologue — read or paraphrase before First 15 minutes (one-off for this adventure)

**GM: this is a narrated cold open, not a scene the players act in. Read or paraphrase it in your own voice before anything else. It sets up the night; it is not itself the opening trouble.**

*Before the story begins, read this aloud, in your own voice, as a short story — not a briefing:*

The week before the state final, Colston practiced in the evenings, when the light went orange and the gnats came up off the infield grass. Marcus Webb was always first out of the blocks and last to admit his hamstring was tight. Renee Ortiz kept a folded index card of split times in her sock because she didn't trust herself to remember numbers under pressure, only to forget she was carrying them. Toby Sharpe organized the team dinners nobody asked him to organize, at the same table at Daisy's every Thursday, because he liked knowing where everyone would be. Dee Whitfield sat cross-legged on the infield with a stopwatch and a spiral notebook, timing splits nobody had asked her to time either, already the kind of person who wanted the whole story written down somewhere.

And Jesse Calloway ran anchor, because from the first meet of the season the crowd had started chanting his name before he even had the baton in his hand, and nobody — least of all Jesse — could explain why that kept happening to him and not to someone else.

The night of the final, the Colston stands were fuller and louder than they had any right to be for a town this size. Marcus took the gun and the first two hundred meters clean, no hesitation, the tight hamstring forgotten. He passed to Renee already ahead, and Renee ran her leg with her fists closed so tight her nails left marks, because losing the card in her sock didn't matter anymore, she just had to hold the lead. She passed to Toby, and Toby — who never once, all season, admitted how much this mattered to him — put his whole chest into the tape and handed off to Jesse still in first, by less than a stride.

Then it was just noise. The chant had started somewhere in the second lap and by the time Jesse had the baton it wasn't really his name anymore, just a single sound the whole crowd was making at once, and he ran the last hundred meters like something was pulling him down the lane rather than legs doing it. He crossed the line first. Colston won states for only the second time anyone could remember. For a few seconds, before Toby got to him first and lifted him half off his feet, Jesse just stood at the finish line breathing hard and grinning like he couldn't quite believe the sound was for him. It was, all five of them would have said without hesitating, the best night of their lives — and for twenty-five years, none of them have let themselves call it anything else.

It wasn't that night that it happened. It was five weeks later, on an ordinary Tuesday in April. Jesse had always gone out alone before sunrise to run the loop past the old mine road — "gotta get faster for next year," he told anyone who asked — and that morning he went out and didn't come back. A jogger found one shoe at the waterline of Miller's Hole, the flooded quarry out past the tree line, laces still tied. The search ran four days before the town quietly stopped calling it a search. There was a service, and a stone, and no body under it. Marcus, Renee, Toby, and Dee were eighteen years old, and they had never in their lives been that sad about anything, and not one of them has been able to talk about that spring since without finding a reason to talk about something else instead.

**Twenty-five years later...**

## Opening mood (GM throughline)

Tonight is Colston's 25th-anniversary honoring of that team, held the same day as the state track final the current squad has actually qualified for — the first time since 2001 that's happened. It should feel warm, a little overwhelming, entirely ordinary right up until it isn't. Nora Sharpe, Toby's daughter, has just been named anchor. Jesse is here. Almost nobody else can see him. Do not speak this paragraph aloud; it's for you.

## First 15 minutes

- **Handed to the players:** their pregens (`characters/`), each already carrying a distinct, specific bond to Jesse Calloway.
- **Where they are:** Colston High School gym, mid-assembly, the 2001 team publicly honored alongside this year's state-qualified squad.
- **Trouble already in motion:** Coach Arnholt names Nora Sharpe anchor for tonight's final, to a big cheer — and at some point in the room, whoever looks will see Jesse Calloway standing very still near the trophy alcove, in his old uniform, watching Nora. No one else reacts to him at all.
- **First decision:** what do they do with what they just saw — say something out loud, corner each other privately, or corner someone at the assembly who might know more?
- **Story points to open:** Story Point 1 (this scene) leads naturally into Story Points 2 through 5, in any order the table chooses, ahead of the evening's Story Point 6.

## Mission in 30 seconds

- You're four of the five who won Colston its only other state title, 25 years ago.
- You're back for the anniversary. Tonight the current team runs the state final — and one of you just watched your own kid, niece, or sibling get named anchor.
- Jesse, your fifth teammate, is standing right there. He didn't drown 25 years ago. Nobody else seems to be able to see him.
- Something in this town takes its anchor runner, weeks after a state win — it took Jesse, and twice before him.
- You have until the anchor exchange of tonight's final to figure out enough, and do enough, to stop it happening to Nora.

## Current situation

**Table start:** the afternoon reunion assembly, hours before the evening's state final (Story Point 1). Phase A (afternoon) covers Story Points 1–5, reachable in any order. Phase B (evening) is Story Point 6, the state final itself — do not let it play as though it has already happened during Phase A.

## Story Point 1 - The Reunion Assembly

**Mood (table):** Folding chairs on scuffed hardwood, a hand-painted "25 YEARS OF COLSTON PRIDE" banner sagging at one corner, coffee urns and cut flowers. Late-afternoon light through high windows. Reunion chatter fills the room, then drops fast as the principal taps the mic and the whole town turns to look at the same four empty seats you're about to fill.

- **Goal:** establish the reunion, introduce Nora as anchor, and deliver the opening trouble.
- **Situation now:** Principal Auerbach (MC), Coach DENISE ARNHOLT, HAL PREWITT, Jesse's parents RUTH and OWEN CALLOWAY, booster DIANE VANCE, this year's team including NORA SHARPE, and the reunited 2001 squad all present.
- **Spoken lines:** Coach Arnholt: "Twenty-five years, and it's like Colston remembered how to want something again." A booster to Nora, too warmly: "You were always going to end up here, sweetheart." Hal Prewitt, quieter, to Toby: "Didn't think I'd see this again."
- **Discoverable:**

| Trait | Fail | Success | Raise |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Notice (-2)` (spotting JESSE CALLOWAY across the gym) | Nothing unusual noticed | A PC sees Jesse — same age as he ever was, in his old uniform, standing very still, watching Nora, not them | His head turns slowly toward the PC when they look directly at him — he knows he's seen |
| `Notice` (reading Nora) | She just looks like a nervous, excited teenager | She's oddly calm for someone about to run the biggest race of her life | She repeats a phrase back to a booster in exactly the cadence they used a moment ago — a small, wrong echo |

- **GM Note:** Jesse is present and can be approached here, but reacts only in fragments — see `npcs/jesse-calloway.md`. He is never a source of exposition.
- **Pressure/escalation:** if the PCs do nothing, Jesse appears again, more insistently, at the pep rally (Story Point 2); the pattern proceeds on its own schedule regardless.
- **Essential information and vectors:** that Nora is anchor, and that something is already wrong, are each independently available here through (1) seeing Jesse directly, (2) reading Nora's off calm, or (3) an uncomfortable comment from Jesse's mother or Hal Prewitt.
- **Failure changes:** nothing story-stopping — missing every vector here still leaves the same information reachable in Story Points 2–5.
- **Reachable next points:** Story Points 2, 3, 4, 5, in any order.
- **Optional / PC hook:** **PC hook — Toby.** As Nora's father, Toby may act on his own unease without a roll if his player wants to.

## Story Point 2 - Pep Rally and the Retired Jersey

**Mood (table):** Sun still high over red rubber track, stands filling with lawn chairs and folding bleachers, a PA system cycling pop music between announcements. Popcorn and cut grass on the air. Someone's already chanting Nora's name before she's out of the locker room, and it keeps building until it isn't really words anymore.

- **Goal:** deliver a live ritual beat and show Nora's erosion advancing in front of the players.
- **Situation now:** the whole town gathered at the field. Boosters drape Jesse's retired #4 jersey over Nora's shoulders for photos while the crowd chants her name; Coach Arnholt gives a pep talk that unknowingly echoes Hal Prewitt's from 2001 almost word for word.
- **Spoken lines:** Booster DIANE VANCE: "Every great one wears it once before the big race. Just for a minute." Coach Arnholt: "Tonight, Colston remembers what it's like to win." Nora, if pressed, flat and calm: "I feel really good. Really ready."
- **Discoverable:**

| Trait | Fail | Success | Raise |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Notice` (Nora during the chant) | She seems thrilled, nothing more | For three or four seconds while the crowd chants, her eyes go still and unfocused, like she's listening to something else | A PC recognizes the exact stillness from somewhere — a home movie, a photo, the way Jesse looked the week before he vanished |

- **GM Note:** the jersey handoff is a recurring ritual gesture, not a one-off — it happened to Wesley Corbin (1966) and Denise Kowalski (1983) too. Nora's erosion is real and accelerating with each ritual beat she passes through.
- **Pressure/escalation:** each ritual beat the PCs let pass without acting narrows the window before the final race and deepens Nora's calm/compliance by the climax.
- **Essential information and vectors:** Nora's visible erosion is independently available through (1) any PC's direct observation, (2) Toby's parental instinct (no roll needed if his player leans on it), or (3) Dee recognizing the jersey-drape pose from an old team photo, if Story Point 3 has already been played.
- **Failure changes:** costs time and a small increment of Nora's detachment; does not end the adventure.
- **Reachable next points:** Story Point 6.
- **Optional / PC hook:** none.

## Story Point 3 - The Trophy Case and the Archives

**Mood (table):** Fluorescent-lit, smelling of old paper and floor wax, a glass trophy case in the front hall crowded with composite team photos going back decades. Three jerseys, all numbered 4, hang behind glass at different heights, a generation apart.

- **Goal:** ground the historical pattern in checkable facts.
- **Situation now:** open to alumni all day; town historian ARLENE COMBS is on hand and happy to pull yearbooks and clippings.
- **Spoken lines:** Arlene Combs: "Funny, we don't have a big write-up on Wesley Corbin past that one clipping. Some things just fall out of the paper."
- **Discoverable:**

| Trait | Fail | Success | Raise |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Research` or `Notice` | Standard championship history, nothing alarming | Wesley Corbin (1966) and Denise Kowalski (1983) both "drowned" at Miller's Hole a few weeks after their state win, both ran anchor | A brief 1966 letter to the editor uses the phrase "the winner's curse" once, half-joking, and is never mentioned again |

- **GM Note:** Arlene has idly cross-referenced these dates before and calls it "a funny coincidence" — she'll say so only if asked directly whether anything like Jesse's case happened before.
- **Pressure/escalation:** none required.
- **Essential information and vectors:** the anchor-leg pattern and the three-generation gap are independently available here, or through Hal Prewitt (Story Point 4), or through Jesse himself (Story Point 5). Dee's own years of amateur research can hand her player one fact here for free, no roll.
- **Failure changes:** none — fully redundant with Story Points 4 and 5.
- **Reachable next points:** Story Points 4, 6.
- **Optional / PC hook:** optional location; not required for any essential fact.

## Story Point 4 - Hal Prewitt's House

**Mood (table):** A small frame house at the edge of town, lawn a little overgrown, blinds mostly drawn even in daylight. A single lamp burns in a front room stacked with newspapers he says he means to recycle.

- **Goal:** a human vector to the same truth, and a chance to turn a guilt-ridden bystander into a reluctant ally.
- **Situation now:** HAL PREWITT is home; he stopped coming to the field years ago. He answers the door because it's Toby, or because whoever's knocking clearly isn't leaving.
- **Spoken lines:** Hal, deflecting: "Jesse drowned. That's what happened. That's all that happened." Hal, if he breaks: "I used to see him. Out past the fence line. Told myself it was grief. Told myself that for twenty-five years."
- **Discoverable:**

| Trait | Fail | Success | Raise |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Persuasion` | He sticks to the official story | He admits he's seen Jesse too, and that something about "the win" has always felt wrong | He gives up his private clippings and the detail that sightings cluster at the shaft fence, not the water |

- **GM Note:** Hal has never told anyone, including his late wife, what he's seen. Shame, not indifference, has kept him silent.
- **Pressure/escalation:** if pushed too hard too fast, he can shut the door for the day — but remains reachable again that evening, more shaken, especially if approached by Toby or shown evidence from Story Point 3.
- **Essential information and vectors:** independent confirmation Jesse still lingers, and a physical lead toward the shaft fence, are available here, or through Story Points 3 and 5. Toby's personal relationship with Hal is its own vector, no roll needed if leaned on.
- **Failure changes:** none story-ending.
- **Reachable next points:** Story Point 6.
- **Optional / PC hook:** optional; fully redundant with Story Points 3 and 5.

## Story Point 5 - Miller's Hole

**Mood (table):** Flat, black water in an old quarry pit, ringed by scrub pine and a rusted chain-link fence sagging where the ground gave way over the old No. 4 shaft. It's quieter here than it has any right to be a half-mile from a packed stadium — no insects, no wind moving the trees the way it moves everywhere else in town.

- **Goal:** a direct, emotional encounter with Jesse, and a fragmentary lead on breaking the climax.
- **Situation now:** empty except for whoever comes looking. JESSE CALLOWAY can be found here more reliably than anywhere else.
- **Spoken lines:** Jesse does not hold a conversation. At most one or two fragments: her name ("Nora—"), a single warning word ("Don't"), or a wordless gesture toward the fence. Never write him a monologue.
- **Discoverable:**

| Trait | Fail | Success | Raise |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Spirit` (unskilled); `Persuasion` if a PC has it, representing a real attempt to reach him | He stays distant, unreachable tonight | He responds to whoever he was closest to (Toby or Renee) with a single fragment of recognition | He leads that PC to the shaft fence and, wordlessly, shows them it's already been disturbed — someone re-opens a section of it every anniversary as pep-rally decoration |

- **GM Note:** the reopened fence section is where the booster committee strings the rally's bunting every year, without any of them knowing or asking why that section always needs "fixing."
- **Pressure/escalation:** none required.
- **Essential information and vectors:** the shaft fence's role is available here, or through Hal Prewitt (Story Point 4), or through simple experimentation at the climax without any prior lead (Story Point 6 does not require this scene).
- **Failure changes:** none story-ending.
- **Reachable next points:** Story Point 6.
- **Optional / PC hook:** optional; a PC approaching the water's edge carelessly in the dark can be asked for a plain `Agility` footing check — no more than that.

## Story Point 6 - The State Final (climax)

**Mood (table):** Floodlights turn the infield stark white against a dark sky; the stands are full and loud, a low continuous roar rather than individual voices. The air smells like distant tailgate cook-fires and the wet-metal smell before a storm that never quite arrives. Every eye in the place is on the exchange zone as the baton comes down the backstretch toward Nora.

- **Goal:** disrupt the moment the mark would seal, during or immediately after Nora's anchor exchange.
- **Situation now:** Coach DENISE ARNHOLT, DIANE VANCE, and a handful of boosters are fully invested in a clean, perfect night. The race itself runs under two minutes once the gun fires; the PCs' real window opens as the baton nears the anchor exchange.
- **Spoken lines:** Coach Arnholt, if a PC tries to pull Nora: "Are you out of your mind? She is not stepping off this track." Diane Vance, physically blocking: "She waited her whole life for this. You don't get to take it from her." Nora, if reached: "I feel so calm. Isn't that good?"
- **Discoverable:** not applicable as a roll table — see "At-hand rules" below for how to adjudicate the intervention itself.
- **GM Note:** this is the exact moment, every generation, the mark seals — see `secrets.md` Secret 2. The entity itself is never a speaker and never appears as a stat block.
- **Pressure/escalation:** treat this as a short clock — about 4–5 beats (the gun, leg 1, leg 2, leg 3, the exchange to Nora). Every beat spent arguing instead of acting is a beat closer to the exchange completing.
- **Essential information and vectors — at least two must remain available regardless of earlier choices:** physically getting Nora off the track before or during the race; breaking the crowd's collective chanting attention (a scene, a blackout, a scream — anything that fractures the room's focus); reaching Jesse and asking him to act, if any rapport was built earlier; disturbing the shaft fence during the exchange, if that lead was found in Story Point 4 or 5.
- **Failure changes:** if the PCs cannot disrupt the moment, the mark seals. Nothing overtly bad happens at the table tonight — see "End states," below, for what changes afterward.
- **Reachable next points:** closing scene.
- **Optional / PC hook:** none.

## Escalation and clocks

- **Afternoon (Phase A):** no hard clock; each ritual beat the PCs let pass (jersey ceremony especially) deepens Nora's calm/compliance by the evening, per Story Point 2.
- **Evening (Phase B, the climax):** a short clock of about 4–5 beats across the 4x400 final — the gun, legs 1–3, and the anchor exchange. This is the only place tonight a bad decision can actually hurt someone.

## Failure-forward reference

- Missing every vector in Story Points 1–5: the PCs still have Jesse's and Nora's visible states as reason enough to act at the climax (Story Point 6 does not require prior knowledge).
- Failing at the climax: the mark seals. This is a complete, playable ending — see "End states" below — not a stopped session.
- Pushing Hal Prewitt too hard, too fast: he shuts the door for the day, not forever; he can be reached again that evening.

- **Renee Ortiz:** outsider distance and Jesse's old letters/texts as an independent vector to his state of mind; not the only vector.
- **Toby Sharpe:** insider access to Hal Prewitt, Coach Arnholt, and Nora; his family relationship should not be the only vector to any essential fact.
- **Dee Whitfield:** research and pattern-finding; her prior amateur work can hand the table a free fact in Story Point 3 without gating anything behind her alone.
- **Reserve / re-entry:** **Elle Kim** (`characters/elle-kim.md`), the 2001 team's manager and now Colston High's school nurse, present at the field by default. Hand her sheet to any player whose PC is removed early — see `characters.md`, "Early removal mitigation." ## End states

- **Full success:** the PCs disrupt the moment; Nora stays herself. Jesse may finally be freed, at peace — or may simply remain as he was, GM's call based on how the table reached him. The town never learns the truth.
- **Costly success:** Nora is saved, but at a price — a PC is hurt in the scramble, Toby is publicly humiliated or estranged from Coach Arnholt and the boosters for "ruining" the ceremony, or Jesse cannot be reached and remains lost.
- **Partial success:** Nora is pulled back from the worst of it, but some of what the night took can't be undone — she's changed in some small, permanent way the PCs will have to live with.
- **Main-objective failure:** the mark seals during the anchor exchange. Colston wins, the town celebrates, and in the following weeks Nora goes out for a solitary training run and doesn't come back — exactly like Jesse, Wesley, and Denise. The PCs know precisely what's coming and cannot stop it before the story ends.
- **Unexpected outcome:** a PC offers themselves in Nora's place, if the fiction supports it; or the party exposes what they know publicly and the town's response is disbelief or quiet hostility rather than gratitude.
