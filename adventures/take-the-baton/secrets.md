# Secrets, Revelations, and Twists

Use only when they improve the adventure. A twist is not required.

## Secret 1 - Jesse Didn't Drown

- **Truth:** Jesse Calloway was not killed in 2001. He was hollowed by the entity under Colston's field and has lingered near the field and Miller's Hole ever since — physically present, barely able to act or speak, and very difficult for most people to consciously notice or hold in memory.
- **Who knows:** No one in Colston consciously "knows" this. Hal Prewitt has seen Jesse and suppressed it as grief for 25 years. Ruth and Owen Calloway (Jesse's parents) sincerely believe he drowned and have never seen him, though they sometimes feel an inexplicable pull toward the field on certain evenings.
- **Why it matters:** it's the emotional core of the adventure and proof, if the PCs can hold onto it, that Nora's fate is not inevitable or natural.
- **Player-facing signs:** Jesse visibly present at the assembly and pep rally, watching Nora, reacting subtly when a PC looks directly at him; his stillness and his old uniform, unaged.
- **Independent discovery vectors:**
  1. Directly seeing and approaching Jesse (Story Points 1, 2, 5).
  2. Hal Prewitt's confession, if reached with compassion or evidence (Story Point 4).
  3. Cross-referencing his 2001 disappearance against 1966 and 1983 in the archives, which at minimum proves the town's official story can't be the whole truth (Story Point 3).
- **If never discovered:** the PCs can still act on Nora's visible erosion alone; Jesse remains a haunting, unresolved presence at the table, which is an acceptable ending, not a failure state.
- **If discovered early:** strengthens the PCs' resolve and gives them an ally (however limited) for the climax; does not shortcut the climax's own intervention requirement.
- **False assumptions/red herrings:** none needed; do not invent a fake alternate culprit. The town's genuine belief that Jesse drowned is not a lie for the PCs to catch anyone in — nobody is lying to them.

## Secret 2 - The Entity Under the Field

- **Truth:** something beneath the sealed No. 4 shaft answers Colston's concentrated collective wanting, and has done so since the 1958 collapse. It takes payment through whoever runs anchor leg at a state-championship win, sealed at the moment of peak crowd attention (the anchor exchange), and claims that person weeks later near Miller's Hole. It has no name, no voice, and no negotiable will — it is never a speaker and never appears as a character with dialogue.
- **Who knows:** no one, fully. The town's prosperity since 1962 is real and unexplained even to itself; nobody has ever consciously connected it to a bargain.
- **Why it matters:** it's the mechanism the PCs must disrupt at the climax, and it's why nobody in Colston can be treated as a villain — nobody chose this on purpose.
- **Player-facing signs:** the pep-rally chant, the retired-jersey ceremony, and the anchor exchange all function as ritual beats without anyone framing them that way; Nora's stillness during the chant (Story Point 2).
- **Independent discovery vectors:**
  1. The pattern in the archives (Story Point 3) — three anchor runners, three disappearances, one recurring role.
  2. Hal Prewitt's private suspicion that "something about the win" has always felt wrong (Story Point 4).
  3. Jesse's fragmentary gesture toward the shaft fence at Miller's Hole (Story Point 5).
- **If never discovered:** the PCs can still recognize Nora is in danger and act at the climax through direct intervention (pulling her from the race, breaking the crowd's attention) without ever understanding the underlying mechanism.
- **If discovered early:** lets the PCs plan a more targeted climax intervention (e.g., disturbing the shaft fence during the anchor exchange) rather than improvising.
- **False assumptions/red herrings:** none. Do not let a player theory (a specific booster is "the cult leader," etc.) get confirmed — correct it gently through play: nobody is orchestrating this consciously.

## Secret 3 - The Pattern: 1966, 1983, 2001

- **Truth:** Wesley Corbin (1966) and Denise Kowalski (1983) vanished the same way Jesse did — anchor leg, state championship, weeks later, near Miller's Hole, no body recovered. The gaps between incidents (17, 18, 25 years) are long enough that no living Colston resident has ever laid all three side by side.
- **Who knows:** town historian Arlene Combs has the raw materials in the archive but has never connected them; she will voice the coincidence aloud only if asked directly whether anything like Jesse's case has happened before.
- **Why it matters:** turns Jesse's death from an isolated tragedy into proof of a repeating, structural pattern — and gives the PCs the confidence that this is happening again, tonight, to Nora.
- **Player-facing signs:** three retired #4 jerseys in the trophy case, spaced a generation apart; old clippings that thin out suspiciously around each disappearance.
- **Independent discovery vectors:**
  1. The trophy case and library archive (Story Point 3).
  2. Hal Prewitt's private clippings on Wesley and Denise (Story Point 4).
  3. Dee Whitfield's own years of amateur research, if her player leans into it (Story Point 3, no roll required for her own prior findings).
- **If never discovered:** the PCs act on Jesse's and Nora's cases alone; still sufficient to motivate and resolve the climax.
- **If discovered early:** strengthens resolve and may inform how they approach Coach Arnholt or Hal Prewitt for help.
- **False assumptions/red herrings:** a fair distinguishable-from-essential detail: the 1966 letter-to-the-editor phrase "the winner's curse" is real color, not a clue to a name or culprit — it's townspeople half-joking, nothing more.

## Secret 4 - The Shaft Fence

- **Truth:** the sagging section of chain-link fence around the old No. 4 shaft, out near Miller's Hole, is unknowingly reopened and re-strung with pep-rally bunting every anniversary by the booster committee — none of whom know or ask why that section always needs "fixing." Disturbing or holding that fence during the anchor exchange is one workable way to break tonight's mark.
- **Who knows:** no one consciously; it's treated as routine maintenance.
- **Why it matters:** gives the PCs a concrete, physical option at the climax beyond social conflict with Coach Arnholt and the boosters.
- **Player-facing signs:** the fence's odd, recurring disrepair; Jesse's fragmentary gesture toward it.
- **Independent discovery vectors:**
  1. Jesse's wordless lead at Miller's Hole (Story Point 5).
  2. Hal Prewitt's detail that sightings cluster at the fence, not the water (Story Point 4).
  3. A PC who simply goes to Miller's Hole and makes a `Notice` roll against the fence itself, unprompted (GM's call — allow this even without Story Point 5 or 4 having been played).
- **If never discovered:** the climax remains fully resolvable through pulling Nora from the race or breaking the crowd's attention (Story Point 6); this is one option among several, never the only one.
- **If discovered early:** lets the PCs stage a two-pronged climax (one PC at the fence, others at the track).
- **False assumptions/red herrings:** none.
