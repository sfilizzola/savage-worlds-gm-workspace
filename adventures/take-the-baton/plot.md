# Plot Model

This is not a scene script. Define stable truths, desired objectives, and adaptable story points. The players choose the path and may change the situation.

## Main throughline

- **GM's desired destination:** the PCs realize what's been happening in Colston for three generations, and disrupt the moment tonight's mark would seal on Nora Sharpe, during the state final's anchor leg.
- **What must remain flexible:** how they learn the pattern (archives, Hal Prewitt, Jesse, or some combination), and how they intervene at the climax (physical removal, breaking the crowd's collective attention, reaching Jesse, or disturbing the shaft/quarry itself). No single vector is required for either.
- **What failure changes:** if the mark seals tonight, Nora does not vanish at the table — she vanishes in the following weeks, exactly like Jesse, Wesley, and Denise before her. The PCs leave Colston knowing it's coming and unable to stop it before the session ends. This is a legitimate, playable ending, not a failure to prevent by any other means.

## Five-beat map

Pacing diagnostic. Not a visit order. Full scores live in `QUALITY.md`.

| Beat | Delivered by | Story point(s) |
|---|---|---|
| Explosive opening (first 10–15 min of play) | A PC sees Jesse, unmistakably present and wrong, across the reunion assembly | Story Point 1 |
| Investigation and meaningful choices | Archives, Hal Prewitt, Miller's Hole/Jesse — any combination | Story Points 3, 4, 5 |
| Escalation or reversal | The pep rally and retired-jersey ceremony visibly advance Nora's change | Story Point 2 |
| Climactic set piece | The state final's anchor leg | Story Point 6 |
| Consequences and closure | End states below | — |

## Story Point 1 - The Reunion Assembly

- **Purpose:** deliver the opening trouble and introduce Nora, the town, and the ceremony's shape.
- **Reachable when:** table start.
- **Situation:** the school gym, packed for a joint assembly honoring the 2001 team's 25th anniversary and introducing this year's state-qualified squad. Principal, Coach Denise Arnholt, Hal Prewitt, Jesse's parents, and assorted boosters are all present. Coach Arnholt names Nora Sharpe as this year's anchor for the first time publicly; the room cheers hard.
- **Spoken lines:** Coach Arnholt: "Twenty-five years, and it's like Colston remembered how to want something again." A booster to Nora, too warmly: "You were always going to end up here, sweetheart." Hal Prewitt, quieter, to Toby: "Didn't think I'd see this again."
- **Essential information:** Nora is anchor, freshly decided; this is specifically the 25th anniversary of the PCs' own championship; something in the room's collective attention already feels wrong.
- **Discoverable rolls:**
  | Trait | Fail | Success | Raise |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `Notice (-2)` (spotting Jesse across the gym or later at the field) | Nothing unusual noticed | A PC sees Jesse — same age as he ever was, in his old uniform, standing very still, watching Nora, not them | The PC realizes Jesse's head turns slowly toward them when they look directly at him — he knows he's seen |
  | `Notice` (reading Nora) | She just looks like a nervous, excited teenager | She's oddly calm for someone about to run the biggest race of her life | She repeats a phrase back to a booster in exactly the cadence they used to her a moment ago — a small, wrong echo |
- **Independent vectors:** seeing Jesse directly; noticing Nora's off calm; a too-specific, uncomfortable comment from Jesse's mother or Hal Prewitt.
- **Escalation:** if the PCs do nothing, Jesse appears again, more insistently, at the pep rally; the pattern proceeds on its own schedule regardless.
- **Failure consequences:** none — this is an information point. Missing every vector here still leaves the pattern discoverable in Story Points 2–5.
- **Links forward:** Story Points 2, 3, 4.
- **Optional/essential:** essential.

## Story Point 2 - Pep Rally and the Retired Jersey

- **Purpose:** deliver a live ritual beat and show Nora's erosion advancing in front of the players.
- **Reachable when:** later the same day, ahead of the evening meet.
- **Situation:** the whole town gathered at the field for the pep rally. Boosters drape Jesse's retired #4 jersey over Nora's shoulders for a photo — "for luck," "for tradition" — while the crowd chants her name. Coach Arnholt gives a pep talk that unknowingly echoes Hal Prewitt's from 2001 almost word for word.
- **Spoken lines:** Booster club president Diane Vance: "Every great one wears it once before the big race. Just for a minute." Coach Arnholt: "Tonight, Colston remembers what it's like to win." Nora, if pressed by a PC, flat and calm: "I feel really good. Really ready."
- **Discoverable rolls:**
  | Trait | Fail | Success | Raise |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `Notice` (Smarts) on Nora during the chant | She seems thrilled, nothing more | For three or four seconds while the crowd chants, her eyes go still and unfocused, like she's listening to something else | A PC recognizes the exact stillness from somewhere — a home movie, a photo, the way Jesse looked in the week before he vanished |
- **Essential information:** the jersey handoff is a recurring ritual gesture, not a one-off; Nora's erosion is visibly measurable and accelerating with each ritual beat.
- **Independent vectors:** direct observation of Nora (any PC); Toby's parental instinct flags it even on a failed roll ("GM Note: as her father, Toby may act on this without a roll if the player wants to"); Dee, if she's already found the old team photos in Story Point 3, recognizes the jersey-drape pose matches one from 1983.
- **Escalation:** each ritual beat the PCs let pass narrows the window before the final race; delaying past the pep rally does not stop the plot but does increase Nora's calm/compliance at the climax.
- **Failure consequences:** costs time and a small increment of Nora's growing detachment; does not end the adventure.
- **Links forward:** Story Point 6.
- **Optional/essential:** essential.

## Story Point 3 - The Trophy Case and the Archives

- **Purpose:** ground the pattern in checkable facts — this has happened before, twice, and no one has ever laid it side by side.
- **Reachable when:** any time the PCs have a quiet stretch before the meet; the library and trophy case are open to alumni all day for the anniversary.
- **Situation:** the school library and adjoining trophy case, full of composite team photos, retired jerseys (#4 appears three times: 1966, 1983, 2001), and local paper clippings the town historian, Arlene Combs, is happy to pull for "old team boys and girls."
- **Spoken lines:** Arlene Combs: "Funny, we don't have a big write-up on Wesley Corbin past that one clipping. Some things just fall out of the paper." 
- **Discoverable rolls:**
  | Trait | Fail | Success | Raise |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `Research` or `Notice` (Smarts) | Standard championship history, nothing alarming | Wesley Corbin (1966) and Denise Kowalski (1983) both "drowned" at Miller's Hole a few weeks after their state win, both ran anchor | A brief 1966 letter to the editor uses the phrase "the winner's curse" once, half-joking, and is never mentioned again in any later issue |
- **Essential information:** the anchor-leg pattern; the phrase "the winner's curse"; the three-generation gap.
- **Independent vectors:** the archive itself; Arlene Combs's memory and gossip; Dee's own years of amateur digging (if her player leans into it, she can supply one fact here for free, no roll, framed as "you already half-knew this").
- **Escalation:** none required.
- **Failure consequences:** none — Story Points 4 and 5 supply the same essential information through other people.
- **Links forward:** Story Points 4, 6.
- **Optional/essential:** essential (redundant vectors; this specific location is not mandatory).

## Story Point 4 - Hal Prewitt's House

- **Purpose:** a human vector for the same truth, and a chance to turn a guilt-ridden bystander into a reluctant ally.
- **Reachable when:** any time after Story Point 1; Hal lives alone at the edge of town and doesn't go to the field anymore.
- **Situation:** a small, over-neat house. Hal keeps his own private clippings on Wesley and Denise in a drawer he doesn't show anyone. He is defensive at first, then breaks if approached with real compassion or real evidence.
- **Spoken lines:** Hal, deflecting: "Jesse drowned. That's what happened. That's all that happened." Hal, if he breaks: "I used to see him. Out past the fence line. Told myself it was grief. Told myself that for twenty-five years."
- **Discoverable rolls:**
  | Trait | Fail | Success | Raise |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `Persuasion` (Spirit) | He sticks to the official story | He admits he's seen Jesse too, and that something about "the win" has always felt wrong to him | He gives up his private clippings and the detail that the sightings always cluster near the old shaft fence, not the water itself |
- **Essential information:** independent confirmation Jesse is still present; a physical lead toward the shaft fence as a place that matters, not just the quarry water.
- **Independent vectors:** evidence from Story Point 3 makes this easier (not required); Toby's personal relationship with Hal is its own vector, no roll needed if the player leans on it.
- **Escalation:** if the PCs push too hard too fast, Hal can shut down for the day — but he can still be reached again after the pep rally, later and more shaken.
- **Failure consequences:** none story-ending; this is one of several vectors to the same information.
- **Links forward:** Story Point 6.
- **Optional/essential:** optional (fully redundant with Story Points 3 and 5).

## Story Point 5 - Miller's Hole

- **Purpose:** a direct, emotional encounter with Jesse himself, and a fragmentary lead on how the climax can actually be broken.
- **Reachable when:** any time the PCs go looking, or are drawn there after seeing Jesse.
- **Situation:** the flooded quarry a half-mile from the field, quiet, dark water, an old chain-link fence around the collapsed shaft's fenced-off remnant nearby. Jesse can be found here more easily than anywhere else.
- **Spoken lines:** Jesse does not hold a conversation. He may manage one or two fragments — her name ("Nora—"), a single warning word ("Don't"), or simply point at the fence line. Nothing more articulate; do not write him a monologue.
- **Discoverable rolls:**
  | Trait | Fail | Success | Raise |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `Spirit` (unskilled; `Persuasion` if a PC has it, representing a real attempt to reach him) | He stays distant, unreachable tonight | He responds to whoever he was closest to (Toby or Renee) with a single fragment of recognition | He leads that PC to the shaft fence and, wordlessly, shows them it's already been disturbed — someone (unknowingly, ritually) reopens a section of it every anniversary as part of the pep rally decorations |
- **Essential information:** the shaft fence, not only the water, is physically tied to the ritual; disturbing or holding it during the anchor leg is one workable way to break tonight's mark.
- **Independent vectors:** this scene; Hal's fence detail (Story Point 4); or simple experimentation at the climax if the PCs never learn this at all (see Story Point 6, which does not require this information to attempt an intervention).
- **Escalation:** none required.
- **Failure consequences:** none story-ending.
- **Links forward:** Story Point 6.
- **Optional/essential:** optional.

## Story Point 6 - The State Final

- **Purpose:** the climax. Nora takes the baton for anchor while the crowd's attention peaks; this is the exact window in which the mark seals, exactly as it did for Jesse, Wesley, and Denise.
- **Reachable when:** that evening, once the meet reaches the 4x400 relay final.
- **Situation:** the packed stands, the field lit, the PA announcer, Coach Arnholt and the boosters fully invested in Nora running clean. The race itself takes roughly a minute; the PCs' real window is the run-up to the gun and the race itself.
- **Spoken lines:** Coach Arnholt, if a PC tries to pull Nora: "Are you out of your mind? She is not stepping off this track." Diane Vance, blocking physically: "She waited her whole life for this. You don't get to take it from her." Nora, if reached: "I feel so calm. Isn't that good?"
- **Discoverable/opposition:** this is not a monster fight. Opposition is Colston's own well-meaning resistance — Coach Arnholt, Diane Vance, and a handful of boosters who will physically and socially block anything that looks like "ruining the ceremony." Treat pulling Nora off the track, or holding the crowd's attention, or reaching the shaft fence in time as a **Dramatic Task** or contested **Test of Wills**, whichever the table's approach fits — use the mechanic the fiction needs.
- **Independent vectors to a successful intervention (at least two must be available regardless of earlier choices):** physically getting Nora off the track before or during the race; breaking the crowd's collective, chanting attention (a scene, a blackout, a scream — something that fractures the room's focus); reaching Jesse and asking him to act, if the PCs have built any rapport with him; disturbing the shaft fence during the race if that lead was found.
- **Escalation:** every full lap the PCs spend arguing instead of acting is a lap closer to the anchor exchange; treat this as a short clock (about 4–5 beats: the gun, leg 1, leg 2, leg 3, the exchange to Nora).
- **Failure consequences:** if the PCs cannot disrupt the moment, the mark seals. Nora runs a spectacular anchor leg, Colston wins again, and the town celebrates. Nothing overtly bad happens at the table tonight — the cost is entirely in the following weeks (see End states).
- **Links forward:** closing scene / end states.
- **Optional/essential:** essential.

## Dynamic forces

| Force/NPC | Wants | Acts when | If ignored | Can change sides when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The entity (never a speaker) | Payment through whoever runs anchor, sealed at peak collective attention | The pep rally chant, the jersey ceremony, the anchor exchange | Takes Nora on schedule, weeks from now, exactly like before | Cannot change sides — it does not negotiate and is never reasoned with |
| Jesse (hollowed, lingering) | To warn or protect Nora; barely able to act | When PCs approach him directly, or when Nora is in the most acute danger | Nora's fate proceeds unchanged | Already effectively on the PCs' side; grows more capable of acting the more the PCs reach him with real recognition, not pity |
| Hal Prewitt | To protect the town's comfortable story, and his own guilt, from being confirmed | When directly confronted with evidence or genuine compassion | Withholds, but does not actively obstruct | Breaks and helps if approached by Toby or shown real proof |
| Coach Arnholt, Diane Vance, and the boosters | A perfect, honoring ceremony and a winning night for Colston | The climax, if the PCs try to pull Nora or disrupt the event | Proceed exactly as planned | Could become horrified allies afterward if shown undeniable proof, but not in time to help tonight |
| Nora Sharpe | Growing pull toward calm, certainty, belonging — not fully her own wanting anymore | Every ritual beat (jersey, chant, exchange) | Drifts further from herself with each beat | Can still be reached and resist if a PC gets to her before the anchor exchange seals it |

## Revelation audit

| Essential revelation | Vector 1 | Vector 2 | Vector 3 | If all are missed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wesley Corbin (1966) and Denise Kowalski (1983) vanished the same way Jesse did | Trophy case / archives (SP3) | Hal Prewitt (SP4) | Jesse's own fragments at Miller's Hole (SP5) | PCs still know Jesse's own case and can act on that alone; the deeper pattern remains an unconfirmed dread rather than proof |
| Jesse did not drown — he's still here, lingering | Seeing him directly (SP1, SP2) | Hal's confession (SP4) | Miller's Hole encounter (SP5) | The PCs still have Nora's visible erosion (SP1, SP2) as reason enough to act |
| The shaft fence, not just the water, is where tonight's mark can be broken | Jesse's gesture at Miller's Hole (SP5) | Hal's private detail (SP4) | Direct experimentation at the climax (SP6 allows this without prior knowledge) | The climax's other vectors (pulling Nora, breaking the crowd's attention, reaching Jesse) remain available without this fact |

## Possible end states

Describe consequences, not scripted endings.

- **Full success:** the PCs disrupt the moment; Nora stays herself. Jesse may finally be freed, at peace — or may simply remain as he was, unresolved, GM's call based on how the table reached him. The town never learns the truth; the "winner's curse" goes back to being a half-forgotten joke.
- **Costly success:** Nora is saved, but at a price — a PC is hurt in the scramble, Toby is publicly humiliated or estranged from Coach Arnholt and the boosters for "ruining" the ceremony, or Jesse cannot be reached and remains lost.
- **Partial success:** Nora is pulled back from the worst of it, but some of what the night took can't be undone — she's changed in some small, permanent way the PCs will have to live with.
- **Main-objective failure:** the mark seals during the anchor exchange. Colston wins, the town celebrates, and in the following weeks Nora goes out for a solitary training run and doesn't come back — exactly like Jesse, Wesley, and Denise. The PCs leave Colston knowing precisely what's coming and unable to stop it before the story ends.
- **Unexpected outcome:** a PC offers themselves in Nora's place, if the fiction supports it; or the party exposes what they know publicly and the town's response is disbelief or quiet hostility rather than gratitude — Colston does not want to know.
