## BG-OH-002 — Operation Nordwind (clock)

- **Title:** Operation Nordwind (North Wind)
- **Date of events:** 31 December 1944 – 25 January 1945
- **File / link:** [Wikipedia: Operation Nordwind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Nordwind); U.S. Army *Riviera to the Rhine* (HyperWar) for the Alsace fighting
- **Provenance / rights:** published histories; check edition if quoting
- **Intended use:** southern-front clock for the briefing. Not a claim that Dinkelsbühl ran the offensive.
- **Confidence:** **Fact** that Nordwind was the last major German Western offensive, in Alsace / Lorraine / Upper Rhine, in this window. **Fiction** that this town’s hydra materially helped troop or rail movement toward that front (hundreds of km; no sourced exchange here).
- **Table pin:** a night in **early January 1945**, while Nordwind is on. Recommended: **6–7 January** (after the first attacks, before the 25 January halt). Do not treat a named hour of the offensive as a fail clock.

## BG-OH-003 — January 1945 winter (weather)

- **Title:** Munich / Bavaria daily weather, January 1945
- **File / link:** [Munich 1945 (NOAA-derived tables)](https://www.extremeweatherwatch.com/cities/munich/year-1945); Bavarian station extracts e.g. [chroniknet Bayern 12.1.1945](https://chroniknet.de/historisches-wetter/bayern/12.1.1945)
- **Provenance / rights:** compiled meteorological tables; not a Dinkelsbühl station
- **Intended use:** winter pressure (cold, snow, ice, short day). Not a frostbite death gate at the LZ.
- **Confidence:** **Fact** that January 1945 in Bavaria was a hard winter (Munich lows commonly well below 0 °C; 6 January 1945 Munich listed about −6 °C / −14.5 °C). **Inference** that Dinkelsbühl (~400 m, Middle Franconia) was also frozen, with snow and ice on walls and water. **Unknown:** exact snow depth and wind on the Wörnitz that night.
- **Table pin:** sub-zero night, snow on the ground, ice on stone, long dark. Cold taxes time if they linger; it does not remove a player in the opening.

## BG-OH-005 — Rathaus and Altrathausplatz

- **Title:** Neues Rathaus / Altes Rathaus / Altrathausplatz
- **File / link:** [Altes Rathaus (Dinkelsbühl)](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altes_Rathaus_(Dinkelsb%C3%BChl)); Bavarian monument list for Segringer Straße 30 (ehem. Reichsposthalterei, since 1855 Neues Rathaus)
- **Intended use:** dummy site vs live board geography
- **Confidence:**
  - **Fact:** civic administration since 1855 at **Segringer Straße 30** (Neues Rathaus). That building was built 1733 as **Reichsposthalterei** (imperial post station) and Gasthof zum Stern.
  - **Fact:** **Altes Rathaus** at **Altrathausplatz 14**, east toward Wörnitztor / Ledermarkt; town hall until 1855.
  - **Inference:** a 1944 briefing officer could mark the *Rathaus* as “the post / the exchange” because the Neues Rathaus *was* the old post house. That leftover is fair.
  - **Fiction:** a live Fernmeldeamt in either building in January 1945; Brandt’s billets; operators on Altrathausplatz.

## BG-OH-007 — Ranks (table speech)

- **Title:** Heer and Allied rank titles, January 1945
- **File / link:** Weird War II p.153 (Heer / Kriegsmarine / Luftwaffe title table); p.61 (British Army titles); US sheets use period titles. U.S. enlisted *pay grades* in 1945 were 1st–7th Grade, not the 1949 E/O codes (EO 9041; WW2 book E/O column is a game overlay).
- **Intended use:** what to print on sheets and say at the table
- **Confidence:**
  - **Fact:** Hauptmann, Oberleutnant, Feldwebel, Gefreiter are ordinary Heer titles. U.S. First Lieutenant, Sergeant, Corporal, Private are ordinary 1945 U.S. titles. British Sergeant / Corporal / Private likewise.
  - **Inference:** a mixed team talks in English glosses (Sgt / Cpl) even for Polish, Free French, and Dutch members. French *Sergent* / Dutch *Korporaal* are the same jobs.
  - **Fiction:** women holding those ordinary combat titles on both sides (world rule). Historically, German *Nachrichtenhelferinnen* (“Blitzmädel”) ran boards and radios as **auxiliaries**, not as Oberleutnant / Gefreiter combat ranks (DGPT / Wehrmachthelferinnenkorps, unified 29 Nov 1944).
- **Table pin:** keep the printed titles. Do not put E-5 / O-2 on the player sheets as 1945 fact. Do not lecture that Keller’s rank is historical.

## BG-OH-008 — Nachrichten practice (pattern, not this town)

- **Title:** How German signal work was split (Fernsprecher / Funk / keys)
- **File / link:** U.S. TM-E 30-451 (Mar 1945) via [Lone Sentry, GHQ signal units](http://www.lonesentry.com/manuals/tme30/ch2sec6sub16.html); [Lexikon: Nachrichtenverbände](https://www.lexikon-der-wehrmacht.de/Zusatz/Heer/Nachrichtenverbaende-R.htm); DGPT on Nachrichtenhelferinnen
- **Intended use:** justify three heads as a *plausible tiny detachment*, not as a sourced Dinkelsbühl exchange
- **Confidence:**
  - **Fact:** German practice separates landline work (*Fernsprechvermittlung* / Ortsvermittlung) from radio (*Funk*). A *Feldnachrichtenkommandantur* is an **army-area** static signal HQ for permanent plant — not a small-town office. Reichspost built and ran cable plant that the Wehrmacht used (e.g. Amt 500 / “Zeppelin” at Zossen — scale illustration only).
  - **Fact:** women auxiliaries operated boards, teleprinters, and radio so men could be sent forward. They were not meant to bear arms as infantry.
  - **Inference:** a handful of *Nachrichten* people in a rear town could keep a board in one building, a set/aerial on high ground, and key books with an NCO. Killing any two of those would gut a *tiny* net.
  - **Fiction:** that this net exists in Dinkelsbühl; that it helps Nordwind; that the three rooms are a Fernmeldeamt. A Wehrmeldeamt (BG-OH-006) is a reporting office, not an exchange.
- **Table pin:** keep the three heads. If asked “was this real?”, the answer is no; the split is how the job works, not a 1945 installation list.

## BG-OH-009 — Night length and moon (6–7 January)

- **Title:** Astronomical night, Dinkelsbühl latitude; moon 6 January 1945
- **File / link:** Last Quarter on **6 January 1945** ([Catalina Sky Survey moon calendar](https://catalina.lpl.arizona.edu/moon-phases/month/1945-01); [TheSkyLive](https://theskylive.com/moon-calendar?month=01&year=1945)). Day length at ~49°N early January is about **8½–9½ hours** (modern Dinkelsbühl ephemeris 6 Jan: sunrise ~08:13, sunset ~16:37 CET — same astronomy in 1945; Germany on MEZ).
- **Intended use:** short night, default Dark
- **Confidence:**
  - **Fact:** Last Quarter that calendar day; ~half-lit moon. Last-quarter moon rises around midnight.
  - **Inference:** first half of the night is darker; after moonrise a clear snow sky can be Dim. Cloud cover that night is **unknown**.
  - **Do not use:** Greenwich sunrise/sunset from moon-phase sites as Dinkelsbühl clock times.
- **Table pin:** they have a long dark and not a full moon. Default **Dark (−4)** (SWADE p.102). Dim only with a lamp or a clear, moonlit stretch after midnight.

## BG-OH-010 — Kit (period names)

- **Title:** Small arms and charges in use by January 1945
- **File / link:** Weird War II pp. 41 (TNT, satchel), 52 (U.S.), 60 (British), 152 (German). Volkssturm: [Wikipedia: Volkssturm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkssturm) (public 16–18 Oct 1944).
- **Intended use:** what may appear on sheets without being anachronistic *as objects*
- **Confidence:**
  - **Fact:** M1 Carbine, M1911A1, Mk2, Sten, Enfield .38, Kar 98k, MP40, P38, Stielhandgranate, ½-lb TNT, satchel charges are all in service by late 1944.
  - **Fact:** Volkssturm exists by this night; weapons were short (rifles leftover, sometimes little else).
  - **Inference:** a deep raid might carry civilian coats and papers (SOE / Jedburgh habit). Jedburgh teams were typically **three men** (British, American, French) — not this four, and not with women in combat MOS.
  - **Fiction:** this team, this loadout in this town, women as ordinary combat NCOs, IR 468 issue.
- **Table pin:** keep the printed guns. Do not add StG44, MG42 squads, or jets. Do not treat the mixed drop as a documented Jedburgh.

## BG-OH-012 — Pont-à-Mousson (briefing town)

- **Title:** Liberation of Pont-à-Mousson; Lorraine rear by January 1945
- **Date of events:** 4–17 September 1944 (liberation); table pin 6–7 January 1945
- **File / link:** U.S. Army *The Lorraine Campaign* (CMH; XII Corps Moselle crossing); local accounts of 80th Infantry Division at Pont-à-Mousson, 4–17 September 1944
- **Provenance / rights:** published campaign history; check edition if quoting
- **Intended use:** a real conquered town in Allied Lorraine for the calm briefing. Not a claim that this raid or a night strip here is documented.
- **Confidence:**
  - **Fact:** Pont-à-Mousson on the Moselle was fought over and liberated in September 1944 (80th Infantry Division; full clearance of the right bank by 17 September).
  - **Inference:** by the night of 6–7 January 1945 the fighting is in Alsace (Nordwind) and on the Saar; this town is Allied rear, quiet enough for a calm room. Distance to Dinkelsbühl is on the order of **300–350 km** — a plausible night lift.
  - **Fiction:** Capt. Ellis Ward; this four-person briefing; a night strip outside town; en-route flak on this flight; the missed west DZ.
- **Table pin:** commandeered schoolroom, bilingual street, no shells on the roof. Nordwind is the *reason* for the raid, not the window rattling.
