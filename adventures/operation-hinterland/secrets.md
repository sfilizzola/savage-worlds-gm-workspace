# Secrets, Revelations, and Twists

GM-facing. These are predetermined truths of this adventure where marked. They are not session history.

## Secret 1 - The briefing named the wrong building

- **Truth:** The **Rathaus** is a **false head**. It is not the exchange. Signals moved (or intel always had the obvious door). Officers may still keep an office there. Cutting it does **not** count as a head.
- **Who knows:** signals people; some clerks; officers who deal with the net. Not every civilian. **Capt. Ellis Ward** does not know. He believes the Rathaus is a real head.
- **Why it matters:** this is the plan fuckup they must improvise around. They learn it on the ground, not in the briefing room.
- **Player-facing signs:** empty or ordinary interior; wire that leaves; a habit patrol on a dead address; no aerial here.
- **Independent discovery vectors:**
  1. Enter or watch the briefed site.
  2. Read the skyline or a feed line that does not terminate there.
  3. Testimony from a clerk, operator, or officer, or a leftover note.
- **If never discovered:** they can still gut two real heads by reading the town. Wrecking only the dummy site is not success.
- **If discovered early:** more night for the real net. Do not punish them with a thinner town.
- **False assumptions/red herrings:** the dummy site may look "military" enough to fool a rushed glance. Fair tell: no live traffic.

## Secret 2 - The hydra has three heads

- **Truth:** Three real heads: board, loft, books. Cut **any two** and the hydra dies. The hydra rule **and the three types** are handed at briefing. The three **locations** are not. Ward pins the Rathaus as the exchange (the board). That pin is wrong.
- **Who knows:** the signals detachment and the responsible officers. Operators may only know their own room. Ward knows “board, aerial, books — cut two”; he believes the Rathaus is the board; he does not know the live pins.
- **Why it matters:** prevents a single locked room from ending the mission. Players already know they need two heads. They must still find them.
- **Player-facing signs:** landline gear in one place, aerial in another, a satchel that moves.
- **Independent discovery vectors:**
  1. Observation.
  2. Dummy-site leftover.
  3. People (clerk, NCO, officer, civilian who hates the hum).
- **If never discovered:** they may still hit two obvious real sites. If they hit one and leave, that is a wounded hydra — partial or fail, not a hidden rule.
- **If discovered early:** they can plan which two to take. That is allowed.
- **False assumptions/red herrings:** "blow the Rathaus" as complete success. Distinguishable once they see no traffic. The November 468 overlay on BG-OH-001 is the same class of lie.

## Secret 3 - The fuse is officers, not a hidden army

- **Truth:** Named responsible officers can escalate (curfew, hunt, a call if the hydra still lives). There is no off-map battalion waiting on one Stealth fail.
- **Who knows:** the town lives under **Hptm. Stein** and **Oblt. Keller**. The briefing may name them; locals will have them.
- **Why it matters:** delay and noise have a face. Escalation stays on a clock.
- **Player-facing signs:** rounds, a staff car (Stein's Kübelwagen, kept off Weinmarkt — see `locations.md` "Extraction vehicles"), a voice that others obey, a threatened call. Ward names Stein and Keller in the briefing; that is not the same as meeting them.
- **Independent discovery vectors:**
  1. Briefing names.
  2. Street observation.
  3. Civilian or clerk talk.
- **If never discovered:** dawn and a living hydra still escalate without a named face.
- **If discovered early:** they can avoid, use, or hunt an officer. Capturing one is optional, not required.
- **False assumptions/red herrings:** none required. Do not imply SS fortress strength.

## Secret 4 - The drop misses (opening, not a twist)

- **Truth:** Ward marked west / Segringer Tor. Weather and a dark DZ put them on the east / Wörnitz face, together. Flak is en route, not this garrison. The town stays asleep.
- **Who knows:** GM. Ward does not know. The four learn it on the snow.
- **Why it matters:** the briefing cannot tell them they landed wrong. Discovery is play.
- **Player-facing signs:** water on this side of the wall; the rehearsed gate is not in front of them; the map draws water on the east.
- **Independent discovery vectors:**
  1. Water on the briefing map vs water here.
  2. Segringer is not the near gate.
  3. Lang can name Wörnitztor (not the only vector).
- **If never discovered:** they still have water in front of them and a wall. Do not hide the water behind a failed Notice.
- **If discovered early:** correct — that is the opening.
- **False assumptions/red herrings:** do not treat this as a second twist. The dummy Rathaus remains the one twist.

## Secret 5 - Standing fictions (accuracy)

- **Truth:** The raid, the hydra, the 4-person team, this briefing and night strip at Pont-à-Mousson, and women in ordinary ranks on both sides are invented for this table. Dinkelsbühl's walls, winter, rear-area quiet in January 1945, and Pont-à-Mousson as Allied rear are the researched frame.
- **Who knows:** GM. Players need the world rule at the table (gender is not a question). They do not need a lecture on which facts are fake.
- **Why it matters:** researched prep must not launder fictions as sourced history.
- **Player-facing signs:** none as a "secret to discover."
- **Independent discovery vectors:** n/a.
- **If never discovered:** correct. These are design notes.
- **If discovered early:** n/a.
