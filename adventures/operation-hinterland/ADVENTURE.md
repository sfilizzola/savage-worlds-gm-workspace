## One-sentence premise

Four Seasoned Allied specialists, briefed in Allied Lorraine and dropped onto the wrong face of Dinkelsbühl in early January 1945, must kill a rear-area signals hydra (any two of three heads) before it keeps helping German movement toward the southern Western Front.

## GM intent

A winter infiltration of a real walled town: a calm briefing, a rough jump, then thin streets and improvisation after a stale target pack. The main story is killing the hydra — any two of three heads. The players invent the path. Failure changes cost, time, and danger; it does not require a scripted last stand. No occult. Gender is not a table question.

## Player-facing mission

**At briefing (SP0), Ward tells them this in so many words:** He names **Operation Hinterland**. Night of **6–7 January**. Nordwind in Alsace is the clock; they are not going to that front. The target is **Dinkelsbühl**, deep German rear, ~250–300 km from the fighting: a signals station still passing traffic toward Alsace. November *Garnisonsplan* (BG-OH-001): walls, **east water (Wörnitz)**, four gates. Rehearsed drop: **west / Segringer Tor**. Rear-area town, thin garrison, not a fortress; they are not there to fight the garrison or empty the houses. The station is a **hydra**, not one shack. **Three heads:** the **board** (landline exchange), an **aerial**, and the **books** (traffic). **Cut two and it dies.** Cut one and it can still talk. HQ has one pin: the **Rathaus as the exchange**. Find the aerial and the books on the ground. Stein and Keller named as responsible, not the objective. Extraction is theirs after two heads are off. There is no second mission (no POW snatch, no photos). HQ does not tell them they will land east, and Ward does not know the Rathaus is a **false head**. He does not know the live pins (Altrathausplatz, Grüner Turm, Brandt). Full spoken beats: `RUN.md` Story Point 0.

## Predetermined truths

Facts that are true regardless of player action. Distinct from planned events.

- Early January 1945 (table pin: night of **6–7 January**), heavy winter, night. D-Day is months past. The relevant *briefing* clock is Nordwind / Alsace (31 Dec 1944 – 25 Jan 1945). This town’s hydra helping that front is fiction.
- Dinkelsbühl is a real, inhabited, still-intact walled town in Middle Franconia, deep German rear. The Allied fighting in Alsace is on the order of **250–300 km** away. This town’s hydra helping that front is still fiction.
- The garrison is hollow: a handful of regulars, Volkssturm or convalescents (Volkssturm is period), civilians in their houses, and a small signals detachment that actually watches the hydra (the detachment is fiction). Named, responsible officers are known and can escalate. This is not a fortress.
- The hydra has **three heads**: **board** (switchboard, Altrathausplatz), **loft** (aerial, Grüner Turm), **books** (papers with Brandt). Cut **any two** and the hydra dies. Cut one and it is wounded: it can still scream.
- A wounded hydra can still scream. A dead hydra cannot call a stronger garrison from the next town. An alarm is a local hunt first.
- The **Rathaus** is a **false head**. Ward believes it is a real head and pins it. It is not the exchange. Officers may still use it. The three real heads remain findable without it and without one NPC. Checking it still pays (leftover sign, habit, clerk, wire **east** to Altrathausplatz, books toward **Nördlinger**, aerial still visible on the **north** wall if they look). Cutting it does **not** count.
- Table start is a calm briefing in a commandeered schoolroom in **Pont-à-Mousson** (Allied Lorraine, behind the fighting). **Capt. They must **orient** from the briefing map (water is drawn east; the rehearsed west gate is not in front of them). Closing from here is a short correction, not a travel scenario. The town stays asleep.

## Objectives and stakes

### Main objective

Kill the hydra: any **two of three heads** (board, loft, books) so this station cannot keep helping troop or rail movement toward the southern Western Front. The building is not the objective. The Rathaus does not count.

### Secondary objectives

None. Civilians and named officers are pressures and stakes, not extra victory conditions. Players are told the hydra rule **and the three types** at briefing, not later. **Locations** of the real heads are found in town.

### Stakes

- If the PCs act quickly: night, thin streets, officers not yet moving as a fuse. The hydra can be wounded or killed before it screams.
- If the PCs delay: dawn, more eyes, time spent at the false head. A wounded hydra can still call. Extraction gets expensive.
- If the PCs fail: the hydra keeps passing traffic and papers; southern-front movement stays better coordinated for a defined window. They can still leave alive.
- Standing constraint: torching the civilian town or a massacre is a costly outcome (hunt, officers, conscience, extraction). It is not a hidden automatic fail. The hydra can still die on a bloody night.
