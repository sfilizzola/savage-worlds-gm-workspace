# Hazards, healing, and survival of the Incapacitated

Environmental hazards live in the Adventure Tool Kit (pp.125–127). Injury, Incapacitation,
and Healing are in the Rules chapter (pp.95–96) and are repeated here only where a hazard
resolves through them. Condition definitions are in `states.md`; combat procedure is in
`combat.md`.

## The Basics (p.125)

Each Hazard differs, but most are an **attribute check made periodically** against a
dangerous effect. Failure usually causes **Fatigue** (p.100).

## Bumps & Bruises (p.125)

Moving through injurious terrain: **Athletics** roll; failure gains a level of Fatigue.
Recovery is one level per **24 hours** (not per hour). A **Healing** roll can treat it:
success relieves one level, raise relieves two; one attempt per healer unless circumstances
change.

## Climbing (p.125)

Athletics; 2″ of Pace per inch climbed. Out of rounds, no roll unless there's a reason. In
rounds: success = normal progress, failure = no progress, Critical Failure = a fall
(secured: falls half the restraint's length and takes Bumps & Bruises Fatigue; unsecured:
Falling, p.127). Modifiers: +2 for plentiful holds or good equipment; −1 to −4 for smooth
surfaces, no equipment, bad weather.

## Cold (p.125)

- **Trigger:** every **four hours** spent in weather **below freezing (32°F)**, make a
  **Vigor** roll. Failure = one Fatigue level.
- **−2 per 20 degrees below freezing**, to a maximum of −4.
- **Clothing:** −2 with only light clothing; +2 modern winter gear; +4 advanced gear.
- **Death:** Incapacitated victims die after **2d6 hours** instead of waking Exhausted.
- **Recovery:** only once the victim has adequate warmth and shelter.

**Gap to note:** the printed rule only engages **below 32°F**. Cool-but-above-freezing
exposure (a wet, unconscious casualty on 40-something-degree concrete) has no printed
check. Do not extend the interval or the penalties without a GM ruling; the survival of an
Incapacitated Extra has its own printed mechanism (Aftermath & Extras, below).

## Disease (p.126)

Immediate **Vigor** roll to avoid infection on exposure. Three categories — Chronic,
Debilitating (Fatigued 2d6 days), Lethal (Fatigued, then Vigor each turn or take a Wound).
Cured only by running its course or specific medication; symptoms treatable with a Healing
roll once per day, each treatment reducing Fatigue one level for four hours.

## Drowning (p.126)

2″ of Pace per inch swum. Under hazardous conditions, **Athletics** to move: failure = no
progress, Critical Failure = a Fatigue level. Breath: **2 + Vigor die** rounds, halved if
unprepared. Life vest +2. **Death:** Incapacitated characters perish in a number of rounds
equal to their Vigor die; rescue before then allows resuscitation with **Healing at −2**.

## Electricity (p.126)

Outlet or small-farm electric fence: **Vigor at −2**. Success = Distracted, failure =
Stunned. If the source was gripped, the victim cannot let go until a **Vigor at −4** (free
action at the start of each turn); each failed roll adds a Fatigue level, and Incapacitation
releases the grip.

**High Voltage (p.127):** military-grade fence or power line causes **4d6 damage** (5d6 if
standing in water or rain). Dramatic games knock the victim 1d4″ clear; realistic games
require the same Vigor −4 each turn to let go, taking the damage each turn until they do.
Armor does not protect against electrical damage unless specifically designed to.

## Falling (p.127)

**1d6 + 1 per 2″ (4 yards)**, maximum 10d6 + 10. Deep snow reduces damage 1 point per foot.
Into water from 10″ (20 yards) or less, a successful **Athletics** halves damage and a raise
negates it; above 20 yards, treat as solid earth.

## Fire (p.127)

A flammable target hit by fire (GM's call) rolls **1d6**: catches fire on a **6**; *very
flammable* on 4–6; *volatile* (person soaked in gasoline) on 2–6. It immediately takes the
listed damage.

| Damage | Description |
|---|---|
| 1d6 | Spot contact, steam |
| 2d6 | Bonfire, burning room |
| 3d6 | Flamethrower |
| 5d6 | Lava |

Fire damages again at the **start of the victim's turns**; roll d6 immediately after — on a
6 it grows in intensity (usually to a 3d6 maximum for organic beings), on a 1 it drops a
level or burns out below 1d6. An **Athletics** action can put out a fire on oneself,
another, or a blaze up to a Medium Blast Template, modified for intensity, tools, and
flammability. **Armor protects normally** unless the hazard says otherwise. Flaming weapons
add no damage but may set the target alight.

## Incapacitation, Bleeding Out, and Healing (pp.95–96)

Repeated here because most hazards end in one of these.

- **Extras are Incapacitated by a single Wound** (p.95). They may be dead, injured, or
  merely out of the fight; an Incapacitated character takes no actions and **may or may not
  be unconscious — GM's call** (p.95).
- **Incapacitated by damage/injury:** immediate **Vigor** — Critical Failure dies; failure
  means Injury Table and **Bleeding Out**; success and raise vary the Injury's permanence
  (p.95).
- **Bleeding Out (p.95):** Vigor at the start of each turn (or each minute out of combat);
  failure kills, success repeats, raise stabilizes. An ally may stabilize with a successful
  **Healing** action.
- **Healing (p.96):** 10 minutes per Wound level; **−1 without a first aid kit**. Success
  removes one Wound, raise two; Critical Failure adds one. **Healing at least one Wound on
  an Incapacitated patient removes that state** and restores consciousness.
- **The Golden Hour (p.96):** Wounds can only be treated with Healing **within the hour they
  were sustained**. A failed attempt bars *that healer* from those injuries; another
  character may still try. After an hour, only Natural Healing or the healing power applies.
- **Natural Healing (p.96):** Vigor every five days; success one Wound, raise two; Critical
  Failure adds one.
- **Aftermath & Extras (p.96):** when it matters what happened to Extras Incapacitated
  during a fight, **make a Vigor roll for each — those who succeed survive** and must be
  cared for, imprisoned, or released. This is the printed mechanism for "did that Extra
  live," including hours later.

## Explosives — no printed civilian authority

There is **no core entry for commercial or degraded explosives**. **Repair** covers "the use
of demolitions and explosives" (p.34) with no yield. The only printed bulk-blast profiles
are military **grenades (p.78)** and **mines (p.79)**, all Heavy Weapon, resolved via Area
Effect Attacks (pp.97–98). Fire (p.127) covers burning, not detonation. Any scenario that
needs dynamite, blasting powder, or unstable old stock must obtain a GM ruling rather than
borrow a military profile silently.
